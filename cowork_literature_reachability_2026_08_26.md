## 5. PART (ii) — the outward sweep: CANDIDATES ONLY

> **★ THE RULED BOUND, ON THE FACE OF THE LIST WHERE IT CANNOT BE MISSED.** Every item below is a
> **candidate input to source (ii)** of the four ruled derivation sources and **nothing more**.
> **Nothing here is admitted, ranked, established, or ruled.** No item has been read beyond what
> §6 records. A successor that treats this list as findings has misread it.

Each item names **what it decomposes differently** from the joint (key × mode × scale-degree chord,
segmentation as a modelled variable) shape this project rests on — because that difference is the
only reason the sweep was widened.

**Boundaries and segmentation treated differently**

1. **BACHI — "Boundary-Aware Symbolic Chord Recognition Through Masked Iterative Decoding"**,
   arXiv:2510.06528. *Decomposes differently:* the chord **label** is factored into root × quality ×
   bass and decoded by confidence-ordered masked filling, while **boundaries are a separately
   supervised signal** conditioning the encoder rather than a decoded variable. Also reports that
   the best decoding order differs by repertoire — quality-first for classical, bass-first for pop.
   *(The only item whose content was fetched and summarised; see §6.)*
2. **"Unsupervised Learning of Harmonic Analysis Based on Neural HSMM with Code Quality
   Templates"**, arXiv:2403.04135. *Decomposes differently:* a neural **hidden semi-Markov** model —
   duration and therefore segmentation explicit in the state — learned **without labelled
   analyses**.

**The tonal state itself treated differently**

3. **Jacoby, Tishby & Tymoczko, "An Information Theoretic Approach to Chord Categorization and
   Functional Harmony"**, JNMR 44(3), 2015; open author copy at norijacoby.com. *Decomposes
   differently:* the functional **categories are derived** as an information-theoretic optimum
   rather than assumed as the state's alphabet.
4. **White & Quinn, "Chord Context and Harmonic Function in Tonal Music"**, Music Theory Spectrum
   40(2), 2018. *Decomposes differently:* function induced **from context statistics** over a
   corpus rather than declared.
5. **Quinn, "Voice-Leading Prototypes and Harmonic Function in Two Chorale Corpora"**, 2011.
   *Decomposes differently:* the unit is a **voice-leading prototype**, not a chord identity.
6. **"A Clustering-Based Approach to Automatic Harmonic Analysis"**, TISMIR (10.5334/tismir.114).
   *Decomposes differently:* the label alphabet is **clustered from the data**, and the paper links
   harmony to **form**, which our shape carries nowhere.

**Hierarchy and multi-resolution instead of a single segmentation**

7. **Sapp, "Visual Hierarchical Key Analysis"** (keyscapes), 2005; open copy at CCRMA. *Decomposes
   differently:* key is estimated at **every window size simultaneously** — there is no single
   segmentation to commit to, which is a direct alternative to treating segmentation as one
   modelled variable.
8. **Viaccoz, Harasim, Moss & Rohrmeier, "Wavescapes: a visual hierarchical analysis of tonality
   using the discrete Fourier transform"**, JNMR 2023. *Decomposes differently:* tonality
   represented in the **DFT coefficient space** rather than as a discrete key label.
9. **The GTTM computational line — Hamanaka's ATTA / deepGTTM / σGTTM**, gttm.jp and Springer
   chapters. *Decomposes differently:* a **time-span reduction tree** over the whole piece; harmony
   falls out of hierarchical reduction rather than being decoded per span.

**Other**

10. **Gedizlioğlu & Erol, "A regularization algorithm for local key detection"**, 2024.
    *Decomposes differently:* the key-versus-segment-length trade-off is handled as an explicit
    **regularization term** rather than by a transition penalty.
11. **Nápoles López, "justkeydding" — key-finding based on an HMM and key profiles**, 2019.
    *Relation to what we hold:* same author as AugmentedNet, which the register holds; this earlier
    key-finding work it does not.
12. **"The Potential of Unsupervised Induction of Harmonic Syntax for Jazz"**, TISMIR
    (10.5334/tismir.217). *Decomposes differently:* harmonic **syntax induced unsupervised**;
    idiom is a different repertoire, which bears on the ruled idiom split.
13. **"RNInfer: A Large Language Model Approach to Functional Harmonic Reasoning in Symbolic
    Music"**, OpenReview. *Decomposes differently:* no explicit state space at all — the analysis is
    produced by a language model's reasoning. Carried because it is a live direction, not because
    it fits our shape.

**Not a candidate, recorded so it is not re-found as one:** "Enhancing Automatic Chord Recognition
through LLM Chain-of-Thought Reasoning" (arXiv:2509.18700) is audio-side, and this project's subject
is symbolic.
