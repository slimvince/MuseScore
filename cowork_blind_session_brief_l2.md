## 0. Terms, explained before anything rests on them

A reader of this brief knows music theory and knows nothing about this project. Every project term is
explained here, once, before it is used; standard music theory is used in its standard sense.

- **The analysis** — the harmonic-analysis software this project builds: given a notated score, it
  decides the tonality, the chords, and the moments at which one chord gives way to the next. **How it
  CURRENTLY does any of this is exactly what this session must not know.**
- **A deriving session** — one that writes what the analysis SHOULD do for one subject, from music
  theory, from published research, and from the ruled material it is given — WITHOUT reading what this
  project's code or this project's specifications say it DOES. Such a session is **implementation-blind**,
  or **blind**.
- **A layer** — one stage of the analysis, responsible for one question, consuming only what earlier
  layers published. **L0** is the notated record (not a layer: the input contract); **L1** finds the
  moments at which a harmony may begin and says what the notation shows at each; **L2** is this
  session's subject; **L3** reads further facts off L2's settled reading.
- **A charter** — the ratified statement of one layer's job: the question it answers, what it may
  consume, and what it publishes. Charters were derived and ratified in an earlier phase and are
  **given**.
- **The input contract** — the ratified specification of L0 and L1, which states exactly what L1
  publishes and therefore exactly what this layer receives (§3). **Given.**
- **The boot pack** — the ten files that replace, for this session, everything this project would
  otherwise have a session read. §3 names them.
- **A statement** — one atomic sentence of the derived answer, in the six-field form of §4.
- **A slice**, **a change point**, **metric strength class**, **a cue** — defined in the charter and in
  the input contract. They are not redefined here.
- **An exemplar** — a score, or a published analysis of one, staged so that a case can be MET; never a
  sample from which anything is counted (§3).
- **Tonality** — used for what is commonly called "the key". The bare word *key* means tonality and never
  a lookup key; *bar* is the metric unit, never *measure*; *score* means the musical score and never a
  number; *note* is a pitch event and never a remark; *figure* is the figure of a chord (its inversion or
  figured-bass figure) and never a number; *root* is a chord root and never an underlying cause;
  *resolution* is the resolution of a dissonance and never a level of detail.

## 1. What this session is for, in one paragraph

Derive, blind, **how the analysis should read, over a stretch of notated music, the tonality at each
moment, where each harmony gives way to the next, which sounding notes belong to the harmony and which
elaborate it, and what chord is read over each span — and how it publishes that reading with its rivals**
(§2). Write the answer as statements in the six-field form of §4, each carrying its defense in the same
breath. The session produces statements, open questions, a measured-cost record and an independence
record. **It produces no code, no comparison against anything this project already has, no fitted value,
and no verdict on anything this project currently does.**

## 2. The subject, in plain words

**L2's charter question, in the charter's own words:** *over this music, what is the tonality at each
moment, where does each harmony give way to the next, which sounding notes belong to the harmony and
which elaborate it, and what chord is read over each span?* The charter calls L2 **the one entangled
decision**: the four parts are decided together, not one after another. The subject question:

> **How does this layer reach that one decision from what L0 and L1 publish, in what form does it publish
> the decision and its rivals, and what does it never do?**

**The charter settles what L2 publishes and fixes two things about how it scores candidate readings**
(the coupling between tonality and chord is a cost and never a veto; the rule admitting a candidate is
part of this specification and carries its own defense). **It deliberately leaves the rest to this
derivation**: how the score over candidate readings is formed, what its terms are, what they read, and
how any weight would be fitted.

The faces below are the writing side's decomposition of that question, listed so that none is silently
dropped. **The session is free to find the decomposition wrong and to say so.**

**(a) The candidate readings, and the rule that admits them.** What is a candidate reading of a stretch
of music — which tonalities, which chords (degree, quality, figure, applied target), which segmentations —
and what rule decides which candidates the search may even consider? The charter says that rule bounds
every claim the analysis can make about its own ceiling, because a reading the search cannot reach
cannot be found. **Its defense is owed here, whatever it is.**

**(b) Where one harmony gives way to the next.** The charter fixes that a span boundary is a subset of
L1's change points. What decides which change points become boundaries, what evidence published by L1
bears on it, and how is a boundary rival — a reading differing only in where a boundary falls — carried?

**(c) The tonality at each moment.** Tonic and mode per span. What distinguishes a change of tonality
from a chord applied to another degree inside one tonality? What does the written key signature
contribute? The charter's L0 section says in its own words that the key signature is a weak prior and
never a fact about the tonality, and the input contract says what L0 carries about any tonality or mode
tag a file declares. **This session derives what the tonality decision does with each; it does not
re-decide what L0 carries.**

**(d) Which sounding notes belong to the harmony, and which elaborate it.** Per sounding note, the
chord-tone assignment, and where the reading has one, the elaboration relation — passing, neighbour,
suspension, anticipation. How is this decided together with the chord rather than before or after it,
and what evidence published by L1 bears on it?

**(e) The chord read over each span.** Degree, quality, figure and applied target, read against the
span's tonality. **The chord symbol — root, quality, bass — is L3's read-off and not L2's decision**; the
charter says why.

**(f) The score over candidate readings, and how its parts combine.** What terms rate a reading, what
does each read, and how are they combined? **The coupling between tonality and chord is a cost, never a
veto** — the charter fixes it. **No value is fitted by this session**: a term whose value would have to
be fitted from annotated music is written with its source class *measured* (§4), the fitting it would
need described, and no value given.

**(g) The rivals, their mass, and the bar on early discarding.** Per span, the rivals with their mass,
**including rivals that differ in where the boundaries fall and not only in the label**. The charter
forbids discarding a rival before the whole sequence of spans has been scored. What is *mass* — what
does it mean, and what may a consumer read into it — and how is a rival that differs over several spans
published so that it is not mistaken for several independent ones?

**(h) The boundaries of the layer.** What L2 publishes to L3, and what it must not: the charter bars any
intermediate quantity of L2's own scoring from crossing. What L2 does where the input contract publishes
something L2 wants in a form it cannot use — **that is an open question naming the input-contract
statement concerned; L2 never re-derives an L1 fact.** And what L2 does where the working span is only
part of a piece.

**Scope.** Common-practice tonal music as notated in a score. **Cadence type, phrase and section
grouping, the chord symbol, figured bass and harmonic rhythm are L3's and OUT of scope**; where a
statement here depends on one of them, the dependency is written as a statement in its own right.

## 4. The form of every statement — six fields, one rule per statement

Atomic, because a paragraph cannot be compared against anything. Every statement carries all six
fields; a statement that cannot carry the sixth is marked UNVERIFIABLE rather than left to look
checkable.

1. **The statement** — what the analysis must do, or must be ABLE to do.
2. **The defense** — the music theory, the published research fetched and read, or the kind of
   measurement that would decide it, each load-bearing claim labelled FACT / THEORY / CONJECTURE.
   *"Because the current implementation does this"* is not available to this session and is not a
   defense; a statement supportable only by appeal to what software usually does is marked UNSUPPORTED.
3. **The source class** — *derived* (from theory or research), *given* (fixed by the charter or by the
   input contract, cited to its own words), or *measured* (resting on a measurement or a fit this session
   cannot make — written with the measurement or fit it would need, its establishment status
   UNESTABLISHED, and no value).
4. **The status** — *settled* or *open*.
5. **The premise it rests on, and that premise's false-negative path.**
6. **What would falsify it** — in CODE where the statement is behavioural (name the OBSERVABLE, the
   DECISION RULE over it, and the near-miss it is NOT falsified by); in the RESIDUAL where it is a
   modelling premise with no code site. Name observables in plain terms; leave code sites to a later
   session.

**★ THE *given* SOURCE CLASS NOW HAS TWO SOURCES.** A statement the charter or the input contract already
settles is *given* and carries no defense of its own beyond that source's words. **A session that finds
itself marking many statements *given* should say so**, because that is evidence the subject has already
been answered and the derivation is thinner than the phase expects.

**★ AND THE *measured* SOURCE CLASS WILL CARRY MORE HERE THAN IT DID FOR L0 AND L1.** L2's score over
candidate readings will have terms whose values could only come from fitting against annotated music.
**No such value is given, guessed or borrowed from a paper as though it were this analysis's own**; the
statement says what would have to be fitted, on what, and with what held back for evaluation.

**What cannot be settled is written as an open question, never filled with the most plausible reading.**
