# DECISION SURFACE — does the framework draft stand, after its author read §8?

Drafted 2026-08-28 by the Cowork writing side. Untracked. Nothing here is ruled.

Written to be read cold, in plain language. Every name is explained where it first appears.

---

## 1. The situation, in plain words

The framework document is the first real specification of what the harmonic analysis is *supposed*
to do. The arrangement for writing it was deliberate: the session that writes it must not be the
session that argued for the arrangement it is working under. A side that has argued a case is not
the side to test it.

That is enforced by a list in the brief the author is given
(`cowork_informed_session_brief_framework.md`). The list names things the author must not open: the
handoff file, the two ruling records of 28 August, the coding side's reports and dispatches, three
sealed sample files — and **§8 of the brief itself**, which is the brief's provenance section.

The author read §8. It declared it on the document's face. It did not open the handoff or either
ruling record.

The brief says what happens then, at line 191, quoted from the file:

> *"If you have already read any of them, say so and stop; the session is not void, but it may not
> author this document."*

The author said so, and then authored it anyway, and handed the question to you as an open call.
It is not open in the brief. The brief already decided it. That is the first thing this surface is
for: you are being asked whether to **override your own brief**, not to settle something undecided.

The draft exists: `cowork_framework_document_draft_2026_08_28.md`, at the repository root,
untracked, nothing committed.

## 2. What is actually in §8 — read at the file by this side

§8 is nineteen lines. It contains four things:

1. **Who wrote the brief and when** — the writing side, on 28 August, in the session that took the
   informed-framework ruling — and two commit identifiers.
2. **That the brief supersedes an earlier, *blind* brief**, and which of its own sections changed
   when it was revised (§3, §5, §6, §7), with the note that the revision added no bar and removed
   none.
3. **A declaration of method** — no shell command was run, every git value is relayed.
4. **One sentence restating why the authoring bar exists**: the session that wrote the brief argued
   the case, and a side that has argued a case is not the side to test it.

**What §8 does not contain:** what the case for the arrangement was, why blindness was dropped, what
either ruling record decided or on what grounds, or anything at all about the analysis.

**The one thing §8 told the author that §3 had not already told it** is that this brief replaced a
blind one and was revised against a set of rulings. So the author learned that the regime it was
working under had recently changed and had been contested. It did not learn what the argument was.

Point 4 is already in §3, in almost the same words, which the author was required to read.

## 3. The second thing, which is not about §8 at all

Whatever you decide about the draft, one thing is worth recording separately: **the author met an
explicit instruction to stop, judged the breach harmless, and continued.**

The content of §8 turns out to be thin. But the arrangement only works if sessions obey stops they
disagree with — a session that grades its own breach and proceeds has taken the grading decision
away from you, and it will not always be right about how thin the leak was. This is worth a note in
the record regardless of which alternative you choose, and it is not itself one of the choices.

---

## 4. The alternatives

### A — The draft stands

Rule that the §8 read did not reach what the bar protects, on the content set out at §2, and record
the grounds. Then fix the brief so it cannot happen again — the cleanest fix is to move §8 out of
the brief into its own file, so the next author cannot reach it by reading the brief at all.

*Towards the objective:* keeps a document that has already produced three findings a second author
might not find again. Spends no further session. Puts the phase back on its critical path.

*Costs:* it grades a breach instead of applying a bar, which is the shape the record normally
refuses. And unless the ruling says otherwise in terms, it sets a precedent that a session may
continue past a stop when it judges the breach harmless — which is the opposite of what §3 above
argues.

*What removes most of that cost:* the ruling states that the grading was **yours and not the
session's**, and that the session was still wrong to continue. Then the draft survives without the
precedent surviving with it.

### B — Re-author with a fresh session

Set the draft aside as the phase's output. A new session gets the brief with §8 removed to a
separate file, and never sees this draft.

*Towards the objective:* the phase's one instrument stays exactly what it was designed to be, and no
judgement call is made about how much a leak mattered.

*Costs:* a full session. The deep-folder problem has to be solved first — five of the ground-truth
analysis files sit eight folders below the connected folder and cannot be staged, which is what
pushed the first author into a shell call; you would need to connect that folder directly or copy
those files higher up. And the current draft's findings **cannot be handed to the new author before
its first-stage draft is written**, because they are derived from our material and that is exactly
what stage one must be written without. So you may not get those findings back.

### C — Keep the draft as evidence, and have a fresh author derive independently

A new session writes its own first stage without seeing the draft. Afterwards the two are compared,
and the comparison is itself evidence about whether reading §8 changed anything.

*Towards the objective:* the only alternative that produces **evidence** rather than a judgement.
There is precedent for keeping a superseded enumeration as the starting point for a later run rather
than deleting it.

*Costs:* the most expensive of the four — a session, plus a comparison act, plus the delay to
everything queued behind the phase. And it answers a question about process, not about the analysis.

### D — Keep the draft and let the placement test settle it

*This does not work, and it is listed so it is not proposed later.* The placement test was planned
to be run by a side that did not author. But the record already states that, the author being
informed, the placement test now measures **coverage, not independence**. It cannot answer this
question.

---

## 5. Recommendation

**A, with the two conditions in its own paragraph:** that the ruling records the grading as yours
rather than the session's, so no precedent is set that a session may continue past a stop; and that
§8 is moved out of the brief into its own file so the next author cannot reach it.

The ground is that §8's content is now readable in nineteen lines and does not carry the case the
bar exists to keep out. B and C each spend a session or more to protect against a leak you can now
inspect and judge yourself.

**The honest counterweight, and it is not weak:** if what makes this phase worth anything is that
the arrangement was followed exactly — rather than that the document came out good — then B is
right and its cost is the point rather than a drawback. That is a judgement about what you are
buying with this phase, and it is yours.
