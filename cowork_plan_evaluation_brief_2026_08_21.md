## 0. Why this brief exists — stated plainly, because it decides how you must work

The plan has been evaluated once and commented on at length, and **neither pass was an
evaluation of the plan on its merits.** Both failures are recorded here because the brief is
written to prevent them, and you cannot avoid a failure whose shape you are not told.

**The first pass was briefed to refute.** The plan's own §15 set the brief as *"REFUTE, do not
assess"* and stated *"a review returning 'the plan looks sound' is a failed review."* A brief that
forbids a positive finding cannot return a balanced one. That review's verdicts are, in
consequence, uniformly negative — which is what it was asked for, and which tells us nothing
about what in the plan is worth keeping.

**The first pass also judged conformance where it reported merit.** Its largest stated return was
that the plan is not situated against the ruled six-phase structure. Every row of that finding
reads *"the ruled structure holds X, the plan does Y"*. That is a statement about obedience, not
about whether the plan is any good, and it silently mixed with eight other findings that were
measured and stand on their own evidence.

**The second pass read the critique instead of the plan**, mixed three generations of one rule
without opening the ruling record, and declared a ruled rule defective on the strength of a
verdict the first pass had itself flagged as unchecked.

**So: this is the first evaluation of this plan as a plan.** Treat every prior verdict about it —
including any you encounter in the handoff record, a close, a commit message, or a status entry —
as **an unverified claim to be tested, never as an input you inherit.**

## 3. The rules are IN SCOPE as objects of evaluation

This is the clause that separates this brief from the last one.

**You may — and where it bears on your answer, you must — evaluate the standing rules the plan
operates under, and say if one is wrong.** A rule is not evidence that a rule is right. If the
ruled structure asks for something that does not serve maximum-precision inference, say so, give
your grounds, and name what should replace it.

**You may not change a ruling, and you are not asked to.** Only the user rules. A session's act is
to report — with grounds — that a rule should change. Reporting that is not a breach of the rule;
obeying a rule you believe is wrong without saying so is a breach of this brief.

**Establish the CURRENT generation of any rule you rely on, at its ruling record, before you rely
on it.** The rules bearing on this plan have been superseded at least once. Where you cannot
establish which generation is live, say CANNOT ESTABLISH and stop there — do not pick the one that

## 4. Required output — and the two clauses that make it an evaluation

### 4.1 Every finding is labelled MERIT or CONFORMANCE

- **MERIT** — this is wrong (or right) on its own evidence, and the verdict would survive if every
  ruling in this project were deleted tomorrow. Cite the measurement or the primary source.
- **CONFORMANCE** — this collides with a standing rule. State which rule, at which generation, at
  its ruling record, **and state separately whether you think the rule is right.**

**A conformance finding never carries merit weight, and the two are never combined into one
verdict.** Mixing them is the specific defect this brief exists to prevent.

### 4.2 Both polarities are required, and an all-negative return is an incomplete evaluation

You are asked what is **good** in this plan as seriously as what is bad. For each, the same bar:
grounds, cited.

Report, at minimum:
- **What should be KEPT** — the parts that are right, and why, whether or not the plan states them
  well. Include anything good that fell out of the lineage between versions (§1).
- **What should be REPAIRED** — right in intent, wrong in mechanism. Give the repair.
- **What should be DROPPED** — wrong at the root, not repairable. Give the grounds.
- **What is MISSING** — the thing a plan for this job needs and no version of this one contains.
- **What you CANNOT ESTABLISH** — recorded as such, never resolved by argument.

**A finding that a component is sound is a real finding and is reported with the same weight as a
refutation.** This brief does not reward refutations. It rewards being right.

## 5. The counterfactual — the part that feeds the successor plan

Verdicts alone do not produce a better plan. So, after §4 and separately from it:

**Describe the approach you would take to reconstruct these specifications, derived from the
ultimate objective — the most precise inference obtainable — rather than from the existing plan.**
Take it as far as you honestly can: what the units are, what order they run in, what proves the
method before it is trusted at full width, what each step costs, and what would tell you the
approach is failing.

Where your approach coincides with the plan's, say so — that is convergence and it is evidence.
Where it differs, say what the difference turns on.

**If the honest answer is that the plan's approach is substantially right, say that.** The point of
this section is not to produce a rival; it is to find out whether an independent derivation lands
in the same place.
