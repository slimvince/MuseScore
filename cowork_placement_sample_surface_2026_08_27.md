## 0. Terms, re-explained from scratch, so nothing below assumes you remember an identifier

**The framework phase.** The third of the six ruled phases. Its job is to decide the analysis
architecture before any individual specification is written.

**The frame.** What the framework phase produces — one document stating what the analysis
maximizes and over what, plus per-unit sets of statements inside it.

**The placement test.** The adversarial check on the finished frame. Statements are taken from
**outside** the frame and each is placed somewhere in it. The brief is *place these, and report
every one you cannot* — never *does this frame look complete?* Every statement that fits nowhere is
a finding about the frame. There is no pass mark; you rule per finding.

**The placement population.** Where those statements come from, ruled at the successor plan's §6.2
and repeated as eight strata in yesterday's Ruling 3: ruling records; decision surfaces; dossiers;
the DEFERRED entries of the decisions register; the evidence inventory; the declared dormancies;
every current document heading; every heading ever deleted from the document set.

**Sealing.** Yesterday's Ruling 3 requires the drawn set to be *"written down and closed before the
frame's author begins."* Its stated ground is structural, not suspicion: a sample drawn afterwards
allows the frame to have been shaped around material its author had just been reading, and no
declaration made later can undo that.

**Claude Code, called CC below.** The coding assistant that runs in the repository on your machine,
one dispatch per session. **The writing side** is a Cowork session — this one — which writes
surfaces, records rulings and writes dispatches to disk. It does not open a dispatch; you do.

**The blindness barrier.** The standing rule that a session deriving specification content may not
read our own code or any document derived from it.

### DECISION 1 — Which side draws and seals the placement sample

**The subject.** Ruling 3 says what the sample must be and when it must be closed. It does not say
who draws it. Two facts constrain the answer, and they pull in opposite directions.

*The first:* the deleted-headings stratum can only be enumerated from the repository's history.
This side reads your machine through the file bridge and runs no shell command against the
repository — a standing method of this project — so a Cowork session cannot produce that stratum at
all. **Only CC can enumerate the population.**

*The second:* Ruling 1 already made CC the side that RUNS the placement test. If CC also chooses
which statements go into the sample, **the side being tested picks its own examination questions.**
That is the same class of defect the sealing property exists against, arriving through a different
door.

**Alternative A — CC enumerates the population and draws the sample, using its own judgement of
what is representative.**

*For:* one dispatch. CC has the history, the whole document set and the context to read across it.

*Against:* it puts selection and placement in the same side. Nothing in the report would let a
reader distinguish *the frame held everything we placed* from *we placed what the frame holds*.

*Towards the objective:* weak. *Towards the principles:* weak — it makes the test's independence a
claim rather than a construction.

**Alternative B — CC enumerates the population and writes it down; a later Cowork session, which
is neither the frame's author nor the placing side, draws the sample from that enumeration and
seals it.**

*For:* enumeration (mechanical, history-dependent) is separated from selection (discretionary), and
the discretion sits with a side that neither authors the frame nor runs the test.

*Against:* two round-trips — one dispatch, then a session, then the frame. The enumeration of the
heading strata may be large enough that a Cowork session reading it through the bridge is doing the
volume work Ruling 1 allocated away from Cowork for exactly that reason.

*Towards the objective:* strong on independence, moderate on cost.
*Towards the principles:* strong.

**Alternative C — the selection RULE is written into the dispatch by the writing side; CC
enumerates, applies the rule mechanically, writes the sealed sample, and commits it.**

The rule is deterministic and leaves CC no choice: a declared ordering per stratum, a declared
census-or-sample threshold, a declared take-rate for the strata above it. CC measures the counts —
which nobody can know before the enumeration exists — and the rule then fixes the sample without a
judgement call.

*For:* selection is authored by a side that is neither the frame's author nor the placing side,
which is what the independence requires; the volume work stays where it can be paid; one dispatch.
**And the seal becomes the strongest form this project has** — the sealed sample is a committed
file at a named tip, dated before the frame's author starts, rather than a promise in prose.

*Against:* a mechanical rule cannot notice that a stratum's enumeration came back malformed. The
dispatch has to carry a STOP for that case rather than relying on the placing side's judgement.

*Towards the objective:* strong. *Towards the principles:* strong — the discretion is exercised
once, in writing, before the counts are known, by a side with nothing to gain from either outcome.

**Alternative D — leave it open and decide when the enumeration is in hand.**

*For:* no commitment before the shape is known.

*Against:* the sample must be sealed before the frame is authored, so this defers the one thing the
order requires be settled first. It is also the pattern this project has now hit repeatedly, where
a constraint stands in prose with nothing scheduled against it.

*Towards the objective:* weak. *Towards the principles:* weak.

**Recommendation: C.** It is the only alternative that keeps the selection out of the placing
side's hands without spending a second Cowork session on volume work, and it converts sealing from
a declaration into a commit. Recorded with it: **this session, having written the rule, is
thereafter not eligible to author the frame** — the frame's author must be a later fresh Cowork
session, which is what Ruling 1 requires anyway.

### DECISION 2 — On what basis the size of each stratum is set

**The subject.** Ruling 3 requires *"a size declared per stratum"* and, in the same breath, refuses
the only measurement that could have supplied a number. So the basis is genuinely unruled. No count
of any stratum is asserted anywhere in this surface: **this side has measured none of them**, and
they cannot be measured before the enumeration runs.

**Alternative A — one flat number per stratum, ruled now.**

*For:* simple; the report's arithmetic is trivial.

*Against:* a flat number is either larger than a small stratum contains, or a token slice of a large
one. It also has to be invented today against no measurement, which is precisely what Ruling 3
declined to do from the sizing record.

*Towards the objective:* weak. *Towards the principles:* weak — a number with no ground is the
thing the ruling refused.

**Alternative B — census the small strata, sample the large ones, at a threshold and take-rate
declared before the counts are known.** Every stratum whose enumeration returns at or below the
threshold goes in whole; each stratum above it contributes a declared share, taken by a declared
ordering.

*For:* the per-stratum finding stays interpretable, which is the whole reason Ruling 3 stratified —
*the frame cannot hold dormancy declarations* is a different finding from *the frame cannot hold
deferred register entries*, and a stratum taken whole says the first of those without an
uncertainty range at all. Cost scales with what is actually there rather than with a guess. The
threshold and rate are declared in advance, so no number is chosen after the counts are visible.

*Against:* the total is not knowable until the enumeration returns, so the cost of the placement
test is bounded only in shape, not in size. If a heading stratum comes back very large, the take is
still large.

*Towards the objective:* strong. *Towards the principles:* strong — it declares the rule before the
data, which is the discipline the sealing property is an instance of.

**Alternative C — a fixed total budget, divided across the strata in proportion to their counts.**

*For:* the cost is bounded exactly, which nothing else here does.

*Against:* proportional division gives the small strata almost nothing, so the strata most likely to
expose a shape the frame cannot hold — the declared dormancies, the evidence inventory — contribute
one or two statements each and their findings carry no weight. It optimizes the number this project
has no ruled basis for at the expense of the property Ruling 3 stratified to get.

*Towards the objective:* moderate. *Towards the principles:* weak.

**Recommendation: B.** It is the only alternative under which a stratum taken whole yields a
finding that needs no uncertainty range, and it is the only one whose numbers are not invented
before anything is counted. If the enumeration returns a stratum so large that the declared take is
not payable, that is a finding to put to you, not a licence for the drawing side to quietly shrink
it — and the dispatch should say so.

### DECISION 3 — What is done about the two strata the frame's author cannot avoid reading

**The subject, and it is the reason this decision exists at all.** Ruling 3's ground for sealing is
that the frame must not be shaped around material its author had just been reading. **Sealing does
not achieve that for two of the eight strata.** The frame's author is a fresh Cowork session that
must read the governing records to know what it is allowed to do — this handoff, yesterday's ruling
record, the opening surface, the plan, the phase-definition surface. Those are *ruling records* and
*decision surfaces*: two of the eight strata the sample is drawn from.

So for those two strata the placement test asks whether a frame can hold statements its author had
read before authoring. It can still return an unplaceable statement — that finding stands. But a
*placeable* result from those two strata is weaker evidence than the same result from the deleted
headings, and nothing in the current design records the difference.

**This is not an argument for blinding the author.** A frame authored without reading its own
constraints is the worse failure. The question is only what is done about the asymmetry.

**Alternative A — declare it and stratify the report.** The frame's author records, in the frame,
the list of documents it read. The placement report gives the unplaceable proportion per stratum,
never as one pooled number, and the two overlapping strata carry the overlap on their face.

*For:* costs nothing and adds no act. It makes the overlap measurable — the author's read list
against the sealed sample's sources — rather than assumed either way. It is this project's own
discipline for a bound that cannot be removed: name it, carry it, do not let a later reader
discover it.

*Against:* it removes no weakness; it only stops the weakness being invisible.

*Towards the objective:* strong for what it costs. *Towards the principles:* strong — a declared
bound that travels with the result.

**Alternative B — draw the two overlapping strata only from records the frame's author will not
read**, fixed by giving the author a closed read list.

*For:* restores real independence for those strata.

*Against:* the read list has to be closed before anyone knows what the frame's author will need,
and a deriving session that cannot read a ruling it turns out to be bound by will either breach the
list or derive against a constraint it never saw. Restating the withheld rulings into a pack
instead is the carrier that has already turned one rule of this project into three materially
different wordings.

*Towards the objective:* moderate. *Towards the principles:* weak — it trades a measurable bound
for a new contamination route of the kind that has bitten here before.

**Alternative C — drop the two overlapping strata from the sample.**

*For:* the remaining six strata are clean.

*Against:* it is a disposal, and this project's disposal discipline requires the worth test with a
finding, a date and a reason. It also removes the strata where design intent actually lives, which
is most of what the test is for.

*Towards the objective:* weak. *Towards the principles:* weak — discard without the ruled test.

**Recommendation: A**, with the author's read list written into the frame rather than left to be
reconstructed. B is the shape that would fix the asymmetry and this side does not press it: what it
buys is bounded, and what it costs is the exact failure mode that voided the derivation method
once already.
