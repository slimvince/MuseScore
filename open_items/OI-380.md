**What this row is about.** The user ordered two things on 2026-09-07, in his own words: *"1. tidy

governing-document amendments: an edit that supersedes part of a governing document MOVES the newly

## The three findings, each read at an object

**(1) The rule has not been breached since it was ruled, and the growth it was suspected of causing
has another explanation.** The whole range from the pruning measurement's pin to this batch was
walked one parent link at a time out of the commit objects, and every commit that touched
`CLAUDE.md` in it was attributed at its own diff — `tools/audit/claude_md_growth_2026_09_07.json`.
SIX commits touched the file. One is the executed governing-surface split, the only commit in the
range that removes text on balance. Three are AMENDMENTS, and in every one of them **the superseded
wording is absent from the diff**: it went to `CLAUDE_ARCHIVE.md` in the same act and a compact
dated pointer was left at the site, which is rule (D)'s own prescribed shape. What those amendments
ADDED is the pointer and its ground, which the rule requires. The remaining two add new operative
rule text with nothing superseded. **So the growth is dominated by amendment apparatus — confirmed —
but it is NOT caused by the rule being unfollowed — refuted.** No count or figure is restated here
(#17f, **D-431**).

**(2) A lint now watches the rule, and its first measurement finds no breach anywhere.**
`tools/audit/prune_at_amendment_lint.py` fails when a governing document carries a span saying that
its OWN former wording is preserved IN PLACE — the pattern rule (D) explicitly supersedes — and
passes when a supersession is recorded as a pointer to where the text now lives. Its reach was
measured over all five governing documents BEFORE it was enrolled, in both directions: every hit,
every near miss, and every supersession the record writes as a pointer, per file
(`tools/audit/prune_at_amendment_lint.json`). **The result is zero breaches in any of the five.**
Every hit carries a date at or before the rule's own ruling date, which places all of it before the
rule existed. That is an independent confirmation of finding (1), taken by a different route.

**The lint's one cut is derived rather than authored**, and it is what makes the check a forward
guard instead of a permanent red: a preserve-in-place statement dated at or before the rule's own
ruling date was the lawful pattern when it was written and is reported as PRE-RULE BACKLOG; one
dated after it is a BREACH; one carrying no date at all is a BREACH too, so a statement cannot
escape the check by carrying none.

**(3) What remains cannot be moved under the ruled span unit, and every candidate was read whole
before that was concluded.** `tools/audit/gen_claude_md_prune_backlog.py` derives the population at
this batch's own pin — the cut, the classifier and the standing archive-pointer constraint imported
from `CLAUDE.md`'s own finer unit, the spans earlier sittings settled excluded from the committed
record of those acts, and, for the first time, the spans the preserve-in-place recognizer adds. The
candidates were READ WHOLE and **every one STAYS**. Each is the same shape: a LIVE statement that
carries the OPENING of a preserved wording — the six-phase supersession block, which states the
governing structure and the rule that replaced the truth half, and the phase-2 ratification note,
whose operative half is what the ratification did NOT do. Read whole, neither IS a preserved former
wording, so the reading test fails at its first half and the ruled doubt default (§5(E)) keeps both.
The tool's `--apply` STOPS and reports rather than forcing a move, which is the ruled behaviour when
a reading keeps its whole population.
