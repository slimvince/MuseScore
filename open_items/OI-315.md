## Why this is more than one stale paragraph

**The register carries it as a LIVE decision.** `D-058` — *The piece-start shortcut* — is homed at
`ARCHITECTURE.md:3510-3514` with status `live`, and its plain restatement says the program *"simply
believes"* a declared major or minor at the start of a piece. That is a decision the code stopped
taking on 2026-06-14. It is not an obscure entry: **D-058 is one of the three decisions the
2026-08-01 adjudication names as provably invisible to the harvest's signature net**, found only by
reading the specification in full — so it was entered with unusual care, and the care went into
finding it rather than into checking it was still true.

**The design document contradicts itself about it, in the same file.** §1 lists it under *what Stage 4
MUST NOT regress* and records the removal; §2.1 then says *"**Initial state**: the **piece-start
shortcut** becomes the HMM's start prior"*, and §5's reuse map gives it the disposition **"REUSE as
start prior (reproduces the pin)"**. One of those is the pre-removal draft and the other is the
correction; the document carries both without marking which superseded which. Its own §1 sentence
notes the consequence in passing — *"The HMM's initial-state prior is now whatever the note-based
opening produces (no declared start-anchor to reproduce)"* — which is the correction, sitting three
sections away from the two statements it refutes.

## What is NOT in doubt, and what is

Not in doubt: the code. `keyresolver.cpp:291-293` states the removal in its own comment, and the
design document independently dates it to Stage 4b-i, 2026-06-14, with the pins re-targeted to
`PieceStartOpening_NoteBased_DeclaredMinor/Major`.

In doubt, and **deliberately not settled here**: whether the specification paragraph should be
corrected, annotated as historical, or replaced by a statement of the note-based opening; and
whether D-058's status becomes `superseded-in-fact` (a later BUILD replaced what it governs without
a ruling that names it — the register's own vocabulary has the term) or something else. Both are
register/specification acts, and READ WAVE 1 reads and rows.
