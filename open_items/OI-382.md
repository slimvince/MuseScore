## What the tool does, and what the adjustment is for

`tools/audit/gen_status_batch_bound.py` performs the move Ruling 4's forward clause orders: it reads
each of the then-previous batch's `STATUS.md` entries from a git object at an authored base commit,
matches it verbatim in the live file, and moves it whole into `STATUS_ARCHIVE.md`. **No entry is
ever retyped**, and the match is byte-exact.

One textual difference is expected between the two sides and is DECLARED as the tool's single
adjustment. The newest entry of a batch carries the `Last updated: ` prefix; the next batch's close
writes its own entries above it, and the prefix moves up to the newest of those. So the entry this
act must find in the live file differs from the base commit's by exactly that prefix and nothing
else. **Any entry needing a SECOND adjustment is a STOP, not a second constant.**

## The defect, established at the objects

**(1) The test was unanchored.** Until 2026-09-07 it read `if PREFIX_ADJUSTMENT in text:` — a
substring search over the WHOLE entry. Read at the git object of the commit that changed it,
`f7d6d7c391`, on the `-` side of its diff of this file.

**(2) At HEAD it is anchored to the entry's opening.** It now reads
`if text.lstrip("*").startswith(PREFIX_ADJUSTMENT):` — read at the tool's own source, in
`moved_entries()`.

**(3) An entry does quote the literal in its own prose, and it is the entry the next move had to
find.** At the forward-bound batch's own close commit, `31dc1e5d88`, `STATUS.md` carries the literal
`Last updated: ` **three times over two entries**: once as the real prefix at the head of the newest
entry, once more inside that same entry's narrative, and once inside the Task 1 entry's narrative —
the entry that explains the adjustment and therefore names it. That Task 1 entry opens
`*2026-09-07 (CC — Same dispatch, Task 1. …`, so it carries the literal without carrying the prefix.

**(4) The failure is exactly what follows from (1) and (3).** The unanchored test is TRUE for that
entry, so `text.replace(PREFIX_ADJUSTMENT, "", 1)` strips the NARRATIVE occurrence and produces a
string that exists nowhere in the live file. The anchored test is FALSE for it, so no adjustment is
applied and the entry matches verbatim.

**(5) The tool's own STOP is what caught it, and that is the STOP working rather than failing.**
`apply_move()` refuses to proceed unless each entry occurs exactly once in the live file
(`live.count(rec["_text"]) != 1` raises). **Nothing was moved wrongly**, and no entry was altered.

## Why it is worth a row although the test is now anchored

**Because the discovery was never rowed.** Register rule (c) requires a newly discovered issue to
get an index row and its detail file in the same commit that records the discovery. The repair
landed at `f7d6d7c391` with its cause established at the object and stated at the site (**D-669** —
a maintenance act establishes the cause before it touches the mechanism), but no row was opened.
This row discharges rule (c) late, and the INDEX records the state at HEAD: repaired.

**And because the shape outlives the instance.** The tool's own comment states the general point and
its own limit: the constant's meaning is that the NEWEST entry of a batch carries the prefix, and a
prefix is at the start; the test now says so; and what the anchoring **cannot** do is widen — an
entry needing a second adjustment still reaches the occurrence check and still STOPS. It is the
neighbouring shape to [[OI-330]]'s lesson, that a tool anchoring on a bare string is fragile against
the project's own correct work; here the fragile anchor was not a filename but a literal the record
itself has occasion to quote, because the record explains its own mechanisms.
