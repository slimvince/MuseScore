# OI-244 — The key-prune tie-break ranks by absolute tonic pitch class — not transposition-covariant; the expected shifted key can fall out of the kept candidates

> STATUS IS AUTHORITATIVE IN THE INDEX (OPEN_ITEMS.md) — this file carries narrative and provenance only and is NEVER the status of record

**Section D — joint decoder / candidate admission (declared to Cowork). Compounds [[OI-226]].**

## The finding

`probe_decoder.py:1039-1052` (Cowork-verified at the source): the key-fit prune ranks candidate
keys by `(−fit, absolute tonic pitch class, mode)` and keeps the top K. The middle tie-break term
is an ABSOLUTE pitch-class number, so under transposition the tie order among equal-fit keys
permutes, and the correctly-shifted key can fall outside the kept set entirely. The
transposition-equivariance probe ([[OI-243]]) diagnosed 5 of its 17 label-only violations to
exactly this: the expected shifted state was not in the candidate set.

## Why this is its own row beside OI-243

[[OI-243]] is the spelling-anchor mechanism in the EMISSION; this is the ADMISSION path — the
top-K key prune that [[OI-226]] already records as having entered production with no ratified
basis. The compounding fact this row adds: the unratified prune is also measured
representation-non-covariant. Whatever ratified basis the family design gives candidate admission
must also state its invariance properties (a transposition-covariant tie-break — for example
breaking ties relative to the signature or to the previous segment's key rather than by absolute
pitch-class number — is a design choice for that surface, named here as an observation, not
designed here).
