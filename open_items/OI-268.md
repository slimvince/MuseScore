## Why this is not simply [[OI-237]] again

[[OI-237]]'s sixteen were decisions with **no home at all** that a specification should have
stated. These twenty-two have a home, and in several cases a good one: `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s
cross-cutting bounded-context bullet **explicitly points at** `cowork_bounded_context_design.md`
as "the ONE cross-layer extension spec" (`cowork_bounded_context_design.md:10`), and the
confidence contract names its own architecture home as the §2.15 contracts (`:6`). Register entry
**D-092** — a cross-cutting contract is stated once and never redefined in a layer document — makes
a single non-layer home for a cross-cutting contract *conformant*, not a defect.

So the question this row raises is narrower than "these are unhomed". It is: **does
[[OI-208]] ruling 2's "wherever possible, the owning layer's specification" reach a cross-cutting
contract that a layer specification deliberately delegates to?** If it does, twenty-two homing acts
are owed. If it does not, the register's `gap` classification is wrong for them and needs a fifth
case — "a cross-cutting contract, correctly homed outside any layer" — beside the four it has.

**That is a ruling, not a mechanical move, so this pass did not make it.** The dispatch's own stop
condition applies: writing the entry would have forced resolving something the record leaves open.

## What is NOT in doubt

Whatever the classification, **the twenty-two are real, they bind, and until this wave they were
invisible to the register** — which is the finding that matters for [[OI-207]]. Two of them are
worth naming individually:

- **D-278** is a **shelving with evidence** (the joint key-and-chord step, measured not to pay)
  recorded only in a design document. That is precisely the class [[OI-207]] was opened to hunt:
  the Stage-3.1b shelving went missing the same way.
- **D-266** is a **live prohibition** — Layer 6 may not resume until the bounded-context design is
  coded and regression-tested across Layers 1 to 5 — recorded in a document no session-start read
  opens.

## Disposition

For the user: rule on the question above. Then either the homing acts (with [[OI-237]]'s and

ratifying event), or a register change adding the fifth non-specification case and re-classifying
the twenty-two. **No fix, no design, no specification edit is made here.**

**★ Dated note — 2026-08-02 (CC, the phase-1e second-partition wave): THE EVIDENCE DUTY IS
DISCHARGED, AND THE ARCHIVE POINTS ONE WAY — a ratified contract document was treated as a HOME, not
as a draft.**

The dispatch set this pass a standing duty while reading the archives: *where an archive ruling shows
how delegated contract documents were treated — as homes or as drafts — record it*, because the user
rules this row on that evidence. Reading `cowork_handoff_archive.md` in full produced three
independent observations, all pointing the same way.

**(1) A ratified contract document is cited as the authority, not as a proposal.** The 2026-07-07
entry-point block introduces the reading order as "Key design docs: `cowork_layer5_engagement_design.md`
(the Layer-5 design), `cowork_joint_key_chord_design.md` (the joint step — SHELVED, …),
`cowork_structural_integrity_audit.md` …, `cowork_functional_analysis_research_grounding.md`
(published-fact grounding)" — a session is directed to those documents for the design, not to a layer
specification that restates them. The same block's ratified rulings are recorded *into* those
documents and cited *from* the handoff, which is the behaviour of a home.

**(2) The one-home rule was applied deliberately, and it chose the design document.** At
`cowork_handoff_archive.md:1775-1781` the record states that the Layer-5 engagement design was written
as a **NEW document rather than an edit of the signed one**, with the reason given in the register's
own vocabulary: *"engagement wiring is a distinct concern from the signed dormant-build spec; #6, one
home per concern; the new doc references the signed §5.5/§7/§8/§15 rather than restating"*. That is
principle #6 being used to place a decision in a design document and to forbid restating it elsewhere
— which is exactly what register entry **D-092** makes conformant, and it is the strongest evidence
in the archive that these homes are intentional rather than accidental.

**(3) The same reasoning produced a separate home when the concern was separate.** At `:1722-1730` the
joint key-and-chord design is likewise made a NEW document because the engagement document "scopes
selection-within-a-fixed-key and enumerates the joint step as a *distinct* downstream piece … so #6
wants a separate home". The record uses the word *home* for a `cowork_*` design document, repeatedly
and without qualification.

**What this evidence does and does not settle.** It settles that the practice of record treats a
ratified contract or cross-layer design document as a decision's HOME, chosen under #6 and cited as
authority. It does **not** settle this row's question, which is narrower: whether [[OI-208]] ruling
2's *"wherever possible, the owning layer's specification"* nonetheless reaches such a document, so
that twenty-two homing acts are owed. The evidence makes the second option — a fifth non-specification
case, "a cross-cutting contract, correctly homed outside any layer" — the reading the record's own
conduct supports; but adopting it because conduct supports it would be inferring a ruling from
practice, which the never-work-from-memory rule forbids. **The ruling is still the user's, and this
pass did not make it.**

**One thing the evidence sharpens, for whichever way the ruling goes.** The archive itself is NOT in
this class. A `cowork_*` design document is opened by name and carries a status banner; the
session-handoff archive is declared reference-only by two governing documents. The fourteen decisions
this wave found there are therefore a different problem from these twenty-two, and are rowed
separately at [[OI-272]] — but the two rows want ONE ruling session, because the answer to "does
ruling 2 reach a delegated contract document?" bounds the answer to "what is owed for an archive?".
