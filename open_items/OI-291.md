# OI-291 — ★ STOP: the register's `contract-home` class was never reached by any of the ratified criteria, and the delegation bar excludes documents it currently admits

**Section E — user adjudication. Rowed 2026-08-03 by the phase-1p dispatch's own instruction
(`cc_instruction_phase1p_home_rulings_and_mechanisms.md` §3.3): "if any currently-admitted document

## What was ordered, and what the check found

The user ruled the DELEGATION BAR on 2026-08-03 (**D-432**): which wordings count as a delegation for
the purposes of the contract-home criterion. The dispatch ordered a check to run **before** applying
it, and stated the prediction the check was testing: *"It should not [change any currently-admitted
verdict] — the admitted set was reached on explicit delegation clauses."*

**The prediction is REFUTED.** The check ran in a generator —
`tools/audit/decisions/gen_phase1p_delegation_bar.py`, artifact
`tools/audit/decisions/phase1p_delegation_bar.json` → `pre_apply_check` — over the whole home
population (`population`), and every figure below is a field of that artifact, never transcribed:

- `documents_currently_admitted_that_the_BAR_excludes` — documents the register classifies
  `contract-home` today whose only delegation takes one of the two forms the bar does NOT admit, with
  the form and the file-and-line of the delegation for each. `entries_affected_by_the_bar` is how many
  register entries sit in them.
- `documents_currently_admitted_that_FAIL_CLAUSE_(a)_ANYWAY` — a second, separate set: documents the
  register classifies `contract-home` that are **named in none of the three user-ratified surfaces at
  all**. The bar does not decide these; they fail the earlier clause, and have failed it since the
  phase-1l measurement first reported them (`open_items/OI-281.md`, first measurement note, the clean
  EXCLUDE table).
- `section_level_consequence.by_document` — a third movement, and it is not the bar's doing either:
  once the bar settles clause (b) for a document that names SECTIONS, the already-ratified
  section-level criterion (**D-430**) decides each entry by whether it sits in a delegated section.
  For `cowork_voiceleading_axis_design.md` most entries sit in sections no delegation names.

## The diagnosis — and it is sharper than "the bar is strict"

**The `contract-home` class at HEAD was not produced by any criterion now in force.** It was produced
at phase 1i under the ORIGINAL fifth-home-case test (ratified-or-signed banner + a status banner + a
delegation pointer, `OPEN_ITEMS.md` OI-268, user-ratified 2026-08-02). The three criteria ruled since
— the delegation-specificity criterion, the document-kind test, and the section-level unit (**D-430**)
— were each MEASURED and each deliberately NOT APPLIED, except for the staged 46 entries of phase 1n.
Their measurements are the three dated notes on [[OI-281]].

So the dispatch's premise — *"the admitted set was reached on explicit delegation clauses"* — is a
claim about the register that the register does not support, and it is checkable: `nonspec_kind` in
`tools/audit/decisions/backbone_decisions.json` carries the phase-1i classification for everything
outside the staged 46. **This is itself an instance of D-431's premise clause** (a claim carried
across from a surface that repeats it rather than checked at the primary source), and it is the
process check's own founding test case.

## What was NOT done, and why

**No entry's home class was changed by this wave.** Not the ones the bar excludes, not the ones that
fail clause (a), not the section-level consequence. The dispatch's stop is on APPLYING, and applying
would be building around the surprise (#13).

**The bar itself IS homed** — `CLAUDE.md`, the decisions-register section, rule (i), register entry
**D-432** — because recording a user ruling is not the same act as applying it, and because the
register's own design puts non-conformance in `OPEN_ITEMS.md` rather than in the register
(`CLAUDE.md`, decisions-register preamble). This row is that non-conformance.

**Two adjacent rulings of the same wave are consequently unapplied too**, and each is recorded at its
own place rather than absorbed here: **D-433** (a shelved section can be a home) removes one obstacle
for `cowork_joint_key_chord_design.md`, but the bar leaves those entries excluded for a different
reason — its delegation is a parenthetical naming inside a bulleted list. And W4's ruling to admit
`cowork_engage_arc_plan.md`'s three entries **collides with the bar**: the dispatch's §4 test is
"names it by name for a stated concern", which `CLAUDE.md:129-130` does; the bar is stricter, and that
naming is inside a list of citations. The collision is reported, not resolved by a session.

## What the user is being asked

Not a re-ruling of the bar — the bar is the user's and it is recorded. The question is what to do
about the gap it exposes, and there are three shapes:

1. **Apply the bar and every unapplied measurement in ONE re-classification pass**, over the whole
   home population at once, with the movement enumerated per entry and the former class preserved
   (#12). That is the largest single home-class movement since phase 1i, and it is a deliberate
   re-baseline, not a bookkeeping act.
2. **Apply it only forward** — new entries are classified under (g)/(h)/(i), existing ones migrate as
   their document is next touched, exactly as D-430's staging already works. The register then carries
   two vintages of classification and must say so in its scope note.
3. **Revise the bar** where it excludes something the record means to keep. The two cases worth the
   user's eye are `cowork_prefit_gates.md` — four ratified protocols, delegated only by a citation
   list and a ratification attribution — and `cowork_layer5_function_design.md`, a SIGNED layer
   specification delegated only by *"Full spec:"*. In both, the fix might be to WRITE a proper
   delegation into `ARCHITECTURE.md` rather than to loosen the bar; only the user writes a delegation
   into that document (rule (g)'s guard).

The W4 collision needs an answer either way, and so does whether `cowork_engage_arc_plan.md` — a
user-ratified surface that the criterion USES as a source of delegations — can be excluded as a
delegation TARGET by a bar it helps enforce.

## What is NOT in doubt

The bar itself, ruled by the user; the delegation citations, each located in its surface by the
generator and quoted from the file rather than transcribed (`documents[].delegation_line`); and the
register's present classification, read from its own data.

## Dated note — 2026-08-03 (CC, phase 1q Task 1): the ruling, and the pass

The user ruled **option 1 of the three shapes above**, with the addition that the write list rides
with it: **one re-classification pass over the whole home population, applied**, with a write list
for the homes the record means to keep. The row RESOLVES on that; the write list is carried at
[[OI-293]].

**The measurement and the application ran in the SAME wave**, and that is a deliberate reading of
the instruction rather than a liberty taken with it. The dispatch's own recommendation said the
pass should "run as a measurement first"; taken literally that produces a **fourth
measured-but-unapplied criterion**, which is the precise defect this row exists to record. So the
pass measures, reports the whole movement, and applies it in one act, with the former class kept on
every entry so any part of it is reversible on the user's word.

**What was applied, and what was not.** The three criteria in force — clause (a) (the fifth home
case, [[OI-268]]), the delegation bar (**D-432**), and **D-430**'s *both* halves — applied together
to every entry of the population. The delegation-specificity criterion and the document-kind test
are **superseded** by D-430's own provenance and were not applied as rival tests. Every figure is a
field of `tools/audit/decisions/phase1q_reclassification.json`; none is transcribed here (**D-431**).

**★ The one load-bearing interpretation, stated rather than slipped in.** D-430 admits a section
that a delegation reaches *"BY NAME"*. Seven of the admitted documents are delegated as WHOLE
DOCUMENTS — *"The ratified contract for this layer is X"* — naming no section at all. Read
strictly, no section of those seven is named and every entry in them would fall to `gap`. The pass
takes the other reading — a whole-document delegation reaches every section — **on the record's own
precedent and not on preference**: [[OI-290]]'s row states it in terms, *"`ARCHITECTURE.md:1331`
delegates 'this layer' and admits all sixteen of that document's entries"*, recorded there as the
reason the narrower reading was not adopted. Both readings are written out at
`backbone_decisions.json` → `section_home_criterion.whole_document_reading`, so the choice is
visible and reversible rather than buried in a generator.

**Three things the pass changed that are worth a reader's eye, none of them a delegation problem.**
(i) `cowork_progression_schema_dictionary.md`'s nine entries move IN — they were `gap` under the
phase-1i test because the document self-declares a v1 draft, and D-430 says in terms that a
document's kind and its status banner are not the test. That is [[OI-281]]'s open question,
answered by a ruling already made. (ii) One entry falls out of `cowork_layer3_keymode_design.md`,
whose delegation is the bar's strongest admitted form: D-350 is homed in the document's STATUS
BANNER, which records delivery state. (iii) Three fall out of `cowork_structural_integrity_audit.md`
§3.1, a build-status record. Both are the KIND half doing exactly what it was ruled to do, and both
remedies are at the document, not at the delegation.

**The applier.** `tools/audit/decisions/gen_home_classification.py`, whose `--check` re-derives the
classification and the artifact together. It **replaces** `gen_section_homes.py`, which applied
D-430 alone to phase 1n's staged five documents; two appliers of one criterion is the duplication
#6 forbids, so that file is deleted rather than left beside it.
