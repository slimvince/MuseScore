# OI-296 — Sweep for ratified rules parked in tracking sections — D-341 is unlikely to be the only one

> STATUS IS AUTHORITATIVE IN THE INDEX (OPEN_ITEMS.md) — this file carries narrative and provenance only and is NEVER the status of record

**Section: doc-sync / decisions register. Rowed 2026-08-03 (CC, phase 1s, Task 3) by the dispatch's
own instruction — row the sibling sweep, do not run it.**

## Why this row exists

[[OI-295]] found ONE ratified rule homed in an open-items list and moved it to the section it amends.
Nothing about how it got there was special to that document. A design document accumulates its
open-items section as it is built, a ruling arrives against an item in that list, and the ruling is
recorded where the item already sits rather than where the rule belongs. That is a habit, not an
accident, and a habit produces more than one instance.

[[OI-290]] is the mirror shape at a second document — `cowork_score_census.md` §8c, a rule-stating
section that also holds a findings table. Two instances of one underlying fact, stated at OI-295:
**the section is the unit of the home criterion, and these documents were not written in sections
that separate rules from findings.**

## What the sweep is, and what it is not

**It is not a re-run of the classification.** The classification already reports the cases it can
see, and after the OI-295 move exactly two sections judged RECORDS FINDINGS still hold entries —
`tools/audit/decisions/phase1q_reclassification.json` → `by_document`, the sections whose
`states_rules` is `false`. Both are already named at `not_write_list_cases`: one decision homed in a
STATUS BANNER, and three homed in a BUILD-STATUS RECORD. Their remedy is at the document, exactly as
D-341's was.

**The sweep is the part the classification CANNOT see.** It reaches only sections that hold a
register entry, and only documents in the home population. Three classes sit outside it:

1. **A ratified rule in a tracking section that no register entry records at all.** The register was
   built by reading documents in full, but the reading is [[OI-207]]'s and is not complete; a rule in
   an unread document's §15 is invisible to every mechanism here.
2. **A ratified rule in a tracking section of a document that is nobody's home**, and therefore not
   in the classification's population of 22.
3. **A rule in a section that holds entries but was judged STATES RULES on the strength of its other
   content** — the [[OI-290]] direction. A mixed section admitted as rule-stating hides a findings
   row; a mixed section excluded as findings-recording hides a rule. Both directions are the same
   defect and only one of them produces a `gap` flag.

## What would make it mechanical

Stated so the sweep is not a re-read of everything. The signal that found D-341 is cheap and
specific: a section whose heading names open items, deferred refinements, status or risks, holding a
paragraph that carries a RATIFICATION MARKER (`★ RATIFIED`, `user-ratified`, `DECIDED`) or an
amendment verb (*"the enumeration is amended"*, *"the set now also includes"*). That is a text search
with a defined population, and under **D-436** it would owe a measured detection rate against the two
instances already known and a measured false-positive rate on legitimate tracking prose before it
could be trusted.

## What is not in doubt

That D-341 was one instance and is fixed; that OI-290 is a second instance of the same underlying
fact, unfixed; and that neither was found by looking for the class — both were found by the
classification bumping into them one at a time.
