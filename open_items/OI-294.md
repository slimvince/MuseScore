# OI-294 — Writing the delegations falsified the `CLAUDE.md` rules that asked for them

> STATUS IS AUTHORITATIVE IN THE INDEX (OPEN_ITEMS.md) — this file carries narrative and provenance only and is NEVER the status of record

**Section: doc-sync (#10). Rowed 2026-08-03 (CC, phase 1r, Task 1), while verifying the seven
write-list edits before committing them.**

## What happened

The OI-293 write list was the user's answer to a measured problem: six documents the record treats
as homes had no delegation the bar admits. Writing those delegations fixed that — every one of them
moved, counted at `tools/audit/decisions/phase1q_reclassification.json` → `the_phase_1r_re_run`
([[OI-293]]). But two of them landed in `CLAUDE.md`'s own decisions-register section, beside
the rules that had asked for them, and one landed in `ARCHITECTURE.md` above text those rules cite
by line. Three statements inside USER-RULED text are untrue at HEAD as a result.

## (1) Rule (j) still says no delegation exists

Rule **(j)** (**D-435**, user, 2026-08-03) ends:

> `cowork_engage_arc_plan.md` — a user-ratified surface the criterion USES as a source of
> delegations — is not thereby a delegation TARGET: whether any section of it is a home turns on
> whether some user-ratified surface delegates a concern to it in a form (i) admits, **and today
> none does.**

The write list then wrote one, into `CLAUDE.md` itself, roughly forty lines above (j). The new
paragraph is aware of this and says so in its own closing parenthesis — *"rule (j) records that a
document being a SOURCE of delegations does not make it a delegation TARGET. This paragraph is what
makes it one."* — so the intent is unmistakable and nothing here is a contradiction of substance.
What is stale is the SENTENCE: (j) still asserts the negative in the present tense, and a reader
arriving at (j) first reads a false statement about the file they are holding.

The register repeats it. **D-435**'s `rationale` says *"the bar excludes it as a delegation TARGET,
because the only naming of it is inside a list of citations (`CLAUDE.md:129-130`)"* — the ground for
the ruling when it was made, and no longer a description of the file.

**Why it was not corrected here.** The clause is a substantive claim inside a user ruling, not a
typo. Striking *"and today none does"* changes what a ratified rule says, and the same rule (g)
guard that reserves writing a delegation to the user reserves this too. The dispatch is explicit
that the write-list wordings are the user's act and are not to be revised by the session that
commits them; this is the neighbouring case.

## (2) Rule (i)'s defense cites two line numbers that have moved

Rule **(i)** (**D-432**) grounds itself on the canonical document distinguishing the two acts in
adjacent lines:

> the canonical document distinguishes the two acts in ADJACENT LINES — `ARCHITECTURE.md:1482`
> ends *"Full spec:"* and names its target on the next line, while `:1485` immediately beneath is a
> delegation clause that names its target AND its sections.

Both numbers moved. The same act inserted a delegation pointer for `cowork_prefit_gates.md` near the
top of `ARCHITECTURE.md` and widened two section lists, shifting everything below; and it inserted
the Layer-5 delegation pointer between the two cited lines themselves. At HEAD the *"Full spec:"*
line is `:1493` and the delegation clause is `:1498`, while `:1482` is a provenance continuation line
and `:1485` is a heading.

**This is drift the act caused, not a pre-existing error.** Checked at the committed object rather
than assumed: at `git show 014d3dc129:ARCHITECTURE.md` the *"Full spec:"* line and the delegation
clause sit at exactly the two lines (i) cites, so both citations were correct until this act.

**Why no tool caught it.** The wave re-aimed every drifted register anchor mechanically, each from
the drift report's own line number (`tools/audit/decisions/reaim_home_anchors.py`, whose run prints
the count and the per-entry move). That tool moves the `home`
field of a register entry. A line number quoted inside a rule's PROSE is not a register anchor, is
not in the backbone, and nothing re-aims it — so this class of staleness survives a clean
`--verify`. That is the part of this row worth keeping after the two citations are fixed.

## What is not in doubt

The delegations do what they were written to do: all six write-list documents moved, and the
movement is generated, not asserted (`tools/audit/decisions/phase1q_reclassification.json` →
`the_phase_1r_re_run`). The rules' substance is untouched — (i)'s defense still holds, the two acts
are still distinguished in adjacent lines of `ARCHITECTURE.md`, and only the numbers moved.

## Dated note — 2026-08-03 (CC, phase 1s, Task 2): the user ruled all three corrections, and one of the three turned out not to need making

The user ruled on 2026-08-03 (Y2, option 1B): **correct (j) and D-435's rationale; convert (i)'s
defense from line numbers to a description.** Each edit cites that ruling on the surface, so the
authority is readable and not assumed — which is what the section above says was missing when the
statements were found.

**(1) Rule (j).** The clause *"and today none does"* is struck. What replaces it is a statement of
what the two tests ARE — the arc plan is not a delegation target *by the fact of being a source*, and
whether any section of it is a home is the separate question of what delegations exist, answered by
reading them. There is no present-tense claim about any document's standing left in the sentence,
which is the property that matters: the former clause did not go stale because it was wrong when
written, it went stale because it recorded a fact that could change, inside a rule that cannot.

**D-435's rationale** is re-taken on the same principle. It now says what the ruling settles — the
SHAPE of the answer, read the delegations that exist in the forms D-432 admits — and records that the
arc plan then became a home by being delegated to, which is the rule working rather than an exception
to it. Both the former verbatim clause and the former rationale are quoted whole in the entry's
provenance (#12).

**(2) Rule (i).** The defense is now a description: one line ends *"Full spec:"* and names its target
on the next, and the delegation clause immediately beneath names its target and the sections it owns.
The reason for the conversion is written into the rule text rather than only into this row, because
it generalizes past this instance — a line number quoted inside a rule's PROSE is not a register
anchor, so `reaim_home_anchors.py` cannot maintain it and it goes stale on the next insertion above
it. A pointer to `tools/audit/decisions/phase1p_delegation_bar.json` → `the_defense` is added, where
both lines are located and quoted FROM THE FILE by a generator, so the evidence stays checkable
without a number that can drift.

**★ D-432's VERBATIM was not re-taken, and that is a finding rather than an omission.** The dispatch
directs *"Re-take D-432's verbatim from the corrected text"*. Read at the register, the quoted block
ends at *"…a parenthetical recording where something was ratified."* — and the corrected *Why:*
clause sits immediately BELOW it, outside the quote. The stale line numbers were never in the
verbatim at all; they were in the rule's prose beside it. D-432's `rationale` needed no change either:
it already carried the description form and already cited the generated artifact. So the entry's
correction is entirely in its provenance, where the former wording is preserved.

That is worth stating because it sharpens this row's own point. The reason no tool caught the drift
is not merely that a prose line number is not an anchor — it is that **the drifted text was outside
every field the register machinery reads at all**. Nothing in the register described it, so nothing
could check it.

**What the re-aim did do.** Twenty-eight register anchors drifted from this wave's edits and were
re-aimed one by one from the drift report's own line numbers. The drifts were **+13**, **+9** and
**+6** across three files — not one shift — which is the standing reason the per-citation rule
exists.
