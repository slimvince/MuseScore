# OI-326 — Whether `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s doc-governance hierarchy clause is itself a DELEGATION under D-432 is unsettled — and it decides a whole class of documents at once

> STATUS IS AUTHORITATIVE IN THE INDEX (OPEN_ITEMS.md) — this file carries narrative and provenance only and is NEVER the status of record

**Found 2026-08-04 (CC, READ WAVE 3, dispatch `cc_instruction_reads_3.md`), grading four newly-named
home documents against the delegation bar.**

## What was being done when this surfaced

`gen_phase1p_delegation_bar.FORMS` **stops** on a home document with no authored grade, by design.
Read wave 3 made eleven documents somebody's home for the first time. Seven are named in none of the
three ratified surfaces, which the tool re-verifies mechanically — no judgment. **Four are named**,
and unlike wave 2's population they therefore needed a reading judgment against **D-432**'s four
forms.

## The clause, and the two readings

`ARCHITECTURE.md:346-349`:

> **Doc governance (2026-06-29) — the hierarchy.** This is **THE canonical architecture doc**. The
> **per-layer / per-component design docs** (`cowork_layer*_design.md`,
> `cowork_progression_schema_dictionary.md`, `cowork_progression_schema_design.md`, the
> phrase-boundary design, …) are the **authoritative detail** for their own scope — the rules, the
> mechanisms, the per-layer decisions-with-alternatives — and are **referenced** from here.

**Reading A — a delegation clause.** The predicate delegates a stated concern (the rules, the
mechanisms, the decisions-with-alternatives for the document's own scope) to the named documents.
That is the shape of the bar's first admitted form, *"The ratified contract for this layer is X"*:
the naming is what the sentence is about, not something appended to it.

**Reading B — a naming inside a list.** D-432's not-admitted forms include *"a provenance
attribution, meaning a naming inside a list of citations"*. The documents here sit in a
parenthesised list, ending in an ellipsis.

## Why it cannot be settled one document at a time

The clause names a **class**. Whichever reading is taken applies to every member at once:

- documents already classed `contract-home` on their **own separate anchors** — the progression
  schema dictionary, the layer design documents — where the two readings agree and nothing moves;
- documents whose **only** naming is this clause, where the two readings disagree and the class of
  every entry homed in them turns on it.

So a per-document judgment is not available: taking reading A for one member takes it for all.

## Two smaller instances of the same question, found in the same pass

Recorded here rather than merged into the row above, because each could be answered differently.

1. **The joint-estimator architecture is named in BOTH an admitting and an excluded form.** The
   canonical document's opening banner (`:3-4`) declares a user-ratified **GOVERNING DECISION** and
   names the document as where it is read (*"— see …"*); `:48` names the same document as
   *"spec: …"*, which is the bar's not-admitted example word for word. The grade turns entirely on
   which naming is the stronger, and the bar's own defense is about exactly this pair of acts.
2. **A form that admits over a section-kind that does not.** `ARCHITECTURE.md:994-995` names the
   evidence inventory in a subject-is-X form — *"The catalog of what each layer discovers is
   `cowork_evidence_inventory.md`"* — whose predicate delegates, and binds an obligation to it
   (kept in step with the layer specifications as facts are adopted). But **D-430**'s other half
   asks whether the section STATES RULES, and that document's own §9 says in terms that *"Nothing
   in this document is a build decision — it is the menu the design conversation orders from."* So
   the form may admit where the kind half fails, and the two halves must be applied in that order.

## What this wave did instead of deciding

- Graded each of the four on the **strongest naming**, honestly, and recorded the naming and the
  reasoning **per document** in `gen_phase1p_delegation_bar.FORMS`, including the alternative
  reading where one exists.
- Entered **all 44** wave-3 register entries as documentation gaps — the class the record already
  carries — so **nothing is promoted to `contract-home` on a session's reading**.
- Left `gen_home_classification.py`'s apply mode **unrun**, as [[OI-305]] and [[OI-319]] require, so
  no derived class moves by this wave's doing either.

## Why the ruling is the user's

Two reasons, both structural rather than deferential. Rule (g)'s guard is that **the delegation
confers, and only the user writes a delegation into `ARCHITECTURE.md`** — so a session reading a
clause more generously than the user wrote it would manufacture the authority the rule exists to
withhold. And **D-432**'s defense is drawn from the canonical document's own distinctions, so
extending it to a clause that document did not write as a delegation is a change to the bar rather
than an application of it.

**The mechanism for settling it already exists and needs no criterion change:** the OI-293 write
list. A delegation clause the user writes for any document decides that document without touching
D-432 at all.

## Note, 2026-08-04 (CC, READ WAVE 4, dispatch `cc_instruction_reads_4.md`) — the user ruled, and the ruling was measured before it landed

**The ruling.** Reading A, with one boundary the row above did not draw: the clause **IS** a
delegation under **D-432**, and it delegates **only to the members it names EXPLICITLY**. The glob
`cowork_layer*_design.md` and the trailing ellipsis **confer nothing**. The reason is the one this
row's own "why the ruling is the user's" section anticipated from the other side: a delegation whose
membership is indeterminate could be extended by a session, and extending a delegation is the
authority rule (g) reserves to the user. **D-432 is not amended** — the ruling applies its logic.
Homed as `CLAUDE.md` rule **(k)**; register **D-546**.

**Both smaller instances above are settled, and neither needed a new rule.** The first — the
joint-estimator architecture named in both an admitting and an excluded form — is answered by
**(k1)**: the strongest naming governs, and being cited elsewhere in a weaker form does not undo a
delegation. The second — a form that admits over a section-kind that does not — is answered by
**(k2)**: **D-430** requires both halves, the kind half is decisive, and the halves are applied form
first and kind second. Both are recorded as **confirmations** of the entries they confirm, in
D-432's and D-430's own provenance, rather than as entries of their own; a register entry restating
a decision another entry already holds is the duplication #6 forbids.

**The measurement the user made a CONDITION of applying it (ruling R5), and what it found.** Before
the split landed, the population it moves was measured: the clause's own membership under reading A,
parsed from the clause rather than listed; which of those documents are already `contract-home` on
separate anchors of their own; and which grades therefore move. **Nothing moves.** The reason,
stated because it is more useful than the number: the register's grades were always made on the
STRONGEST naming each document has, so for every glob-matched and prose-referenced member that
naming is a separate anchor — an explicit delegation clause for four of them, a bare appended
citation for two, and no naming at all for the rest — and **exactly one home document in the whole
register was ever graded on this clause**, which is one of the two explicitly-named members the
ruling keeps. Every count is generated at `tools/audit/decisions/reads4_oi326_application.json`; none
is restated here (**D-431**).

**One defect in this wave's own measurement, caught by reading its first output rather than by
reasoning about it.** The tool first took the clause's span to be the whole blockquote, and parsed
`cowork_target_architecture.md` out of it as an explicitly-named delegatee — from the sentence two
lines below the list that **DEMOTES** that document (*"not a second canonical doc"*), which is the
opposite act. The span was narrowed to the delegating sentence and the parenthesised list it
governs, and the reason is recorded in the artifact itself rather than only in the tool.

**What is carried forward, and to where (ruling R2).** The four documents the clause reaches only by
glob or by prose reference — `cowork_layer1_note_model_design.md`,
`cowork_layer2_slicing_design.md`, `cowork_layer6_grouping_design.md` and
`cowork_phrase_boundary_design.md` — are on the [[OI-293]] write list, each with a drafted
delegation for the user to write or reject. Two of them carry a question the draft does not answer
and that is stated with them: the Layer-6 document is a contract for a layer **D-266** currently
prohibits, and the phrase-boundary reference has more than one candidate document, no section of
`ARCHITECTURE.md` owning the primitive it names.

**What this wave did NOT do.** No entry's home class moved; `gen_home_classification.py`'s apply
mode stays UNRUN under [[OI-305]]/[[OI-319]]; no delegation was written into `ARCHITECTURE.md`
(rule (g)'s guard); and the prose reference was routed rather than read in, because R1's words name
the glob and the ellipsis and a prose description is neither.
