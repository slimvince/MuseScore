# OI-277 — The emission is NOTE-COUNT-weighted, so octave doubling moves committed readings on 13.2 % of segments: the third face of the what-does-the-emission-read family

## The finding

The joint estimator's emission and spelling factors are summed **per note record**, so a tone
that sounds in two octaves contributes its term twice. Doubling one upper voice an octave up —
a transform that changes no pitch class, no bass, and no event lattice — moved the committed
reading on **13.2 % of segments** (354 of 408 survived), **re-cut the boundary structure on 9 of
12 pieces**, and lowered every piece's total score.

The measured violation classes, each diagnosed at the source with per-factor deltas:

| class | measured case |
|---|---|
| quality inflation — a doubled tone is promoted into the chord | `bwv2.6@5760`, G minor `V\|Maj` → `V\|Dom7`; the doubled pc 0 is a non-member under the triad (emission delta −2.905) and a member under the tetrad (−0.069); content margin +0.483 → +0.959 |
| a KEY relabel driven purely by the SPELLING factor | `bwv153.1@18000`, `VII\|Dim7` in E major → `VII\|Dim7/V` in A minor; the emission deltas are IDENTICAL for both readings (−0.121 each) and the spelling factor decides alone (chromatic-flat against an E-major tonic, diatonic against A minor); margin −0.232 → +0.281 |
| wholesale re-segmentation | 9 of 12 pieces; e.g. `bwv398` ticks 12480–15840 re-read from a D-major V/I alternation to a retonicized A-major I block; `bwv10.7` ticks 5280–7680 re-cut 2 segments into 3 |

`bass`, `missing_tone` and `boundary` raw deltas are exactly 0.0 in every probed state — only
`emission` and `spelling` move, which is what the pre-run source determination predicted.

## What this is NOT — the classification that decides where it belongs

**This is not the [[OI-228]] class.** OI-228 is a fit/decode MISMATCH: the factor-presence table
was fitted on OVERLAP membership and the decode reads it through an onset lens. Here fit and
decode AGREE — the emission table was counted per note (`gen_note_tables.py:385-410`,
`emission[combo][cat] += 1` per note whose onset falls in a kept ground-truth segment) and the
decode sums per note (`probe_decoder.py:747-753`). Per-note is the **trained semantics of the
model**, so the doubling sensitivity is a MODEL PROPERTY BY DESIGN, not a decoder defect and not
a conformance violation. Nothing here is a knob to turn: moving to a pitch-class-level or
voice-deduplicated emission would be a model change requiring a refit.

## Why it is rowed anyway

Because it answers the same question three other rows circle — **what does the emission read?**
[[OI-228]] asks struck versus sounding; [[OI-243]] found the decode is not
transposition-equivariant because the spelling factor anchors each key at a canonical per-tonic
spelling; [[OI-246]] found concert and written pitch mixed inside one record. This row adds the
third face: **notes versus pitch classes**. Under the standing rule that one fix is designed once
over the whole enumerated family and never per symptom (`CLAUDE.md` Conventions, user-ruled
2026-07-28), the input-representation design cannot be settled without this measurement on the
table.

Two consequences worth stating for the phase-3 plan:

1. **The musical fact that a doubling leaves the sonority's harmonic identity unchanged is not
   represented in the per-note likelihood.** Whether it should be is a design question, and the
   probe measures its size rather than presuming its answer.
2. **Orchestral repertoire is where doubling is the norm** ([[OI-209]]), and the fitted corpus is
   326 four-part Bach chorales, where it is not. So the measured 13.2 % is an in-envelope figure
   for a phenomenon whose out-of-envelope incidence is larger by construction and unmeasured.
