## The two halves, stated apart because that is the whole subject

**D-486** is the convention that a measurement publishes its **coverage denominator** and its
**per-corpus breakdown**, and that a single aggregate value hiding which corpus moved is not
reported. Its two halves are covered very differently.

- **The DENOMINATOR half is mechanical where it matters.** Gate block (A)'s figures are regenerated
  from the measurement's own summary by `tools/robust_stop_restamp.py`, which is established by
  reproducing the outgoing manifest exactly. A published value therefore carries the block it was
  computed from, and cannot be stated at a denominator the run did not have.
- **The PER-CORPUS half is not mechanised at all.** The gate corpora are partitioned per **preset**,
  which is a different cut answering a different question — presets are a presentation concern and
  inference is preset-independent — so the existing split is not a repertoire split and does not
  become one by resembling it. Nothing checks that a value reported over several repertoires is
  broken down by them. `tools/audit/process_check.py` carries only the adjacent shape: it flags a
  bare quantity, not an aggregate that hides which corpus moved.

## Why the gap is a DEFECT rather than an absence — the ruling's own ground

The triage's defect class is for a rule where a mechanical form exists that would catch real
violations without firing on legitimate work, and nothing implements it. The ruling puts this half
there on the convention's **own stated reason**: a single aggregate can hide which corpus moved, and
the reason that matters is measurement integrity — principle **#9** (measure only on corpora known to
be non-stale and accurate) and principle **#24** (every reported value carries its uncertainty; a
difference inside the uncertainty is not a finding). A convention adopted for measurement integrity
whose enforcing half is missing is a gap in the mechanisation, not a rule nobody happened to
automate.
