# OI-295 — A user-ratified rule left with no home because its section is an open-items list

> STATUS IS AUTHORITATIVE IN THE INDEX (OPEN_ITEMS.md) — this file carries narrative and provenance only and is NEVER the status of record

**Section: doc-sync / decisions register. Rowed 2026-08-03 (CC, phase 1r, Task 4), by the
re-classification pass itself.**

## The finding

**D-341** — *the licensed root-motion set is completed by theory; the ascending fifth, the descending
second and the diatonic diminished fifth are added* — is a RULE, and its own verbatim records
*"★ RATIFIED by the user 2026-07-03; the §5.0 enumeration is amended"*. It is homed at
`cowork_layer5_function_design.md` **§15, "Open items & deferred refinements"**.

The delegation the user wrote for that document names the whole document, so under the granularity
clause of `CLAUDE.md` rule (h) it reaches every section, §15 included. The pass then applied the
rule-stating half and judged §15 **RECORDS FINDINGS**. D-341 therefore stays `gap` while the rest of
the document's entries move to `contract-home` (the per-document counts are at
`tools/audit/decisions/phase1q_reclassification.json` → `by_document`).

## Why §15 was judged that way, and why the alternative was not taken

The ground is the **user's own adjacent judgment of the same day**, not this session's taste. The
voice-leading-span criterion, widened in the same write-list act, names §0/§5.1/§5.3/§8/§9 and says
in terms:

> §15/§16 are deliberately NOT named — they are ratification asks, not rule-stating sections.

That is about `cowork_voiceleading_axis_design.md`, a sibling design document whose §15 carries the
identical heading, *"Open items & deferred refinements"*. Judging the two differently would make a
mechanical criterion turn on which document a reader happened to open, which is the case-by-case
exception **D-435** was ruled to avoid.

The section's own content agrees. Read at `cowork_layer5_function_design.md` §15, its body is
delivery status — the status markers it opens with, a demotion, deferrals — which is what a tracking
surface does. Item 12 is the exception, and it is the whole of this row.

**The alternative is recorded and not taken.** Judging §15 rule-stating would move D-341 and close
this row on the spot. It is refused twice over: it contradicts the user's judgment of the same day
on the identically-titled sibling section, and it would admit a delivery-status list as a contract
home, which is the thing the kind half exists to prevent.

## This is [[OI-290]]'s shape, at a second document

OI-290 records `cowork_score_census.md` §8c as a rule-stating section that also holds a findings
table, with most admitted entries being rows of that table. This is the mirror image: a
findings-recording section that also holds one ratified rule. Both are the same underlying fact —
**the section is the unit of the criterion, and these documents were not written in sections that
separate rules from findings.** The census and this layer specification are the second instance of
one shape, which is what makes it a class rather than an accident, and is the reason to row it
rather than note it.

## The remedy, and why it was not executed here

At the **DOCUMENT**, not at the classification and not at the delegation: move the ratified amendment
out of the open-items list into the rule-stating section it amends (§5.0), in the
[[OI-269]]/[[OI-282]] shape — preserve and re-section, never rewrite. No delegation can repair it,
because the delegation already reaches the section.

Not executed in this wave: the dispatch licenses the register, the rows, `CLAUDE.md` and the guards,
and re-sectioning a SIGNED layer specification is none of those.

## What is not in doubt

That D-341 is a rule and was ratified; that the delegation reaches §15; that §15 also records
findings. The disagreement available to the user is only about which of those the kind half should
weigh, and both readings are written out above.

## Dated note — 2026-08-03 (CC, phase 1s, Task 3): the remedy is executed, and half of it was already in place

The user ruled the remedy this row proposed (Y3, option 2B — fix it at the document). It is done, and
the row flips.

**★ One premise did not hold, and it changed what the act was.** The remedy above, and the dispatch
that carried it, both say *move the rule text into §5.0*. Read at the file before moving anything,
**§5.0 already carried the amendment's enumeration** — the three added motions, spelled out, with the
implementation and the spec-ahead-of-code state. It was written there when the amendment was
ratified, and the §15 text says so in its own words: *"extend `isLicensedProgression` (+ this §5.0's
enumeration, now done)"*. Moving the §15 block wholesale would therefore have produced a second copy
of the rule in the same document — the duplication #6 forbids, arrived at by following an instruction
whose purpose was to satisfy #7.

So what moved is the part §15 alone held, and it is exactly the part that makes a rule a rule with a
defense:

- the **ratification** and its finding date — *found 2026-07-02 by the D5 consistency check; ★
  RATIFIED by the user 2026-07-03*;
- the characterization **algorithmic completion per theory, NOT tuning**, which is the ground on
  which the amendment is not per-case tuning;
- the **evidence** — the 6-entry/11-motion failure table, pinned in the consumer's own consistency
  test, with the corrected count and the note that the earlier figure was an arithmetic error.

Moved unchanged (#12). §5.0 now states the rule and carries its defense at its home, which is the
standing requirement that every design decision carries its defense where the decision lives.

**§15 item 12 is kept, as a dated pointer.** It records that the amendment moved and where, and
restates none of it. The tracking history survives, the cross-references from item 13 (*"item 12"*,
*"the §15-12 increment's ripple"*) still resolve, and the code increment is still recorded as owed —
in §5.0, where the rule now is.

**One consequential edit rides with the move**, and it is stated rather than slipped in: §5.0's D5
dependency-map block ended with a parenthesis pointing at §15-12 as where the grammar gaps are *"to
be completed"*. That pointer was true before the move and false after it, so it now names the
amendment above it. Nothing else in the document was touched.

**The classification confirms it.** D-341 moves `gap` → `contract-home`, decided by *"the delegation
reaches this section and it STATES RULES"* (`tools/audit/decisions/phase1q_reclassification.json`),
and `cowork_layer5_function_design.md` drops out of the list of write-list documents still holding a
`gap` entry. Its verbatim and anchor are re-taken from §5.0, the former preserved in the entry's
provenance.

**The §15 kind judgment is retired, not deleted.** With no entry left in §15, the authored
section-kind row for it decides nothing, and the pass stops on a judgment that decides nothing rather
than carrying it silently. The judgment — that §15 RECORDS FINDINGS, on the user's own adjacent
ruling of the same day about the identically-titled §15 of the sibling voice-leading design — is
preserved whole in D-341's provenance. It is the reason this row existed, so losing it would have
erased the finding along with the fault.
