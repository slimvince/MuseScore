## How it was found

By the both-ways decision table the user's **Ruling 25** requires. That table decides every index
row under the standing rule and under the proposed one, and in doing so it enumerates the rows
NEITHER rule reaches — which is where this sits.

## The mechanism, read at the tool and at the index

The ONE index parser (#6) decides a row is resolved by looking for the ✅ mark in its status cell.
The open-items register's own **rule (d)** requires a resolution to flip the INDEX row with
provenance and **does not mandate the glyph**. So a row that states its resolution in words is
following the register's rules and is nonetheless counted OPEN by every derivation that reads the
index: the open-row count, the true-half cuts, the finish line's populations and the apparatus
declaration's candidate cut alike.

## What was measured, and the reading that is authored rather than pattern-matched

Three cells open with a resolution word and carry no mark anywhere. **The per-row reading is
authored, and it does not claim all three are defects:**

- **Two** open with *RESOLVED* and a date, and are closed rows counted as open. A plain defect.
- **The third states a MIXED status** — superseded in fact on one surface, still live on the legacy
  notation path and retiring with it. Which way a single open/resolved bit should fall for that row
  is not settled by its own text, and this pass does not settle it.

Every identity, every cell opening and the per-row reading are at
`tools/audit/oi356_parser_correction.json`; none is restated here (**D-431**).

## Why it is rowed apart from [[OI-356]]

[[OI-356]] is a cell reading as RESOLVED when the row is open. This is a cell reading as OPEN when
the row is resolved. **Same parser, opposite direction** — and a remedy for one does not touch the
other. Enumerating the family is what lets the standing one-fix-per-family rule design both at once
instead of patching whichever is currently visible.

## What is NOT proposed here

Teaching the parser to recognise a word-spelled resolution is a **mechanism change**, which
**D-436** reserves to the user, and it would move verdicts no ruling names — which is exactly the
condition that made this task's own proposed correction a STOP.

**The alternative remedy is a CONVENTION rather than a mechanism**: require the glyph at the head of
every resolved status cell, and the parser needs no change at all. Choosing between a parser change
and a convention is the same user decision, and neither is taken here.

## What is NOT in doubt

- **The rows themselves**, whose own text says what their status is.
- **The register's rule (d)**, which this does not propose changing.
- Nothing about the analysis, any measurement of it, any golden, or anything in
  `tools/robust_stop/`.

## Why no gating verdict is written here

A non-gating verdict is DERIVED from the apparatus cut and never hand-written. And this is not an
establishment obligation on a published rate: the parser produces populations for tracking
artifacts, not a measured value about the analysis — the same reasoning [[OI-356]]'s own row
records.
