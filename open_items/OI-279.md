## The finding

`ARCHITECTURE.md` §6.7 ("The canonical style taxonomy (shared with the Harmonic Vocabulary)",
`:4526-4536`) states the style vocabulary as the **hand-made genre taxonomy** — "common-practice /
jazz / vernacular families — Baroque, Classical/galant, Romantic; trad, swing/songbook, bebop,
hard-bop, cool, modal; blues, ragtime, gospel-soul, rock, pop, folk, barbershop" — and characterises
it as "a **theory-based v1**" whose empirical grounding, "deriving the clusters *and* the per-style
weights by clustering corpora", is future work.

`cowork_progression_schema_dictionary.md` §12.1 (`:317-330`) records that this was **superseded**:

- the taxonomy is now the **five-idiom set** — Diatonic-functional · Chromatic-functional ·
  Seventh-functional · Triadic-modal · Chromatic-coloristic — with the two orthogonal cross-attributes
  *mode* and *chromaticism*, **RATIFIED 2026-06-30** (`cowork_style_taxonomy_proposal.md`) and
  **ENCODED** (the StyleTag swap; `enum class Idiom` + `IdiomSet` in the dormant `harmonicvocabulary`
  component);
- it "**supersedes** the hand-made genre taxonomy below (kept only as historical context)" — and the
  superseded list printed there is, word for word, the list §6.7 still presents as current;
- the placeholder `{Baroque, Jazz, Default}` StyleTag is "**retired**" (`:239`);
- the five idioms are **empirically discovered**, not theory-based, so §6.7's "theory-based v1" is
  false in the other direction too;
- what remains future work is narrower than §6.7 says: the **per-preset weights** by clustering
  (`cowork_style_clustering_plan.md`), not the clusters themselves.

**Three things make this sharper than the ordinary stale-tense case.**

1. **It is in the canonical document.** D-091 — `ARCHITECTURE.md` wins every disagreement — means a
   reader who follows the recorded hierarchy correctly arrives at the retired taxonomy and is right to
   prefer it over the dictionary.
2. **§6.7 cites the very document that records the supersession** as its own authority: "Full proposal
   + the surveyed corpora: `cowork_progression_schema_dictionary.md` §6/§12". A reader who follows the
   pointer meets the contradiction; a reader who does not, does not.
3. **Two register entries quote the stale text as their verbatim** — **D-131** (`ARCHITECTURE.md:4528`)
   and **D-132** (`:4532`) — so the register currently records the retired taxonomy as the standing
   decision, with D-132's status DEFERRED on a piece of future work whose first half is already done.

## What is NOT in doubt

The dictionary's own content, read in full this wave and yielding nine register entries
(**D-406…D-414**). Nor the five-idiom ratification itself: the dictionary states its date, its
proposal document, and that it is encoded, and `ARCHITECTURE.md` §6.7 does not contradict the
ratification — it simply predates it.

## Disposition

A doc-sync act at `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s next touch, plus the two register entries' verbatims re-taken
from the corrected text (D-131 restated over the five idioms; **D-132 needs a status decision, not
just a re-quote** — whether "empirically grounding it is committed future work" is now DEFERRED over
