# OI-281 — Does an `ARCHITECTURE.md` delegation pointer confer contract-home status on a document that self-declares "v1 draft"?

## The question

The fifth home case — a ratified contract document the owning `ARCHITECTURE.md` section points at is
a proper home — was user-ratified 2026-08-02 at [[OI-268]] with a **two-part criterion**: the document
is ratified or signed **and** carries a status banner, **and** the owning section delegates to it by
pointer. The transitive refinement of the same date adds that the ratification may be the user's
**directly OR TRANSITIVELY** — a user-ratified surface explicitly delegating to the document carries
the user's authority to it — with the guard that **an assistant's stamp alone never confers
contract-home status** (`CLAUDE.md`, decisions-register rule (g)).

`cowork_progression_schema_dictionary.md` satisfies the **delegation** half and fails the
**ratification** half in a way none of the other candidates showed:

- **Delegation, satisfied.** `ARCHITECTURE.md:4589-4591` (§7, The Knowledge Base) names the document
  as the component's own specification: "formalised as an **independent knowledge-base component**
  with its own spec (`cowork_progression_schema_dictionary.md`)". §6.7 points at it a second time,
  for the style taxonomy (`:4579-4580`).
- **Ratification, not satisfied as written.** The document's own banner reads "**Status: component
  spec, v1 draft (2026-06-29)**" (`:3`). A draft names no ratifier — neither the user's directly, nor
  a delegating surface's transitively.

Nine register entries (**D-406…D-414**) are homed there and carry the `⚠gap` flag for exactly this
reason. They were ratified as *decisions* by the user on 2026-08-03; that ratification is of each
rule, and it does not decide the document's home class, which is what this row asks.

## The two readings, stated without choosing

**(i) Transitive authority reaches it.** The delegating surface — `ARCHITECTURE.md` §7 — is
user-ratified, and it delegates the component's specification to this document by name. On the
transitive clause that is the user's authority carried across, and the document's own banner wording
is then a stale self-description rather than a statement of authority. *Consequence:* nine gap flags
close at their cause rather than being tracked one by one, which is what #6 asks of a defect with a
single source.

**(ii) It does not.** Rule (g)'s transitive clause was ratified together with the guard that an
assistant's stamp alone never confers contract-home status. A self-declared draft names **no**
ratifier at all — not the user's, not an assistant's — so there is nothing for the delegation to
carry across; a pointer establishes *where the detail lives*, not *that the detail was decided*.
*Consequence:* the nine stay flagged until the document is ratified or re-bannered, which is itself a
user act.

Phase 1i's own precedent cuts both ways and is recorded here so the ruling does not have to rediscover
it: `cowork_structural_integrity_audit.md` was excluded from contract-home status even though its
transitive ratification was judged genuine, because the second half of the criterion failed — the
same shape as (ii). Against that, `cowork_layer5_engagement_design.md` was admitted on transitive
authority alone, its banner reading only "DESIGN (CC, 2026-07-07)" — an assistant's own status word,
admitted because the user-ratified arc plan delegated to it by name three times. Whether "DESIGN (CC)"
and "v1 draft" are the same case is precisely what is unsettled.

## The scope — why this is not one document's problem

**The answer applies to every draft-bannered document `ARCHITECTURE.md` points at, not to this one.**
That is why it is a user ruling and not a homing act: settling it for the dictionary alone would leave
the criterion ambiguous for the next document that arrives in the same state, and the register would
then carry two documents in the same condition with different home classes.

It is the sibling of [[OI-268]]'s other still-open question — **whether a COWORK ratification counts**,
which decides **twenty-one** entries (`docs/decoder_design.md` 4 + `cowork_score_census.md` 17). Both
ask what kind of authority the fifth home case requires; they should be ruled together, and the two
answers must be consistent with each other.

## What is NOT in doubt

The document's content, read in full at phase 1j and yielding the nine register entries; the nine
rules themselves, user-ratified 2026-08-03; and the delegation pointer, verified at
`ARCHITECTURE.md:4589-4591` in this session.

## The criterion as ruled

*A document is a contract home when a user-ratified surface delegates to it **BY NAME**, **FOR A
STATED CONCERN**, and the document is **stable enough to be cited**.* The banner's self-description
is not the test; the delegation's specificity is. Rule (g)'s guard survives intact — an assistant's
stamp confers nothing, because the delegation does the conferring and only the user writes a
delegation into `ARCHITECTURE.md`.

## 2. Do the two precedents come out right?

**Both land where the precedent has them, so this is NOT a #13 STOP.** But how they land matters:

- **`cowork_layer5_engagement_design.md` — ADMITTED. ✅ And on its own original ground.** The arc
  plan's three by-name delegations (`:41`, `:46`, `:53-55`) carry it independently of
  `ARCHITECTURE.md:1483`, which is important because `:1483` was itself WRITTEN at phase 1i/1j as
  the remedy for the missing delegation. Had the pointer been its only support, the criterion would
  have been admitting a document on a pointer the classification itself produced.
- **`cowork_structural_integrity_audit.md` — EXCLUDED. ✅ But on clause (c) ALONE, and for a
  DIFFERENT reason than phase 1i used.** On clauses (a) and (b) it **passes**: `cowork_engage_arc_plan.md:4`
  (a user-ratified surface) says "It does not re-derive the fix details — **those live in
  `cowork_structural_integrity_audit.md`** (§3 fix-queue, §4 sequencing)" — by name, for a stated
  concern, as specific a delegation as any in the ADMIT table. What excludes it is "stable enough to
  be cited": its **§5 is headed "UNCLEAR rows for user adjudication"** and lists seven open
  questions, and `:343-346` states its own sequencing call "is brought to the user" — which the
  record does not show happening. Phase 1i excluded it on the OLD criterion's second half (no
  ratified-or-signed banner), a half the new criterion drops.

**The finding: the precedent survives only because clause (c) is a judgment, and clause (c) is doing
all the work in the hardest case.** The criterion moved the test from the document's
self-description to the delegation's specificity — and then the one case it was built to preserve is
decided not by the delegation at all, but by a reading of how settled a document is. That is not a
refutation; it is the shape of the residue, and it is what the ambiguous 33 have in common.

## 3. Do the two sibling populations answer consistently?

They answer under **one criterion applied uniformly**, and they come out **differently** — which is
consistency of method, not of outcome:

- **`cowork_progression_schema_dictionary.md` (9, D-406…D-414) — ADMITTED.** The delegation at
  `ARCHITECTURE.md:4589-4591` names it as the component's own spec, and `:4539` explicitly declines
  to restate its content. The "v1 draft" banner never enters the test.
- **The Cowork-ratification set (21) SPLITS.** `cowork_score_census.md` (17) is **ADMITTED** on two
  independent delegations from two user-ratified surfaces. `docs/decoder_design.md` (4) is
  **EXCLUDED** — it is not named in `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `CLAUDE.md` or the arc plan at all.

**The material finding here is what the criterion does to [[OI-268]]'s sibling question.** That
question — *does a COWORK ratification count?* — is not answered by this criterion. It is **made
moot**: the criterion never asks who ratified the document, only who delegates to it. Ruling the
criterion therefore **dissolves** the sibling question rather than deciding it, and the 21 entries
split 17/4 on grounds that have nothing to do with Cowork ratification. Whether dissolution is the
answer the user intends for OI-268 is not something this measurement can settle, and it is stated
here rather than assumed.

## 4. What a re-classification would cost, if ordered

Two acts, and they are not the same size:

1. **The clean cases — 36 entries move** (26 in, 10 out), each with the delegation citation recorded
   in its provenance and the former class preserved (#12). Mechanical, one regeneration, the three
   guards. Comparable in size to the phase-1i wave.
2. **The ambiguous 33 need a second ruling, not a session's judgment.** They divide into two
   questions the user would have to answer, and both are general rather than per-document: **does a
   bare citation of the form "Full spec: `X`" delegate, or only cite?** (that decides
   `cowork_layer5_function_design.md`, `cowork_voiceleading_axis_design.md` and
   `cowork_prefit_gates.md` — 26 entries); and **is a SHELVED or explicitly-unsettled document
   "stable enough to be cited"?** (that decides `cowork_joint_key_chord_design.md` and, with it, the
   `cowork_structural_integrity_audit.md` precedent — 9 entries).

Applying the criterion to the clean cases while the ambiguous 33 wait would leave the register in a
state where two documents in materially the same condition carry different home classes — the exact
outcome the scope paragraph above gives as the reason this is a user ruling and not a homing act. So
the honest sequencing is: rule the two follow-on questions first, then re-classify once.
