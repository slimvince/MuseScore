# OI-281 — Does an `ARCHITECTURE.md` delegation pointer confer contract-home status on a document that self-declares "v1 draft"?

**Section E — user adjudication. Rowed 2026-08-03 on the user's ruling of the same date (R5(b) of
`cc_instruction_phase1k_apply_rulings.md`), which directed that the question be recorded rather than

## The question

The fifth home case — a ratified contract document the owning `ARCHITECTURE.md` section points at is
a proper home — was user-ratified 2026-08-02 at [[OI-268]] with a **two-part criterion**: the document
is ratified or signed **and** carries a status banner, **and** the owning section delegates to it by
pointer. The transitive refinement of the same date adds that the ratification may be the user's
**directly OR TRANSITIVELY** — a user-ratified surface explicitly delegating to the document carries
the user's authority to it — with the guard that **an assistant's stamp alone never confers
contract-home status** (`CLAUDE.md`, decisions-register rule (g)).

`cowork_progression_schema_dictionary.md` satisfies the **delegation** half and fails the
**ratification** half in a way none of the other candidates showed:

- **Delegation, satisfied.** `ARCHITECTURE.md:4589-4591` (§7, The Knowledge Base) names the document
  as the component's own specification: "formalised as an **independent knowledge-base component**
  with its own spec (`cowork_progression_schema_dictionary.md`)". §6.7 points at it a second time,
  for the style taxonomy (`:4579-4580`).
- **Ratification, not satisfied as written.** The document's own banner reads "**Status: component
  spec, v1 draft (2026-06-29)**" (`:3`). A draft names no ratifier — neither the user's directly, nor
  a delegating surface's transitively.

Nine register entries (**D-406…D-414**) are homed there and carry the `⚠gap` flag for exactly this
reason. They were ratified as *decisions* by the user on 2026-08-03; that ratification is of each
rule, and it does not decide the document's home class, which is what this row asks.

## The two readings, stated without choosing

**(i) Transitive authority reaches it.** The delegating surface — `ARCHITECTURE.md` §7 — is
user-ratified, and it delegates the component's specification to this document by name. On the
transitive clause that is the user's authority carried across, and the document's own banner wording
is then a stale self-description rather than a statement of authority. *Consequence:* nine gap flags
close at their cause rather than being tracked one by one, which is what #6 asks of a defect with a
single source.

**(ii) It does not.** Rule (g)'s transitive clause was ratified together with the guard that an
assistant's stamp alone never confers contract-home status. A self-declared draft names **no**
ratifier at all — not the user's, not an assistant's — so there is nothing for the delegation to
carry across; a pointer establishes *where the detail lives*, not *that the detail was decided*.
*Consequence:* the nine stay flagged until the document is ratified or re-bannered, which is itself a
user act.

Phase 1i's own precedent cuts both ways and is recorded here so the ruling does not have to rediscover
it: `cowork_structural_integrity_audit.md` was excluded from contract-home status even though its
transitive ratification was judged genuine, because the second half of the criterion failed — the
same shape as (ii). Against that, `cowork_layer5_engagement_design.md` was admitted on transitive
authority alone, its banner reading only "DESIGN (CC, 2026-07-07)" — an assistant's own status word,
admitted because the user-ratified arc plan delegated to it by name three times. Whether "DESIGN (CC)"
and "v1 draft" are the same case is precisely what is unsettled.

## The scope — why this is not one document's problem

**The answer applies to every draft-bannered document `ARCHITECTURE.md` points at, not to this one.**
That is why it is a user ruling and not a homing act: settling it for the dictionary alone would leave
the criterion ambiguous for the next document that arrives in the same state, and the register would
then carry two documents in the same condition with different home classes.

It is the sibling of [[OI-268]]'s other still-open question — **whether a COWORK ratification counts**,
which decides **twenty-one** entries (`docs/decoder_design.md` 4 + `cowork_score_census.md` 17). Both
ask what kind of authority the fifth home case requires; they should be ruled together, and the two
answers must be consistent with each other.

## What is NOT in doubt

The document's content, read in full at phase 1j and yielding the nine register entries; the nine
rules themselves, user-ratified 2026-08-03; and the delegation pointer, verified at
`ARCHITECTURE.md:4589-4591` in this session.

## Disposition

**Deliberately NOT resolved by editing the dictionary's banner.** The phase-1k dispatch says so in
terms: re-bannering the document would decide the question by fiat and would decide it for one
document only. If the ruling goes to (i), the nine flags clear by re-classification and the banner is
corrected as a consequence; if it goes to (ii), the flags stand until the document is ratified on its
own. Any banner edit here also wants to land with the [[OI-280]] label-collision fix, so the file is
touched once.
