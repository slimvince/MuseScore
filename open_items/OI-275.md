## The two sides

`cowork_layer4_chordsymbol_design.md` (**SIGNED, user, 2026-06-24**), §4 step 1, verbatim
(`:208-212`, entered this wave as register entry **D-329**):

> 1. **List the possible chords.** From the slice's pitches, generate **every** tertian chord the pitches could spell —
>    each basic type at each root — and score each by how well the pitches fit it. **Completeness is the priority:** a
>    chord never listed can never be chosen, and the measured dominant error is "the right chord was never on the list,"
>    not "the wrong one was picked among good options."

The same document states it twice more, as a weighed architecture decision (§9, *"List
completely, then select — completeness is the lever"*, with a re-scorer over a partial list the
**rejected** alternative) and as a risk (§11, *"Complete listing is the priority risk. A chord
never listed can never be chosen"*).

Against that, the production joint decoder's **candidate admission**: three gates plus a top-6
key prune, already rowed at [[OI-226]] as having **no ratified basis** in the ratified decode
plan. Measured consequences already on the register:

- [[OI-215]]: an event whose every covering window carries fewer than two distinct onset pitch
  classes admits **nothing**, so the whole decode returns **0 segments** — on **13 of the 23
  committed large scores**, every symphony in the set.
- [[OI-227]]: a **second** gate (the fit / non-chord-tone budget) empties the decode at the
  opposite end of the density spectrum — 21 of the 312 uncoverable events over those scores.
- [[OI-188]]: the ground-truth state is reachable on only **~72 %** of spans; it was
  force-added on 14,257 spans at the fit event.

## Why this is a decision-conformance question and not a restatement of OI-226

[[OI-226]]'s finding is an **absence**: the admission rule has no ratified basis. This row's
finding is an **opposition**: there IS a ratified statement about candidate listing, in a signed
layer specification, and it says the opposite of what the production rule does. That changes what
the family design owes — not merely "give admission a basis", but "reconcile the basis you give
it with D-329, or record that D-329 does not reach this estimator".

## The question the record does not answer, and which is the user's

**Does a SIGNED layer specification bind the joint estimator?** The joint estimator replaced the
layered pipeline for inference (**D-001**, user-ratified 2026-07-14/17), and
`cowork_layer4_chordsymbol_design.md` describes a decoder that is built but dormant. Three
readings are possible and the record does not say which holds:

1. **It binds.** The signed specification states a design principle about candidate listing, not
   an implementation detail of one decoder; nothing in the joint-estimator ratification withdraws
   it, so the admission rule is non-conformant and the family design must satisfy it.
2. **It is superseded in fact.** The joint estimator's own factorization is the governing
   specification for its candidate space, and D-329 lapsed with the path it was written for —
   in which case the register needs D-329's status corrected and the joint estimator's
   specification needs its own listing decision, which [[OI-226]] says it does not have.
3. **It is scoped narrower than it reads** — a statement about the vertical scorer's template
   enumeration rather than about the decode's state space — in which case the scoping was never
   written down.

Deciding between them is a ruling, so this pass did not make one.

## What is NOT in doubt

D-329's own defense is a measurement, not a preference: the record states the dominant remaining
error is that the right chord was never listed, and rejects a strong re-ranker over a cheap
partial list on exactly that ground. And the admission rule's cost is measured, not suspected:
zero segments on 13 of 23 large scores.
