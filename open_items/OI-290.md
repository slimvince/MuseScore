## The finding

The section-level home criterion (**D-430**, user-ratified 2026-08-03) admits a section as a home
when a user-ratified surface delegates a stated concern to it by name and that section **states
rules** rather than recording findings. `ARCHITECTURE.md:319` delegates the shipped-parameter
licence-pool constraint to `cowork_score_census.md` **§8c** by name, and §8c does state rules — three
of them, each cited in the criterion block at
`tools/audit/decisions/backbone_decisions.json` → `section_home_criterion.documents`
(`cowork_score_census.md` → `delegated_sections`): the Stage-5 fitting-pool licence constraint, the
intake rule, and the supersession decision protocol.

**But §8c is not homogeneous.** It also carries the needs-vector TABLE, whose rows record findings
about corpora — what exists, what was searched for and not found, what was acquired. The criterion's
unit is the section, so admitting the section admits those rows with it.

**The counts are generated, not transcribed** (D-431): `tools/audit/decisions/phase1p_delegation_bar.json`
→ `census_8c_mixed_section` carries the admitted set, the derived extent of the needs-vector table,
and the split of the admitted entries into rows-of-the-table and not-rows-of-the-table.
`open_items/OI-281.md`'s phase-1n note §4 and the phase-1n `STATUS.md` entry both state the split as
**nine** of the eleven; derived from the file it is what the artifact's
`of_those_rows_of_the_findings_table` holds, which is one fewer — **a correction of record made by
the generator this ruling ordered, not by re-reading the note.** The one entry the earlier figure
over-counted is the section's opening question-and-answer, which sits above the table.

## The disposition — the remedy is at the DOCUMENT, not at the classification

**The eleven entries stand where the ruling put them.** Nothing in this row asks for a
re-classification; the criterion did what it was ruled to do, and the case it was ruled on is exactly
this one.

**What is owed is at `cowork_score_census.md`:** §8c should be SPLIT so that the rules and the
findings are not one block — the three binding rules in their own section (which is what
`ARCHITECTURE.md:319` actually points at), the needs-vector table and its audit narrative in another.
Then the delegation lands on a section that is rules and nothing else, and no future reader has to
decide whether a table row inside a rule-stating block is a decision.

This is a documentation act at the census's next touch, in the shape [[OI-269]] and [[OI-282]] use:
the existing text is preserved and re-sectioned, never rewritten. It is not executed here — the
phase-1p dispatch licenses the register, `CLAUDE.md`, `cowork_audit_protocol.md` and the rows, and
re-sectioning a user-ratified corpus document is neither.

## The narrower reading — considered and NOT adopted, with the ground

D-430 reads *"delegates a **stated concern** to that section"*. That could be read as scoping the
admission to the delegated CONCERN, so that a decision sitting inside a delegated section but on a
different concern would not be admitted. On that reading most of the eleven would fall out again,
since the delegation at `ARCHITECTURE.md:319` names the licence-pool constraint and the table rows
are about corpus coverage.

**That reading is not the one applied, and the ground is precedent rather than preference.** Through
three waves — the delegation-specificity criterion, the document-kind test, and the section-level
ruling — *"for a stated concern"* has been used as a test of DELEGATION QUALITY, distinguishing a
delegation that names its target from a bare mention. It has never been a per-decision scope limit.
`ARCHITECTURE.md:1331` delegates "this layer" and admits all sixteen of that document's entries
whatever sub-concern each covers; applying the narrow reading here would silently change what the
clause has meant since it was written, and would do it for one document.

Recorded, not chosen. If the user wants the narrow reading it is a ruling on the clause, and it
reaches every entry admitted under (g)/(h), not only these.

## What is NOT in doubt

That §8c states three binding rules; that `ARCHITECTURE.md:319` delegates to it by name; that the
eleven entries are correctly homed in it; and the section's content, read in full at phase 1h and
again in this session.
