# OI-283 — The decisions register's coverage claim states a stale `ARCHITECTURE.md` line count, and the claim has no mechanism keeping it current

> STATUS IS AUTHORITATIVE IN THE INDEX (OPEN_ITEMS.md) — this file carries narrative and provenance only and is NEVER the status of record

**Section F — doc-sync debt (#10) / measurement-claim hygiene (#17f, #19). CC-found 2026-08-03 at
phase 1k, on the standing self-check of the phase's own diff.**

## The finding

`DECISIONS.md`'s "What was read, and what was not" section — the register's own statement of what its
enumeration may claim — opens:

> **Read in full.** ARCHITECTURE.md IN FULL (**all 6,523 lines**: the preamble, §1 … and both
> appendices). Lines 1-3981 were read by the 2026-08-01 adjudication; §1 and §6-§19 plus the
> appendices by the 2026-08-01 completion pass.

`ARCHITECTURE.md` is **7,045 lines at HEAD** and **7,089** after the phase-1k §6.7 restatement. The
figure was already 522 lines stale before this session touched anything: the phase-1i and phase-1j
homing acts wrote sixteen decisions into the document's owning sections, and the coverage sentence
was not re-stamped either time.

The sentence lives in the backbone data, not in prose — `tools/audit/decisions/backbone_decisions.json`,
`header.scope.read_in_full` — so it is rendered into the register on every regeneration and reads as
a current, generated figure. **It is not generated.** It is a hand-typed number carried forward,
which is the shape #17(f) exists to forbid: figures enter documents via generated artifacts, and a
hand-transcribed one has no mechanism that can notice it going wrong.

## Why it is worth a row rather than a silent re-stamp

The number is small; what it measures is not. This paragraph is the register's **bounded-trust
statement** — the thing `DEFECT_TYPES.md` DT-26 (scope-assumed enumeration) requires so that a sweep
complete inside its own file set is not read as complete about the whole question. A coverage claim
that drifts silently is the same defect one level up: it says "read in full" about a file that has
since grown, without saying by how much or by whom.

Two further points make the claim narrower than it reads, and both are unstated:

1. **Most of the growth is not unread material.** The lines added since the 2026-08-01 reads were
   written by the homing passes themselves, out of decisions already in the register. So the register
   very likely still covers the document's decision content — but that is an argument, and the
   paragraph does not make it. A reader has no way to tell "grew by 566 lines of already-registered
   text" from "grew by 566 unread lines".
2. **The line ranges cited are stale too.** "Lines 1-3981 were read by the 2026-08-01 adjudication"
   refers to line numbers in a file that has been renumbered twice since.

## What is NOT in doubt

The register's per-entry anchoring, which is mechanically checked every session and passes: every one
of the 414 verbatim quotes is found at its cited home and starts at its cited line
(`gen_cluster_dispositions.py --verify`). This row is about the prose coverage claim only, not about
any entry.

## Disposition

Two acts, both at the register's next touch:

- **Re-state the claim so it cannot silently drift** — say what was read, when, and at which commit,
  rather than at which line count; a claim stamped to a commit is checkable, a claim stamped to a
  line count that nothing recomputes is not (#16, reproducibility, applied to a coverage claim).
- **Or generate it** — have `gen_decisions_register.py` compute the current line count and the delta
  since the stamped read, which is what #17(f) asks for and removes the failure mode entirely.

Not executed at phase 1k: the dispatch licenses the four banner documents, `ARCHITECTURE.md` §6.7 and
the register's DATA; rewording the register's own coverage statement is a change to what the register
claims about itself, which belongs with whoever is judging phase 1's completion — the phase-1
completion statement is the natural place, since that statement rests on exactly this paragraph.
