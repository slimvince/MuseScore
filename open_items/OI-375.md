## The sentence, and what is unqualified in it

The annotation written into principle #21 in the same commit as this row carries, as its consequence
clause:

> **The consequence for this principle: the ceiling is not obtainable from inside any single corpus
> in this repertoire**, which is why OI-179's measurement is a comparison BETWEEN annotation
> traditions.

and bounds itself immediately afterwards:

> *NOT claimed:* that no corpus anywhere could supply it — the claim is about the enumerated set the
> D-474 block names, and a corpus outside that set is a finding rather than a contradiction.

**The load-bearing word is "repertoire", and its argument is not named.** The standing writing
standard is that every two-place word names the thing it points at, and the mechanical check is to
force the word to be followed by that thing; *this repertoire* does not survive it. What makes the
gap consequential rather than stylistic is that **the record already uses the phrase at two different
widths**, and the sentence is true under one and false under the other.

## The two widths, each read at its own source

**The WIDE width — the one this same principle uses nine lines above.** D-474's block opens *"no
published study reports per-axis inter-annotator agreement for Roman-numeral or key annotation of
**Baroque/classical symbolic music**"*, and closes by saying of the rock and pop-audio bounds that
they *"are **not** a ceiling for **this repertoire**"*. So within principle #21's own text, *this
repertoire* is Baroque/classical symbolic music, contrasted with rock and with pop audio — a width
that **includes** the classical variation repertoire.

**The NARROW width — the one the commissioned design surface uses.**
`cowork_scratch_2026_08_08/draft_oi179_ceiling_measurement_surface_2026_08_08.md` uses the phrase
both ways in one document: at its "off-domain bounds" line it repeats the principle's wide usage
(*"the numbers themselves may not be used as a ceiling for this repertoire"*, of rock and pop), and
at its optional-legs line it calls TAVERN *"**off-repertoire**"* — a width that **excludes** the
classical variations and means, in effect, the chorale repertoire the gate corpus is.

## Why the width decides the sentence's truth, at the objects

**Two members of the enumerated set the sentence claims to be about hold within-corpus dual
annotations, and the record says so in its own words.**

- **TAVERN.** D-474, at `CLAUDE.md` #21: *"TAVERN released duplicate annotations but published no
  such number"*. The source it was drawn from, `cowork_term_theory_grounding.md` §2: *"TAVERN has
  duplicate annotations but published no number"*. And the design surface, naming what those
  duplicates ARE: *"TAVERN's duplicates (**within-corpus, true inter-annotator**, off-repertoire)"*.
- **Dilemmadata (2026).** D-474: *"identifies dual-annotated pieces and computes nothing"*. The
  design surface: *"both are **computable BY US** as additional legs, each with a declared domain
  caveat."*

**So under the WIDE width the sentence is refuted by its own enumerated set**: the ceiling IS
obtainable from inside at least one single corpus in Baroque/classical symbolic music — nobody has
published the figure, which is D-474, but the material is there and the record says we can compute
it. **Under the NARROW width the sentence stands**, because no held chorale collection can now supply
it: BCMH was the only in-repo candidate and the 2026-08-11 laboratory reply establishes it carries a
single reading with no independent second annotation.

**The two claims are different claims, and only the narrow one follows from the reply.** What the
reply establishes is about BCMH. The wide sentence is a statement about five external corpora, and
the reply is silent about all of them.

## The ruling, and why it is neither (a) nor (b)

The two closing acts this row named were **name the argument** and **narrow the sentence to what the
reply establishes**. The user ruled on a surface carrying three alternatives with their principled
costs and their ratings against the ultimate objective, and the ruling is **KEEP THE WIDTH AND
RESTATE WHAT IS ACTUALLY ABSENT** — the reading this row did not reach.

**Both of this row's acts were declined, each with its reason recorded** (an excluded alternative is
evidence about the choice). **Act (b), narrowing** — declined because it would place a narrow reading
of *this repertoire* below the same phrase used at a wide width in the same principle, which is the
ambiguity that produced the defect in the first place. **Striking the consequence sentence** — declined because a
reader of #21 would then still infer the ceiling might come from citation, which is what **D-474**
exists to prevent.

**What the ruled text does instead**, and it is worth stating because it is what this row's own
analysis missed: the two widths do not need to be chosen between, because **what is absent and what
is computable are different quantities**. What is absent is a **published** per-axis value, and that
absence is D-474 and is unchanged. What is not absent is **computability**. The sentence can
therefore be true at the wide width and say so: the ceiling is not obtainable from inside any corpus
of **our gate repertoire**, while within the wider enumerated set it is computable by us,
off-repertoire or of unchecked domain.
