## Why it is a genuine conflict and not merely stale

The constraint is recorded as **user-ratified** and **BINDING**, with a date. Nothing this pass read
amends it, scopes it, or withdraws it. The later fitting protocols that WERE ratified — the held-out
evaluation protocol (**D-270**) and the capacity budget (**D-271**), both ratified 2026-07-19 — settle
*how* the fit is evaluated and *how large* it may be; neither says anything about which corpora a
shipped value may be fitted on. So the constraint and the built system are both on the record and
they do not agree.

Three readings are possible and the record does not say which holds:

1. the constraint still binds and the current fitted values are not shippable as they stand;
2. it was superseded in fact by the 2026-07-17 architecture decision and the 2026-07-19 pre-fit
   gates, and the supersession was never written down;
3. it was scoped to something narrower than it reads — for instance to a *distributed data file*
   rather than to a fitted number — and the scoping was never written down.

Deciding between them is a ruling, so this pass did not make one.

## What is not in doubt

The product is unshipped, so nothing is being distributed today under a licence it may not carry.
That is what makes this a question that can be settled deliberately rather than urgently. It bears
on **D-118** (GPL v3, and every external library must be GPL-v3 compatible) and on **D-117** (the
long-term intent to contribute the work officially), because both concern what may leave this
repository.

**What the fit actually reads, traced end to end.** `tools/joint_estimator/gen_label_tables.py`
sets `WIR_DIR = tools/dcml/when_in_rome`, calls `dcml_parser.load_wir_regions(WIR_DIR, stem)` for
every stem, and stamps `ground_truth_substrate: "dcml_parser.load_wir_regions (OI-142-corrected)"`
into the artifact it writes. That loader resolves each stem to
`Corpus/Early_Choral/Bach,_Johann_Sebastian/Chorales/<NNN>/analysis.txt` by reading the
per-folder `remote.json`. So the fitted tables' ground truth is the *When in Rome* chorale
analyses, one file per chorale.

**Four things are now established.**

1. **The source is not a DCML-lab corpus, so the NC class the constraint names does not reach
   it.** The DCML corpora sit in sibling directories under `tools/dcml/` — ABC, corelli,
   mozart_piano_sonatas, chopin_mazurkas and nine more — and each carries its own
   `LICENSE` file, Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0. `tools/dcml/when_in_rome` is a
   separate clone of MarkGotham/When-in-Rome and carries no licence file at all. The row's
   premise that the chorale ground truth is "the DCML annotations" is a naming artifact of the
   record calling the annotation *format* "DCML"; the material is not from that lab.
2. **Nothing under `tools/dcml/` is committed to this repository.** `.gitignore:24` ignores the
   whole tree and `git ls-files tools/dcml/when_in_rome` returns zero. There are therefore no
   "committed annotation files" — the fork distributes none of this material today, which
   sharpens the row's own "the product is unshipped" observation into a stronger fact about what
   is actually in the repository.
3. **When-in-Rome's own licence statement does not cover this subcorpus by its blanket grant.**
   The README grants CC BY-SA 4.0 to "New content in this repository, including the new analyses,
   code, and the conversion (specifically) of existing analyses", and for "analyses that
   originated elsewhere and have been converted into the format used here" it defers to the
   original source. It then lists the Bach chorales under **"Corpora originating elsewhere"**, as
   RomanText analyses by Dmitri Tymoczko and colleagues that needed no conversion — a
   subcorpus that is neither new content nor a conversion, and so falls in neither branch of the
   grant as written.
4. **No chorale analysis file names a licence or a source.** Every one carries `Analyst:` and
   `Proofreader:` headers and nothing else of that kind; a scan of all 371 finds **zero** with a
   `Source:` header, against the README's own claim that links to the original sources appear
   "within every `analysis.txt` file". The per-folder `remote.json` records `analysis_source` as
   a bare relative path, not a licensed origin. The **scores** are not in doubt: `remote.json`
   points at the music21 public-domain chorale edition.

**Verdict, and why no flip.** The dispatch's condition for flipping this row was unambiguous
evidence. The evidence is unambiguous about what the class is *not* — not NC, not a DCML corpus —
and genuinely silent about what it *is*. Recording "CC-BY-SA" would mean reading the repository's
blanket grant over a subcorpus its own README places outside it, which is a guess. So the
established facts are recorded at **D-292**'s provenance and this row stays open on the narrowed
question: *under what licence did the Tymoczko-origin chorale analyses reach When in Rome?* That
is answerable by asking the maintainers or by finding the TAOM supplementary's own terms; it is
not answerable from anything on this disk. **No refit decision follows either way today** — the
ruling's unconditional half (no NC-derived parameter set may ever ship) is unaffected, and
[[OI-38]] already carries the conditional obligation for the ship-intended fit.
