## The framework's sentence (FRAMEWORK.md §5, L1, "Why metric strength earns its place")

> "It is measured to constrain where harmonies change — harmonic change was counted at 71.5% of
> tactus beats against 2.4% of the lowest metrical level — and removing metrical-accent
> features from a segmental analyser costs about six points of F-measure. [FACT — both.]"

(The second half — the six-point ablation — is VERIFIED exactly: Masada & Bunescu 2019,
Table 5, F-measure 77.6 → 71.2 without accent features. This STOP concerns the first half
only.)

## What the primary says (read at the object)

Temperley 2009, "A Unified Probabilistic Model for Polyphonic Music Analysis" (JNMR 38(1)),
**Table 1, journal p. 6** — "Harmonic changes at beats of different metrical levels in the
Kostka–Payne corpus", with the caption's own gloss "Metrical level (2 = tactus)":

| Metrical level | % of beats with changes of harmony |
|---|---|
| 3 (highest shown) | **71.5** |
| 2 (**the tactus**) | 22.3 |
| 1 | **2.4** |

And the text (journal p. 7): "harmonic changes are allowed only on tactus beats. (As shown in
Table 1, it appears that only a very small percentage of harmonic changes are on sub-tactus
beats…)"; "the probability of change is higher for L3 beats than L2 beats, reflecting the
greater likelihood of chord changes on stronger beats."

## The divergence, stated precisely

Both NUMBERS exist at the primary, but the framework's sentence attaches 71.5% to **tactus
beats** where the primary attaches it to **level-3 beats** (the level ABOVE the tactus; the
tactus row reads 22.3%), and calls level 1 "the lowest metrical level" where the primary's
model carries a level 0 below it (Table 1 simply does not list levels 0; the text's claim
about sub-tactus changes being rare is qualitative). So as stated, the framework's clause is
false at the primary: harmonic change was counted at 71.5% of the STRONGEST level's beats,
22.3% of tactus beats, 2.4% of level-1 beats.

## What it does and does not touch

- **The design point's direction is untouched and, if anything, sharpened:** the primary's own
  gradient (71.5 → 22.3 → 2.4 by descending level) is exactly the "metric strength constrains
  where harmonies change" ground L1's charter rests on, and Temperley's model even hard-limits
  changes to tactus beats on this evidence. No rival gains anything from the correction.
- **What is owed:** the framework's sentence, being ratified text, is not edited by this pass;
  the correction is the user's to order through the ruled amendment route (or to fold into the
  findings surface's verdict for L1). The corrected wording would be: "harmonic change was
  counted at 71.5% of the strongest-level beats against 22.3% of tactus beats and 2.4% of the
  level below" (Temperley 2009, Table 1, Kostka–Payne corpus).
