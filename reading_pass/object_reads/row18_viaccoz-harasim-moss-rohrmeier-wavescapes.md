Carries **DP-F/DP-G/DP-H's SUPPORTS** entry, where the surface says the row *"throws away precisely
the information V3 measures the value of."*

**p. 394:** *"A pitch class is the equivalence class of all octave-related pitches in 12-tone equal
temperament (C, C♯, D, D♯, E, F, F♯, G, G♯, A, B♭, B), **assuming enharmonic equivalence**."*

**p. 392:** *"the method proposed in this article does not rely on the concept of musical keys but
only on the representation of tones as pitch classes"* (p. 393). **No key-finding algorithm appears

## ★ K1 — DP-O's quotation is attributed to the wrong method — **CORRECTED**

**The claim as the findings surface carries it** (§2, DP-O, verdict **NO BEARING**): *"Rows 17 and 18
[RELAYED] are visualisations; **row 18 states in its own limitations that the method does "not
explicitly provide a tree- or graph-structured analysis of the music."**"* The row-18 cross-check
carries the same, listing it among *"the stated limitation that bears on DP-O"*, together with *"a
first approximation"* and *"a first building block"*.

**At the object the sentence exists — and its subject is the KEYSCAPE, not the wavescape. p. 392**,
in the section *"Visual hierarchical music analysis"*, which reviews Sapp's keyscapes and discusses
Figure 1, a keyscape of Bach's Prelude: *"The example demonstrates how keyscapes can be used to
visualize the hierarchical organization of keys in a composition. **Although this visualization does
not explicitly provide a tree- or graph-structured analysis of the music**, it is a useful tool for
music analysis that is easy to understand for a broad audience."*

The same is true of the second phrase: **p. 391**, *"**Keyscapes** … can be viewed as **a first
approximation** for the hierarchical organization of the tonal material"*. Only the third belongs to
this method — **p. 394**, of the synthesis of keyscapes and the DFT: *"a first building block in the
deeper understanding of hierarchical relations in music."*

**The wavescape method's own limitations are stated, and they are different ones. pp. 413–414:** the
vertical partitioning *"fragments horizontal structures such as the chains of fifths in Ligeti's
étude and the 12-tone rows in Webern's variation"*; *"More insightful analyses thus require
additional tools such as set theory or voice-leading analysis"*; the method *"might not provide many
insights into atonal works or musical styles that are based on musical properties other than pitch"*;
and the enharmonic assumption at C1. **None of them is the sentence DP-O cites.**

**What moves and what does not.** DP-O's verdict is **NO BEARING**, and it survives — this method
produces no tree, measures nothing, and its authors say analyses require further tools. **What is
wrong is the evidence cited for it**: a sentence about somebody else's method, offered as this
paper's self-assessment. **By §2's own words this is not a STOP** — NO BEARING is neither SUPPORTS
nor a rival-defusal — but it is a quotation that would not survive being checked by a reader who went
to p. 392.

## ★ K3 — The cross-check's headline finding for this row is itself substantially wrong — **CORRECTED**

**What the cross-check says** (§2, headed *"THE ONE SUBSTANTIVE ERROR FOUND — and it is exactly what a
cross-check is for"*): the first extract's gloss *"hexatonic 0.652 vs augmented-triad 0.172"* is
mislabelled, because *"0.652 is coefficient k = 3 and 0.172 is coefficient k = 6, and the paper
associates k = 3 with HEXATONIC scales and k = 6 with WHOLE-TONE scales"*; and *"Mis-labelled, the
pair reads as hexatonic against augmented triad, **which is a comparison WITHIN one coefficient and
therefore one the wavescape does not make at all**."*

**At the page, hexatonic-against-augmented-triad is exactly the comparison the paper makes, and it
makes it between those two coefficients. p. 403:** *"**Augmented triads are prototypes for the third
and sixth coefficient** … **If the augmented triad were the most significant harmonic structure, one
would expect maximal magnitudes for both the third and sixth coefficients** (Figures 9a and 9b). A
comparison of the average normalized magnitudes of these coefficients (ᾱ₃ = .652 and ᾱ₆ = .172)
reveals that the third coefficient is more relevant … **This color represents the hexatonic scale
H₀,₁ … which supports the hexatonic analysis.**"*

**And the cross-check's supporting premise is false at the paper.** It says the augmented triad is
*"the other family sharing coefficient 3, **not** the family of coefficient 6"*. **p. 398:**
*"augmented triads [maximize the normalized magnitudes] for k = 3,6"*, and Figure 6 (p. 399) prints
the augmented triad's normalised magnitude as **1 at k = 3 and 1 at k = 6**. **The augmented triad is
a prototype of both coefficients** — which is precisely why the paper can test the augmented-triad
reading by comparing them.

**So the first extract's gloss was loose in its wording and right in its analytical content**, and the
correction that overruled it replaced it with a claim — that the wavescape does not make this
comparison — **that the paper contradicts in terms.** The coefficient identifications in the
cross-check (0.652 = k3, 0.172 = k6) are correct and stand.

**Nothing in the findings surface rests on this**; the mislabel and its correction live in the
cross-check and in the eighty-fourth handoff entry. **This is the fourth item in this pass where a
doubled reading settled a question the wrong way** — after row 2's boundaries, row 5's caveat that
never reached the surface, and row 17's downgrade to conjecture.
