## What the paper says, read at the object

**Root, quality and inversion are indeed never revised.** That half is confirmed. **The segmentation
is another matter, at four places that agree with each other:**

1. **p. 5, §2.3 (the method):** *"Any remaining unmerged vectors after this process are treated as
   **separate chord labels** … as they necessarily contain incompatible PCs, and would therefore be
   represented by the same (duplicated) chord label, but with distinct alterations."*
2. **p. 6, §3.1.3:** consecutive chords sharing root, quality and inversion are merged **on input**;
   *"the ground truth targets remain unchanged: **the goal is therefore that our method will leave
   such chords unmerged in its output**."*
3. **p. 6, §3.1.4:** the evaluation cuts windows wherever *"the model's output label changes"*,
   *"includ[ing] … chords which differ only by, e.g., an added tone."*
4. **p. 10:** two of the paper's own reported errors are boundary errors — *"our method … incorrectly
   merges the two chords together"*.

**So the method subdivides the span it is given, deliberately, and its metric and its error analysis
are built around that.** No input boundary is deleted or relocated; boundaries are **added**.

## Why this is a falsifier candidate rather than a detail

**In this framework's own terms, adding a boundary inside a span L2 published is a revision of L2's
segmentation** — which **§5's L3 → L2 "Nothing"** contract forbids and **DP-M** narrows. The coupling
table's *"consumes L2's output and revises none of it"* was the ground for placing row 2 at L3, and
it does not hold at the paper.

**The honest statement of which way it cuts, with both directions given rather than one:**

- **It may STRENGTHEN DP-D's chosen position.** If the best-published post-hoc rival cannot in fact
  sit downstream without revising the segmentation it was handed, then the fifth shape the surface
  added to DP-D's candidate list — *"derived after the chord decision, as an augmentation of L2's
  published output … an L3 read-off"* — is not a shape this paper exhibits. The rival would be
  **less** available as an alternative to deciding assignment inside L2, not more.
- **It may WEAKEN the defusal as written.** The defusal's first leg is that the rival is a decorator
  that revises nothing. If it is instead a component that re-segments, then the comparison DP-D
  needs is no longer *"joint versus a harmless read-off"* but *"joint versus a downstream stage that
  re-cuts the spans"* — which is a different argument, closer to §8.4's territory and to row 5's, and
  the surface does not make it.
- **A third reading is available and is not dismissed:** that the subdivision is an artefact of the
  paper's experimental setup rather than of the method. §3.1.3 is a setup section. **Against that
  reading:** §2.3 is the method section, and the emitted-separate-labels behaviour is stated there,
  independently of the experiment.

**Nothing here touches the second leg of the defusal** — that the paper's stated ground is a
vocabulary problem and not an argument against joint assignment. That leg was re-read at the object
and survives, with one correction of its own (K2 in the row file: the paper does discuss alternatives
and does argue for its shape, but never measures post-hoc against joint).
