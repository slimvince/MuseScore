## §2 — The method, as the paper states it

*(Each subsection names the paper's own section and the PDF pages it sits on, so a later reader can check
any sentence at the page rather than at this file.)*

### §2.1 The lineage the paper places itself in (its §2, pages 2–4)

- **Harmonic analysis (its §2.1).** *"Early systems employed rule-based grammars, probabilistic models,
  dynamic programming, and grammar induction for harmonic and metrical analysis of music [19, 22, 23]."*
  Then: Micchi et al. [15] *"trained one of the first neural networks (a convolutional recurrent model) for
  Roman numeral analysis"*; Nápoles López et al. [18] *"later introduced AugmentedNet, a CNN-LSTM
  architecture that enhanced performance via data augmentation and multi-task learning"*; two further
  multitask systems [14, 16] used *"techniques to mitigate the interdependency between the Roman numeral
  prediction subtasks"*.
- **Why graphs, in the paper's own reasoning.** *"Despite these advances, purely sequential models struggle
  with the inherent complexity of polyphonic scores—they require the score to be serialized or binned into
  time slices, which risks losing voice-leading details and long-range dependencies."*
- **ChordGNN [9]** *"constructs a graph of all notes in a piece and applies graph convolution to aggregate
  note features into chord predictions"*, treats Roman numeral analysis as multi-task classification, and
  *"introduces an edge contraction algorithm to pool information from note-level to chord-level
  representations"*. The paper states it *"achieved high performance on Roman numeral analysis,
  outperforming previous CNN/CRNN models on standard datasets"*.
- **The stated current state of the art.** *"The most recent approach by Sailor [20] has used a transformer
  model and BERT-like pretraining to build a more robust encoder, which in turn resulted in the current
  state-of-the-art performance."* **[20] is RNBert** — the paper that is row 50 of this same slice, and the
  member the previous sitting carried.
- **Cadence detection and phrase segmentation (its §2.2).** Traditional methods used hand-crafted features
  and classifiers, *"such as SVMs trained on features representing intervallic sequences that form full (or
  authentic) or half cadences [2]"*; Karystinaios and Widmer [8] *"reformulated cadence detection as a node
  classification task on a note-level graph representation of the score"*, a GNN predicting *"cadence
  likelihood for each note (or group of notes), capturing non-local context through message-passing"*.
  Derivative work [10] refined it further, *"although the lack of a unified benchmark prevents a direct
  comparison"*.
- **GNNs in MIR generally (its §2.3).** Recommendation, emotion modelling, generation, performance
  modelling; Jeong et al. [7] combined a GNN with a hierarchical RNN for expressive piano performance;
  graph-based representations in generation [3, 12].
- **Unified multi-task learning (its §2.4).** *"By interleaving training examples from heterogeneous
  datasets, rather than processing tasks sequentially, unified MTL avoids the catastrophic forgetting
  endemic to task-incremental pipelines and mitigates domain shifts between specialized corpora."* Three
  named common solutions to conflicting gradients and annotation schemes: *"i) tailor optimization by
  assigning task-specific weights to losses or gradients, either heuristically or via uncertainty
  estimation … [11, 13]; ii) dynamic task sampling by shuffling mini-batches across tasks according to
  dataset size, task difficulty, or performance criteria … [5], iii) logit-level fusion by combining the
  raw outputs of task-specific classifiers to reinforce shared features and encourage co-adaptation between
  tasks [17]."* **The paper's own framework is then positioned as all three at once plus NCT filtering:**
  *"our framework extends this paradigm with data-shuffling, custom weighting, logit fusion, and NCT
  filtering to achieve robust, unified score analysis."*

### §2.2 The architecture (its §3.1, pages 4–5)

- **The input.** *"Input to our model is music scores represented as graphs, where vertices are notes and
  edges represent temporal relations between those notes, similar to [7, 8]."*
- **The backbone.** *"The backbone of our model is a Hybrid Graph Neural Network similar to the one
  introduced in [10]."* Concretely: *"the score graph enters a series of Heterogeneous Graph Convolutional
  blocks. In parallel, the note features are segmented per piece and padded where necessary and then they
  are passed through a sequential model such as a GRU with the same number of layers as the number of graph
  convolutions. The common representation is then used by a series of 2-layer MLP classifiers. Finally, a
  logit-fusion layer is added where the logit prediction of each task communicate with each other."*
- **What the heterogeneous encoding contains.** *"the GCN integrates information at multiple levels—notes,
  beats, and measures—thereby functioning as a heterogeneous graph neural network. For note-type nodes, the
  input features are distilled to essential attributes such as pitch spelling and duration. In contrast,
  the features for beats and measures are computed as the mean values of the features from their associated
  notes."*
- **★ THE SENTENCE THAT FIXES THE OUTPUT GRANULARITY, AND IT IS LOAD-BEARING FOR EVERY TABLE BELOW:**
  *"Despite this layered encoding, all predictions are ultimately made at the note level."*

### §2.3 The weighted multi-task loss (its §3.2, page 5)

- **The training regime.** *"Training AnalysisGNN proceeds by interleaving examples from all tasks via a
  data-shuffling strategy, combined with a custom weighted multi-task loss and logit fusion. At each update
  step, mini-batches are sampled across the current set of tasks 𝒯, ensuring that no single task dominates
  the optimization."*
- **Equation (1), transcribed in words and symbols as printed:**
  `L_clf = (1/|𝒯|) Σ_{t∈𝒯} ( L_t / (2 σ_t²) + log(1 + σ_t²) )`
  with the paper's own gloss: *"where ℒ_t is the cross-entropy for task t and each σ_t is a learnable scale
  controlling the task's weight."* The paper states it is *"a dynamically weighted cross-entropy loss
  similar to [11], but normalized by the number of tasks"* — **[11] is Liebel & Körner, *Auxiliary tasks in
  multi-task learning*, arXiv:1805.06334 (2018)**, at the paper's own reference list.
- **The claim made for the form, which carries no measurement:** *"This formulation both balances gradients
  from heterogeneous annotation schemas and regularizes the learned weights to prevent any σ_t from
  collapsing to zero."*

### §2.4 The logit-fusion mechanism (its §3.2, pages 5–6)

Transcribed in the paper's own order, because the mechanism is where a prose summary can go wrong:

1. *"for each task t in the current set 𝒯:"* `z_t = Clf_t(h)` *(raw logits)*; `p_t = Proj_t(z_t)`
   *(projection into a common d-dimensional space)*.
2. *"We then stack all projections into a matrix"* `P = [p_1; p_2; … ; p_|𝒯|] ∈ ℝ^{|𝒯|×d}`.
3. *"and refine them via a transformer-style self-attention layer:"*
   `P̃ = LayerNorm( P + MultiHeadAttn(P, P, P) )`, *"where MultiHeadAttn denotes standard multi-head
   attention and the LayerNorm adds a residual connection."*
4. *"Finally, each task's refined logits are obtained by selecting its row from P̃ and passing it through
   the corresponding fusion head:"* `ẑ_t = Fusion_t(P̃_t)`, *"where P̃_t is the t-th row of P̃. We then
   compute the cross-entropy loss on ẑ_t for each task t."*

**The paper's own statement of what this buys:** *"This attention-based fusion allows each task head to
attend to and integrate information from all other tasks by sharing confidence and contextual cues,
leading to more coherent multi-task representations."*

**★ THE ATTENTION IS OVER TASKS, NOT OVER NOTES OR TIME.** The matrix stacked is one row per task in the
current set, so the self-attention runs across the task dimension. This is stated because it is exactly the
kind of mechanism a summary can turn into its opposite.

**★ AND THE LOSS IS TAKEN ON THE FUSED LOGITS, WHICH PUTS THE FUSION INSIDE THE TRAINING OBJECTIVE AND NOT
AFTER IT.** *(★ ADOPTED FROM THE FIRST EXTRACT AT THE CROSS-CHECK AND RE-DERIVED HERE AT THIS FILE'S OWN
TRANSCRIPTION, which is the standing rule for an adoption.)* Step 4 above ends *"We then compute the
cross-entropy loss on ẑ_t for each task t"* — **ẑ_t being the refined logits, not the raw ones** — so the
heads are trained to agree rather than reconciled afterwards. **This read transcribed that sentence and did
not draw the consequence; the first extract drew it.** The consequence follows from the sentence as
transcribed above and needs nothing further.

### §2.5 The Non-Chord-Tone branch, and the asymmetry between training and inference (its §3.2, page 6)

- **What it is:** *"we introduce a Non-Chord-Tone (NCT) prediction branch. This auxiliary head labels each
  note as functional or non-function in regards to the underlying harmony and structure, with its own
  cross-entropy loss ℒ_NCT."* — *"non-function"* and the missing space after *"ℒ_NCT."* are the paper's own
  and are recorded at §7.1.
- **★ AT TRAINING TIME THE NCT PREDICTION DOES NOT MASK ANYTHING:** *"During training, we predict NCT
  labels but do not mask out non-chord-tones in the multi-task losses, since this preserves passing-note
  examples in the gradient signal and prevents the model from collapsing (by masking everything) or
  misclassifying functional notes."*
- **★ AT INFERENCE TIME IT GATES:** *"At inference time, however, we can leverage the NCT predictions as a
  gating mechanism by classifying each note as chord-tone or non-chord-tone, and pass only those identified
  as chord-tones to the task-specific heads. This selective inference reduces computational overhead and
  mitigates error propagation by focusing predictions on musically functional notes."*
- **THE BOUND THIS READ ATTACHES, AND IT IS A BOUND AND NOT A FINDING:** the word *"can"* is the paper's
  own, and **the fourteen pages as read do not state whether the reported results were produced with that

### §2.7 Graph construction, augmentation, splits and masking (its §4.2, pages 7–8)

- **Construction:** *"the tabular data (described in the previous section) is first converted into graph
  representations. In these graphs, each node corresponds to a musical note, and relevant analytical labels
  are propagated to the nodes using the GraphMuse Python package [10]."*
- **Augmentation:** *"Furthermore, we apply transposition augmentation to our training data while
  preserving pitch spelling sensitivity, resulting in an approximate tenfold increase in available
  scores."*
- **The training exposure:** *"During training, the model is exposed to the entire collection of datasets
  for a fixed number of iterations, with every batch containing a combination of all three datasets and all
  tasks."*
- **The splits:** *"When it comes to validation and testing, we assign one test and validation split per
  dataset. Accordingly, each dataset is divided into training, validation, and test sets, with the test set
  covering roughly 20% of the total data. The AugmentedNet dataset provided predefined data splits for
  training, validation, and testing, while for other two datasets we perform a random split."*
- **The masking, which is how heterogeneous annotation is handled at all:** *"we frame each score graph as
  a semi-supervised node classification problem, masking out any invalid or missing labels at the note
  level. For example, in the DLC dataset, some pieces lack cadence annotations and certain notes may have
  missing or unencodable harmony labels. By masking these problematic samples during training, we prevent
  invalid data points from influencing the model while still leveraging all available notes for predicting
  the valid labels."*

- **The scope statement:** *"Although our model makes predictions at the note level—assigning a label for

- **The structural tasks named:** *"detecting the presence and type of cadences, identifying phrase and

  annotations are heterogeneous: chords, phrases, cadences, sections, pedal points, and Roman numerals from

- **★ AND NOT A PRETRAINED ENCODER.** *(★ ADOPTED FROM THE FIRST EXTRACT AT THE CROSS-CHECK AND RE-DERIVED

- **Three new note-level boolean properties:** whether a note is the bass, whether it is the root, whether

- **A public implementation:** `github.com/manoskary/analysisgnn`, as printed in the abstract.
- **★ AND THREE THINGS IT HANDS DOWNSTREAM NOWHERE, WHICH MATTER TO A READER LOOKING FOR THEM.**
