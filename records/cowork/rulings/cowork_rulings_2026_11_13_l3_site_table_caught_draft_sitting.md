## Ruling 1 — a caught draft sentence does not count: B

**The alternatives put**, as a surface in one turn and the choice question alone in a later turn, plain text, no widget.
A: a draft sentence caught and corrected before the landing counts as a degradation tell and forces a handover, as
entries 827 to 832 applied the rule [827 to 830 relayed from entry 832]; the most cautious reading; it has given one
ruling per sitting, against the aim of the user's ruling of 2026-11-13, with fifteen rulings left (P2-R23 to P2-R37).
B: it does not count; a handover is forced only by a defect found after a landing, by a tell of another kind, or by
capacity; every sitting still runs the sweep before each landing and reports what it caught; with the guard that a
rising count of caught sentences from one landing to the next within a session forces a handover. Against B: a session
runs longer on first drafts that come out too wide, leaning on the sweep to catch each one. The writing side argued for
B: the caught sentences are the kind the sweep exists for, and entry 832 §2 item 4 records the same kind under two
models.

**The user's word: "B".**

**As ruled.**

1. A draft sentence that the sweep before a landing caught and corrected, so that it is not in the landed file, is not
   a degradation tell that forces a handover under the rule of 2026-11-10.
2. A handover is forced by: a defect found in a landed file after its landing; a degradation tell of another kind (the
   user's standing list of 2026-08-15 [relayed: not read at its ruling record by this sitting]); or capacity, judged out
   loud after each landing.
3. Every sitting still runs the sweep before each landing, and reports to the user and in its handoff entry what the
   sweep caught.
4. The guard: if the number of draft sentences a sweep catches rises from one landing to the next within a session, the
   session hands over.
5. Written into the plan of handoff entry 833, by this sitting, and of every later entry while the sweep runs.
