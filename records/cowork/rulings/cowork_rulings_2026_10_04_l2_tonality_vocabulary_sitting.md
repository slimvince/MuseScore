## 2. The rulings

### Ruling 1 — the mode vocabulary (question 1; OQ-L2-2 answered)

**The user's words, verbatim: "I agree with you recommendations."** — given upon the re-cut surface whose
recommendation on question 1 was position one.

**The ruling.** L2's tonality vocabulary in the first L2 specification is **two modes, major and composite
minor** (the composite minor practice: natural, harmonic and melodic as one tonality with variable sixth and
seventh degrees, as D-524 states it). Modal evidence reaches the user through D-276's counted, un-rounded
per-degree inflections, never through an inferred mode label. **The modal extension is written INTO that first
specification as a named increment, not left to a later idea:** the modes in order — Dorian and Mixolydian first;
Phrygian and Lydian as the next pair; Aeolian is minor's natural variant and needs no state; Locrian is out on
musical grounds (no stable tonic triad) — and what each needs before it is admitted as a tonality: a theory-built
pitch profile and chord-expectation table whose source is read on our side (#1; D-531 is the precedent for a
confirmed hand-built table; Levine's *Jazz Theory Book*, reached through the Modal Harmony Ontology, is a
candidate source, not yet read); a premise-ledger entry with the near-neighbour flip's false-negative path
enumerated (#17e — Mixolydian against major, Dorian against minor, each one degree apart; Row 23.269 records the
legacy decoder declaring "Mixolydian briefly" on an isolated dominant seventh; OI-174 is the same shape one scale
further out); a desk simulation on real cases (#17c); a grading convention for church modes ruled in measurement
design — the DCML standard's own same-tonic reduction (Dorian and Phrygian to minor, Lydian and Mixolydian to
major), which is NOT D-210's parent-collection rule and needs its own ruling; and held-out ground truth outside
the DCML standard for the evaluation (#20), which the jazz corpus of D-500 is meant to supply. **The trigger is
the premise-ledger entry:** the increment may land as a declared-bound increment (#24, second paragraph) before
modal ground truth exists, and is measured when it does.

**OQ-L2-2 is answered by this ruling**: L2 does not admit Dorian, Phrygian or Mixolydian as tonalities in the
first specification; it reads modal passages as major or minor with the inflections counted and published; the
church modes enter by the named increment under the #17 gate. **L2-S6 stands as derived**, its status line
amended from "Open for modes (OQ-L2-2)" to settled for the first specification with the increment named, and its
falsifier's clause "before OQ-L2-2 is ruled" replaced by "before the named increment's gate is passed". OQ-L2-1
(the closure measurement, double-accidental tonics) stays open as the derivation leaves it, and reports any
annotator-written modal tonality in the grading repertoire as an exclusion with its cost, which L2-S6's own
premise already provides for.

**The ground, as put and as agreed — stated precisely because it is NOT the ground the first writing of the
surface gave.** It is not that ground truth is missing. The user's stated principle, recorded with this ruling
in his words: *jazz is a common use case the final product supports; the question on modes is when and not if;
and lack of ground truth, where good methods exist for recognizing a mode, is not an acceptable ground for
leaving it out — only the absence of any method would be.* This side's reading, tagged as a reading: a method
exists in the sense that matters — a mode is a pitch profile plus chord expectations, and the joint decode is the
same machinery as Feisthauer's 42-tonality finder and the tree's 21-mode legacy decoder with different tables; what
is missing is the tables (uncountable from the ground truth held, because that ground truth writes Dorian as
minor and Mixolydian as major) and the gate. **So the modes are sequenced behind the premise gate the user
ratified for every inference-affecting design (#17), not excluded for lack of ground truth.** Towards the
objective: no modal state is built on an unexamined flip hazard; the measurable repertoire loses nothing; the
modal evidence is published now. Towards the principles: #17 and #8 (the gate before the build), #19/#20
(nothing trusted unfitted or self-graded), #12 (the counts carry the information), #6 (one state space,
extended rather than forked), #14 (the increment is its own ratified, revertible change). Meta level: the
analysis's own specification.

**Declined: position two** — Dorian and Mixolydian admitted as tonalities in the first specification now, with
theory-built tables and a declared precision bound. Towards the objective: a Mixolydian passage read in its own
mode from the first ratified text, with its own chord degrees (I7, ♭VII) rather than borrowed-chord readings —
the real gain, and the one that matters for jazz. Against it: the tables and the flip hazard enter the derived
specification before any premise ledger or desk simulation exists, the order #17 forbids ("no prediction, no
build"); the state's effect on the measurable repertoire is unknown and unmeasurable until modal ground truth
arrives; and the deriving session did not derive these states — they would be the user's addition to a derived
text, lawful at ratification but outside the derivation's defence. **Declined: position three** — the whole
seven-mode vocabulary now: everything said of position two, plus Phrygian, Lydian and Locrian under-researched by
the field's own account and OI-174's measured failure one family further out.

**The external evidence, met on the merits as Ruling 4 required:** the Irish pair is melodic, tonic-given and
per-tune, and states the field's own gap; the DCML reference pins the reduction the grading side must use;
Eerola & Schutz support a graded mode axis at the evidence level, which is D-276's shape; the Modal Harmony
Ontology is a vocabulary and reasoning scheme, not a detector, and is the candidate theory source for the
increment's tables. None of it establishes a note-to-mode method for polyphonic harmonic music; none of it moves
D-524's state; the `e.phrygian` instance is L2-S6's named false-negative path and is what OQ-L2-1 counts.

### Ruling 2 — the tonic is spelled (question 2)

**The user's words, verbatim: the same, "I agree with you recommendations."** — the surface's recommendation on
question 2 was position one. **Voidable on the user's word alone, since one word covered two decisions.**

**The ruling.** L2's tonic is **spelled**: seven letters, each natural, sharp or flat — the 42 tonalities of
L2-S6 with the two modes. L2-S1(ii) and L2-S6 stand as derived on this point.

**The ground.** Towards the objective: L2's chord field is a degree WITH alteration (♭II, ♯IV) read against the
span's tonality, and the chord-identity question L2 owns includes telling a German sixth from a dominant seventh;
both are spelling facts relative to a spelled tonic. The notation supplies the spelling (`FRAMEWORK.md` L0, lines
305–310: "spelling is read, and it is evidence for the tonality"; the framework relays Temperley's 83.8 % → 87.4 %
on using spelling). A pitch-class tonic collapses C♯ major and D♭ major, and the collapse is not always
recomputable from what is kept — the enharmonic modulation of OQ-L2-17 is the case the key signature does not
resolve. Towards the principles: #12's recomputable-collapse clause fails at exactly that case; #1 (the one
published key finder the derivation cites uses the 42 spelled tonalities — FACT relayed from the boot pack's
extract, not read by this side); #7 (spelling is L0's fact, consumed not re-derived); #6 (one spelling
interpreter in the tree, D-105, index line only). Costs, carried: the state count is 42 tonalities rather than
24 — the tractability question, OQ-L2-8 ★, its own surface; the ratified framework's §0 term ("a tonic pitch
class") and the factorization document's state (Row 10.8, "(ratified)") each need an amendment, the mechanism
Ruling 2 of 2026-08-29 calls working rather than a defect. Meta level: the analysis.

**Declined: position two** — the tonic a pitch class (12 tonics, 24 keys), as the incumbent joint estimator's
state, the legacy decoder and `FRAMEWORK.md` §0 give it. Towards the objective: the smaller state space; an
enharmonic modulation needs no respelling. Against: the spelled degree and the German-sixth distinction then
rest on a spelling chosen after the tonality is decided, the information #12 says to carry; the framework's own
L0 text calls spelling evidence for the tonality, which a pitch-class tonality can use but cannot publish.
Towards the principles: #14 (the ratified incumbent); D-200 (make it work first); the decision-neutrality
corollary says the existing implementation's reuse value is secondary. **Declined: position three** — spelled in
the publication, pitch class in the search state: a design of the search, not of the vocabulary (OQ-L2-8's
question), inheriting position two's #12 cost and adding a second representation of one object (#6); L2-S2 still
requires the enharmonic readings an analyst writes to be admissible, which a 24-state search cannot distinguish.

**UNSETTLED, not filled:** whether Ruling 2 of 2026-08-29 reaches `FRAMEWORK.md` §0's term definitions; which act
