## Ruling 1 — an extra research iteration, after the detail specifications and before measurement design

**How it arose.** After the targeted research reading for L2-S22 landed
(`ratification_surfaces/cowork_l2_context_convergence_research_2026_10_07.md`), the user wrote, verbatim:

> *"At some (later) point, maybe just before audit code vs spec (?), we should do an extra iteration on public
> research/algorithms/software with the objective to double check that what our specs say is what we believe will
> meet the ultimate and prime objective: best possible inference. The kind of research we then will look for can then
> be based on the then current specified architecture / algorithms etc > more specific/targeted than we might have
> been until now. What do you think?"*

This side agreed and suggested placing it slightly earlier than the audit — at the end of the detail-specification
phase, before measurement design — with the reasons and scope below. **The user's word, verbatim:** *"put this
research-iteration in the plan as you suggested, then carary on."*

**What is ruled.**

1. **When.** One research iteration over all layers, **after the last layer's detail specification is ratified and
   before the measurement-design phase opens** (the phase order of
   `ratification_surfaces/cowork_phase_definition_surface_2026_08_15.md` §3, as `CLAUDE.md`
   Conventions states it, *checked* there; the surface itself not opened: preparation → pilot → framework → detail specifications → measurement design → audit). *Why there:*
   measurement design fixes what is measured and fitted, so an algorithm changed afterwards would force part of it to be
   redone; and the audit compares code against the specification, so a specification changed after the audit would
   force that part of the audit to be redone.
2. **Its objective.** To check that what the specifications say is what is believed to meet the ultimate and prime
   objective, best possible inference (#4). The question asked per statement: **is there a published method, algorithm
   or public software that would infer better than what is specified?** Under the user's ruling of 2026-10-07 that no
   design rule may block that objective in the long run, a finding giving reason to suspect that a rule costs precision
   sends that rule to a measurement.
3. **Its scope — targeted, not a second reading programme.** Aimed at the load-bearing statements: those resting on
   this side's own derivation rather than on a ruling or a fetched source, those marked unestablished, and the open
   questions. Statements grounded in a ruling or a fetched source need less. Public software doing the same job is
   included beside papers, as reference points for the best existing results.
4. **How it is read.** Under the theory-grounding corollary to #1/#2 (D-473): FACT / THEORY / CONJECTURE labels,
   central sources extracted independently and cross-checked, no equation out of a text not fetched. Where a source is
   load-bearing, a copy placed in the repository so it can be read whole is preferred to a web fetch (this sitting's
   fetches returned only a summarising model's quotations, never the pages).

**What it does not do.** It changes no specification, no statement of any layer's algorithm, no phase definition and no
ruling; it adds a step to the order of work. It does not replace the targeted readings a family may need before then
(as the L2-S22 reading of this sitting). Whether the phase-definition surface's text is amended to name the step is left
to the application act; this record is the step's home until then.

**Ground.** Towards the objective (#4): the specifications are checked against the best published work at the point
where they are complete enough to aim the search. Towards the principles: #1 and #2 (specific research over general —
the iteration is aimed by the specified architecture), #5, D-473, D-200's order (nothing is built before). Meta level:
the order of work on the analysis's own specification — a planning act, kept narrow in scope at point 3.
