## 2. Ruling 2 — Δ2: §9.0 is ruled before the layer-ownership question, which is NOT decided

**The question of what a unit is — a factor of the model, a decision the analysis makes about the
music, or a reconciliation of the two — is ruled FIRST.** Δ2, whether a layer owns the answer to one
question or one evidence source's contribution to it, **is not decided here.**

**The ground.** Both the incumbent rule and the derived rule are statements about **units**, so
neither can be graded before the unit is fixed. Deciding Δ2 first would be deciding a consequence
ahead of its premise. The framework document offers §9.0 as this phase's first ratified finding, for
the user to rule, and declines to settle it itself.

**Recorded as a leaning and NOT as a ruling**, so that it is not later mistaken for one: on the
evidence as it stands the writing side would land on the split — the fact layers and the voice-leading
axis keep *(evidence-source × question)*, and the four entangled questions take one question, one
answer, one act — because the evidence establishes entanglement for those four and not beyond them.
**No weight rests on this and nothing is to be built from it.**

## 3. Ruling 3 — Δ3: the segmentation rival is agreed in principle and HELD

**The user agreed to Alternative A:** the harmonic span becomes a decided object, and a rival reading
may differ in where the boundaries fall rather than only in its labels.

**HELD, on two independent grounds, neither of them a preference.**

**(a) The framework document's own §1.4.** Ratification of the decomposition is separately HELD until
the external research list arrives and is dispositioned. **Δ3 is a decomposition decision**, so ruling
it now would breach a hold this sitting has no authority over.

**(b) The §10 code-site fill-in bears on it directly and is one dispatch away.** **B7 of §10 is Δ3
restated as a behavioural statement** — *"a rival that differs in segmentation is published as such,
and not flattened onto the committed reading's boundaries."* Binding B7 to a code site either finds a
site or finds none, which converts Δ3 from an argument between two documents into a fact about the
system. **Ruling ahead of evidence that cheap is what the standing investigate-by-default rule
forbids.**

**The evidence as it stands, recorded so the hold does not lose it.** The ground truth publishes a
rival differing in segmentation — one bar read as three statements principally and four in the
recorded variant [FACT — the framework document reports it read at the object; **not re-established
here**]. The incumbent carries alternatives as ranked labels with an uncertain mark, over a fixed
slice grid [FACT — read at `ARCHITECTURE.md` §2.15 by the writing side].

**One correction to how the cost of Alternative A is usually stated, established at both texts.** The
founding principle claims that making segmentation a fact is what makes over-grab structurally
impossible. **That benefit comes from the exhaustive candidate grid — every onset opening a candidate
— which the derived framework keeps at L1 and extends to releases.** So Alternative A costs the claim
that a boundary is not a judgment. **It does not cost over-grab impossibility.**

## 4. Ruling 4 — Δ5: retiring merge-equal is agreed in principle and HELD, in the same act as Δ3

**The user agreed to Alternative A:** grouping equal analyses is retired as the mechanism by which
coarser objects are derived.

**HELD on the same two grounds as Ruling 3, and it is one act with it.** Merge-equal is the founding
principle's mechanism and the absence of segmentation rivals is its consequence; fixing either fixes
both, and neither can be ruled while the other is held.

**The evidence, recorded and not re-established here.** The ground truth falsifies merge-equal in both
directions [FACT — the framework document reports both read at the object]: one analysis writes the
same label twice at consecutive onsets, a boundary that grouping by equality erases; another writes a
dominant and then its seventh as two statements inside one harmony, two labels that grouping by
equality would keep apart.

**One alternative is excluded on the user's own standing convention rather than on preference.**
Treating those two ground-truth objects as annotation artefacts is not available: the standing
convention is that the human annotation is the only ground truth and our own output is never a
standard of correctness, so reclassifying the annotation's distinctions as artefacts inverts it.
