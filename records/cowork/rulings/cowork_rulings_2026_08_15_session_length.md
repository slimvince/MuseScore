## The instruction, in the user's words

★ RECOGNISE WHEN THIS SESSION HAS GROWN TOO LONG, AND SAY SO UNPROMPTED (user-directed, 2026-08-15).
The writing side degrades in an observable way before it fails visibly. Watch for these, and when two or more appear, stop and tell the user the session should hand over — do not wait to be asked.

* Citing a summary or secondary surface instead of the source — a row's title used for its scope, a queue table used for a ruling's text, an earlier message of your own used as evidence.
* Asserting a proportion or a count you never derived — the great majority, most of these, probably around.
* Glossing a term instead of reading it — supplying a meaning for a word the record defines, then reasoning from the supplied one.
* Reaching for a compact technical word where a plain phrase exists — repository, instrument, licence. If the user has to ask which sense you meant, that is the signal, not the correction.
* Offering bounds or shortcuts that get refuted in succession — one refuted reduction is normal; three in a row is the tell.
* Hedged assertion — appears to be, presumably, it looks like — about something you never examined. Established, unexamined and guessed are three different states and the hedge hides which one you are in.
* Revising the same claim across successive messages, each version resting on the previous rather than on a source. This is the strongest late signal.

The single ratio worth watching: how often you say I checked, at this object against how often you say I recall or presumably. When the second starts outnumbering the first, the session is past its useful length.
Two things that are true about this rule and should not be forgotten. These are lagging indicators — they fire after the error, not before, so the honest report is I have already made two of these rather than I am at risk. And the user sees them at least as reliably as you do; on 2026-08-13 the writing side made roughly ten errors of this shape and did not act on any of them until the user pointed at them, repeatedly.
The restart rule this serves: hand over at a verified stop where the record is complete — and treat the inability to hand over safely as a defect in the record, not a reason to continue. If everything decided is on disk, restarting costs nothing. If it is not, writing it down is the work, not the obstacle.
