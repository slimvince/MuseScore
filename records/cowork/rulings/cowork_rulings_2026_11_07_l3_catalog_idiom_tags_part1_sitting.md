## 2. The user's thought on labels, recorded (not a ruling)

Put by the user after the question and before his answer, in his words: *"We should recognize what is in the score,
infer "things" in the score - these two should be done regardless of presentation and instead be based on music
theory's "things" that appear in music. These "things" (including but not restricted to known "patterns") should be
handled abstractly. Not until we need to present a human being with anything all these abstract facts (actual and
infered) we make a decision on how to actually present it - just like the "user preferences" could be (might not
necessarily end up like this though) "styles" (jazz/pop/romantic/baroque/...) that are NOT the abstract things we
internally deal with but must be "translated"."*

**The writing side's readings of what it bears on, not decided:**
1. Key Ruling 1's *"the entry's name"*, published per match, read as the abstract identity of the recognised thing;
   the human name of a progression (and written forms such as `subV7/I`) a presentation label. The amendment of
   `FRAMEWORK.md` should say so plainly.
2. The five idioms are defined by harmonic content (they superseded the hand-made genre taxonomy, Rows 46.47 and
   46.48), so an idiom tag is closer to an abstract property of an entry than to a display label; mode and chromaticism
   can be read off an entry's chords. User-facing style words (jazz, pop, …) would be a translation onto them, at
   presentation.

The thought is consistent with the placements ratified at §1; neither reading changes one.
