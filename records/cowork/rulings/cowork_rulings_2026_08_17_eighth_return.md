## 3. Ruling 3 — a vocabulary replacement, standing: TOWARDS the objective, never AGAINST it

**The user identified a presentation defect at this sitting and it is ruled as a standing
vocabulary replacement, binding all plan prose, both sides.** The user's words, verbatim:

> *"Stop saying "against the objective" the ultimate objective - it is very easily interpreted the
> opposite way - especially when you use "against:" just before it where it HAS the opposite
> meaning. Say "towards the objective" or something similar. EXTREME langauge precision with as
> little risk as possible for misunderstandings is key, for me and probably also for you and CC."*

**THE REPLACEMENT:** a decision surface rates each alternative **TOWARDS the ultimate objective**
and **TOWARDS the guiding principles** — never *against* either. The standing rule that every
choice carries its alternatives with pros and cons, each naming its principle and each rated on
both axes, is unchanged in substance; only the rating word changes.

**THE DEFECT, stated so the rule is understood rather than obeyed by pattern.** In the surface that
occasioned this, each alternative carried a bolded **Against:** heading — the case OPPOSING that
option — and then, four lines below, *"Against the principles"* and *"Against the ultimate
objective"*, where the same token meant the OPPOSITE: measured TOWARDS a standard. One token, two
contrary senses, in adjacent lines, sixteen times in one surface. **The general form is what
binds:** a word carrying opposite senses in adjacent context is a defect whatever the word is, and
the writing side fixes it at the word rather than trusting the reader to disambiguate. This joins
the earlier vocabulary rulings — *a changed passage* for hunk, *the current commit* for bare HEAD,
*untrusted source* for witness, *measurement tool* for instrument — under the same discipline: the
reader knows music theory, not this repository's internals, and precision is the writing side's
duty, not the reader's work.
