## 2. THE RULING — what a decision surface must do with its alternatives

**The user's words, verbatim: "MAke sure decision alternatives are fact based and weighed against
ultimate objective and guiding principles. If they conflict, ultimate objective takes precedence."**

**The ruling, in three parts:**

- **(a) EVERY ALTERNATIVE IS FACT-BASED.** An option's pros and cons rest on established fact — read
  at a source, or derived at the record — and never on an asserted causal claim about our own system.
  **A claim about our own system that is checkable is checked before it carries load in a surface
  (#18), and one that cannot be checked is LABELLED as a reading rather than stated as a fact.**
- **(b) EVERY ALTERNATIVE IS WEIGHED AGAINST BOTH the ultimate objective — maximum-precision
  inference (#4) — AND the guiding principles.** Both, explicitly, per option. **A rating that says
  "nothing changes" on either axis is not a rating and does not discharge this.**
- **(c) ★ WHERE THE TWO CONFLICT, THE ULTIMATE OBJECTIVE TAKES PRECEDENCE.** This is the part the
  record did not previously carry: it fixes a precedence the existing rules leave open.

**What it sharpens rather than replaces.** **D-424** already required a surface to name the principle
behind every pro and con and to rate every option on two axes; **this names what the two axes are**,
and adds the fact-basis requirement and the precedence rule. **D-599** (apply the standing principles
first; what remains for the user is ratifying the derivation) is untouched and still runs first.
**D-658** (where the record does not settle the question, gather facts and make no recommendation) is
untouched.

**Why, in the ground the user has already put on the record.** Ruling 2 of 2026-08-30 widened this
pass's scope on exactly this argument, in his words: *"it is the entire architecture as a whole that
is evaluated against the ultimate objective, not separate pieces individually."* **A surface that
rates options on process hygiene rather than on the objective cannot serve that.**

## 3. ★ ONE QUESTION THE RULING RAISES, PUT BACK TO THE USER AND NOT ANSWERED HERE

Part (c) says the objective takes precedence over the guiding principles on conflict. **Read at its
widest, that could be taken as licence to override a SAFETY principle — #18 (no unverified causal
premises), #19 (no unestablished measurement tools, corpora, gates or figures), #20 (fit and
evaluation separated) — by asserting an objective benefit.**

**This side does not believe that is what was meant, and does not act on the wide reading**, for a
reason drawn from the principles themselves: **#18, #19 and #20 exist BECAUSE unestablished things do
not serve maximum-precision inference.** So a claimed conflict between the objective and one of them
is, in the ordinary case, **evidence that the objective claim is itself unestablished** — which is
precisely the defect §1 records this session committing.

**But the narrowing is not this side's to take** (D-643: a claim invoking a ruling quotes it in full,
never the branch that supports the claim). **The question is therefore recorded and put back:** does
part (c) reach the establishment principles, or does it govern only where the conflict is between the
objective and a principle of *method or form*? **Until it is answered, this side applies part (c) as
written and flags any case where the wide reading would change an outcome.** *No such case has arisen:
at the V4 surface the objective and the principles agree, so the precedence rule does not bite there
and was not exercised.*
