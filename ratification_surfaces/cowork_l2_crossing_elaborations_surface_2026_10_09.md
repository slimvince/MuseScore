## §A.1 What this family is

The derived statement, whole (*checked*, `cowork_blind_derivation_l2_2026_09_27.md`, its lines 822–842):

> **L2-S33. The elaboration term for an event whose anchor, the preparing or resolving note, lies in an
> adjacent span reads that adjacent span's candidate reading. The candidate score is therefore not the
> "weak" semi-Markov form, in which a span term sees only its own label. It is a form in which the pair
> term also carries the elaborations that cross the boundary.**
> - *Defense.* A suspension is prepared in the previous harmony and resolved in its own. An anticipation
>   sounds before the harmony it belongs to [THEORY]. The published weak-form system had to invent "a
>   heuristic to determine whether an anchor note is harmonic whenever the anchor note belongs to the
>   previous segment", because its "features … do not have access to the candidate label y_{k−1}" [FACT
>   (relayed, 08) — Masada & Bunescu, Appendix B, p. 15]. The general semi-Markov form permits
>   previous-label-conditioned segment scores. Both published musical systems use the weak form for speed
>   [FACT (relayed, 08) — Yang, Cwitkowitz & Duan 2023, §3.2, as the extract compares them with Sarawagi &
>   Cohen]. Using the weak form would replace a decided fact (the adjacent chord) with a guess, which is
>   the first-running-detector defect in miniature (L2-S23).
> - *Source class.* Derived.
> - *Status.* Settled. Its cost falls on L2-S36.
> - *Premise.* Elaborations cross at most one boundary. **False-negative path:** a long pedal point
>   crossing several spans. Its assignment per span (L2-S9) and the pair terms handle each crossing
>   separately, which is enough if "pedal point" is an admitted relation (OQ-L2-4).
> - *Falsifier.* CODE. **Observable:** the elaboration term's inputs. **Decision rule:** falsified if the
>   term for a suspension reads a heuristic stand-in for its preparing span's chord rather than that
>   span's candidate chord. **Not falsified by:** a precomputed consonance feature used beside it.

**In plain words.** Some non-chord notes are defined by a note in the *neighbouring* harmony. A suspension is held
over from the previous chord, where it was a chord tone, and then resolves. An anticipation sounds just before the
chord it belongs to. When L2 scores a candidate reading, the part that judges such a note must know what chord the
reading puts in the neighbouring stretch. L2-S33 says it reads that neighbouring stretch's *candidate* chord — the
chord of the reading being scored — and not a guess made beforehand. That is why the link between two adjacent
stretches (the "pair term" of L2-S31) also carries the notes that cross the boundary.

- **Masada & Bunescu 2019** (extract lines 166–179): the chord-tone / figuration decision is made "INSIDE the segment

**The two forms, in plain words.**
- **Form 1 — the name inside the decision (the derivation as written).** For each candidate reading, every non-chord
  note is scored *as* a passing note, a suspension, an appoggiatura, and so on, each relation with its own fitted
  rates, using its approach and departure (L2-S24), its duration and beat (L2-S25), and, where its anchor lies across
  the boundary, the neighbouring stretch's candidate chord (L2-S33). The relation is part of the reading that wins, and
  readings that differ only in a note's relation are rivals with their own mass.
- **Form 2 — the name read off afterwards.** The candidate score decides, per note, chord tone or elaboration, using
  the same evidence — approach and departure, duration and beat, and whether the note's anchors are chord tones of the
  candidate chords on either side (L2-S33 still holds: the anchor is judged against the neighbour's candidate chord,
  not a guess). Once the reading is decided, each elaboration is named by the standard definitions from the decided
  chords and L1's published intervals, and published as part of the reading. The name never influences which chord
  wins.

**What differs between them, as far as this side can establish.**
- *For the chords and boundaries* (*my reading*): given a decided reading, the standard definitions name most
  elaborations without ambiguity, so the two forms differ mainly in how the elaboration evidence is parametrised —
  one rate per relation, or one elaboration rate shaped by covariates. Which predicts chords better is not known; no
  source found compares them (§A.2). Masada & Bunescu credit their figuration heuristics, as "most likely", for one correct
  label in their Rock error analysis, where a suspension and a neighbour tone are told apart (extract lines 320–329,
  *checked* at the extract; the paper not opened); McLeod &
  Rohrmeier's ground for deriving after a base label is training data per label.
- *For the published name* (*my reading*): under Form 1 a note that two relations fit can carry both, with mass; under
  Form 2 the definitions must break such a tie by a rule. Under Form 2 the name is a deterministic function of the
  decided reading and of facts already published, so nothing else is lost (#12, the recomputable-collapse clause).
- *Grading*: no corpus known to the derivation labels each note's relation independently of the chord labels
  (OQ-L2-13), so the name itself cannot yet be graded under either form; a comparison of the two can be graded
  through the chords and boundaries.
- *Cost* (the search sizing, `ratification_surfaces/cowork_l2_search_sizing_2026_10_05.md` §3.6, *checked*): with the
  choice made per note inside a stretch, "negligible next to §3.4"; with the assignment carried across boundaries,
  "2^m to 9^m" per label for m held notes. *My reading:* L2-S9's consistency rule is about chord tone against
  elaboration only, so what a carried state must hold is the binary, not the name, under either form. Cost is not a
  ground for choosing now in any case (D-200: make it work first).
