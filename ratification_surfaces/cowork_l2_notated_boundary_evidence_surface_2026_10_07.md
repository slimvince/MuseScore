## §A.1 The statement, whole, and what it means

From `cowork_blind_derivation_l2_2026_09_27.md`, its lines 375–395 (*checked*):

> **L2-S13. L1's notated boundary evidence enters as terms on a boundary reading: the flags with their
> scope, the positioned marks and the per-voice relations. An absent flag is never read as an absent
> boundary. No flag forces or forbids a boundary.**
> - *Defense.* The evidence and its form are L1's published list [RULED — IC S-39, S-41, S-50]. The input
>   contract states in terms that "an absent flag is never read as an absent boundary" [RULED — IC beside
>   S-39, Ruling 48]. The notated evidence is phrase-shaped, not harmony-shaped. The ground truth writes a
>   phrase boundary where the harmony does not change and restates the chord [RULED — charter DP-J].
>   Exemplar 5 shows the same shape: `m12 I || b4 I` and `m4 I || b4 G: I` [FACT at the file]. A fermata
>   is not the cadential arrival's exact place. […] So the evidence bears on the likelihood of a boundary
>   *near* the flag, not only at it.
> - *Source class.* Given — input contract, for the evidence and for "absent is not absent". Derived,
>   for its role as terms.
> - *Status.* Settled.
> - *Premise.* Notated punctuation correlates with harmonic boundaries without coinciding with them.
>   **False-negative path:** a flag at a change point where the harmony continues (the restated tonic
>   after a fermata). The term then pulls toward a boundary the reading does not need. The fit weighs it.
> - *Falsifier.* CODE. […] falsified if removing every flag from a passage makes some reading
>   inadmissible, or if adding a flag makes some reading inadmissible. **Not falsified by:** the candidate
>   scores changing.

(The cut marked […] in the defense drops the de Clercq sentence on where a chorale study placed the cadential
arrival relative to a fermata; the cut in the falsifier drops its observable. Neither bears on the rows below.)

**In plain words.** L1 (the layer below, which cuts the music into moments where some note starts or stops)
publishes, at each such moment, a set of *flags* for what the notation shows there: a bar line with its type
(single, double, final), a repeat sign, a fermata, a voice's rest beginning, every voice silent, a change of
time signature, key signature or tempo, a breath mark, a caesura. Each flag
carries the *scope* it was read at — the whole texture, or the one staff or voice it is written on
(`cowork_derived_specification_l0_l1_2026_09_03.md` S-39, lines 1093–1104, *checked*, which carries Ruling 48's
scope clause) *(§B item 1; former wording, preserved (#12): "… a voice's rest beginning, every voice silent. …
S-39, lines 1093–1099, *checked*, as amended by Ruling 48")*.
L1 also publishes a list of every notated mark of this kind with its position *(§B item 16; former wording,
preserved (#12): "every notated mark")* (S-41, lines 1177–1183, *checked*), and per
voice the silence before each new note and the melodic step into it (the "per-voice relations", L2-S24, lines
623–629, *checked*). L2-S13 says:

1. These marks are **evidence** for where a chord change falls. Each enters the candidate score as a term on
   reading a chord boundary at (or near) that moment.
2. A missing mark never means "no chord change here".
3. A mark never **forces** a chord change and never **forbids** one. A chorale can write a fermata where the
   harmony carries straight on (L2-S13's own false-negative path) *(§B item 2; former wording, preserved (#12):
   "A chorale often writes")*. How much each kind of mark counts is fitted, not set by hand.

**What L2-S13 is not.** It does not decide where a *phrase* ends. Picking phrase ends from the marks is a
phrase decision, made by the layer above (L3) when it reads the phrases off (Ruling 48, §A.2).

1. **A flag is used for two things in the old texts.** (a) Where a chord changes: 10.33(i), 48.14 ("segment-boundary

   transition decision"), and 44.8(iii) ("phrase-aligned key spans"). L2-S13 says "terms on a boundary reading".

   fields and the evidence at their shared boundary." The fields include the tonality, so a term in which a
   fermata makes a change of tonality at that moment more likely is within the stated form. *My reading:* the
   (b) rows are carried by L2-S13 read with L2-S31; naming L2-S31 beside them tells a later reader where the
   tonality half lives. L2-S34(i) is worded "a cost for a tonality change, graded by the distance between the two

2. **The nesting rows (21.49(ii), 43.100(ii)).** For "chord-spans ⊂ punctuation-spans" to hold, every phrase end
   would have to be a chord boundary. In L2 nothing makes that hold — a phrase end is a chord boundary only where
   the reading puts one — and the record says why *(§B item 15; former wording, preserved (#12): "In L2 that cannot
   be made to hold, and the record says why")*:
   - no flag forces a boundary (L2-S13), and the user confirmed the same for rests (silence Ruling 2);
   - two adjacent spans never carry the same tonality and chord (L2-S14, lines 397–400, *checked*), so L2 cannot
