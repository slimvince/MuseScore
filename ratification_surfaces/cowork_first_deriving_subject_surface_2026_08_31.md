## 3. ★ THE PRIOR QUESTION, AND IT IS THE FRAMEWORK'S OWN, NOT AN INFERENCE OF MINE

`FRAMEWORK.md` **§9.0** is headed, in the document's own words:

> **"The prior question: what is a unit? — PUT TO THE USER, NOT SETTLED"**

Three readings are on the table: a unit is a **factor of the model**; a unit is a **decision the
analysis makes about the music**; or a unit is a **reconciliation of the two**. The document **works at
the second** and says why — the ground truth is a record of decisions and contains no factor; analyst
disagreement is disagreement about decisions; the ledger's admitted facts are overwhelmingly that a
decision is underdetermined; the multi-task literature's separately-predicted heads are decisions
whose measured pathology is decisions disagreeing.

**Why it stands in front of this decision.** The section states its own consequences, and I quote them
rather than characterise them:

> *"What the factor reading would change: L2 would decompose into roughly ten units; this framework
> would own the factor roster and the conditional-independence premises; and the boundary contracts
> would become independence claims rather than published-fact contracts. What the reconciliation
> reading would change: **every decision would carry its factor set as sub-units, roughly doubling the
> unit count and making every charter two-tiered.**"*

**Read plainly: the factor reading reshapes L2 and the boundary contracts; the reconciliation reading
reshapes EVERY charter, L1's included.** So whichever subject is derived first, a later ruling on §9.0
can reshape what was derived. **That is the framework's statement, not my prediction about our
system.** *(I am careful about the distinction: twice today I put a plausible reason of my own into the
record as though it were a rule's content, and both times it was wrong. This one is quoted.)*

**What the reading pass found about §9.0 — verdict ENRICHES, and it does not settle it.** Everything
read is about decisions, not factors: annotation standards, inter-annotator agreement, label
vocabularies, two defensible ground-truth columns for one passage. **Nothing read models an analysis as
a factor roster.** One exception is worth the user's attention at the moment he rules: **row 3's unified
chord model represents a chord as a decision carrying an explicit attribute set beneath it** — score
pitches, then pitch functions including chord-tone status, then chord properties, with the
unabstracted level kept in the same object. **That is precisely the "reconciliation" reading, exhibited
by a working representation rather than argued for.** It infers nothing, so it cannot settle the
question, **but it shows the third reading is buildable, which §9.0's own framing left open.**

**So the evidence leans where the document already works — decisions — with one live demonstration
that the third reading is constructible.** That is a cheap ruling to take and an expensive one to leave
standing.

## 4. The facts that bear on the ordering

**(a) Every chosen design point survived the pass. No falsifier, in twenty rows.** So there is no
reason to delay derivation to protect the frame. **This removes the strongest possible argument for
"not yet."**

**(b) ★ L1's territory is entirely CHOSEN and entirely supported; L2's is where the open questions
live.** Of the framework's four points that are **NONE CHOSEN — underived**, **three sit in or against
L2's path**:

- **DP-N** — which theory of the cadential six-four the label vocabulary takes. The pass's verdict:
  **NO BEARING.** Row 3 was asked directly and does not discuss it. The only thing found is a
  *mitigation* — graded chord-label distances that soften the measurement cost whichever vocabulary is
  ruled. **The framework already says this one "needs a ruling or new research", and the pass looked
  and found neither.**
- **DP-O** — whether the framework commits to a hierarchical reading of harmony. **NO BEARING, "and
  the absence is itself the finding":** four rows sit in its territory and **not one supplies the
  falsifier DP-O names.** Row 19, the fullest implementation of the time-span-reduction line, **does
  not model harmony hierarchically at all** and its measured arm is monophonic.
- **DP-Q** — whether the analysis may decline to read a span. **ENRICHES:** three independent
  statements that some music is not well described by chord vocabulary — **none of them on our
  repertoire** — so the premise is corroborated and the question is not answered.

**L1's own content, by contrast, runs entirely inside CHOSEN points** and the pass supported all of
them: change points at onsets **and releases** (row 1 demands exactly "metrical levels of onset and
offset"); the grid critique (BACHI replaces frame quantisation with one representation per onset and
reports it as the fix); metric strength; and the cadence-cue split of DP-I, where two independent
studies measure F ≈ .80 on authentic cadences without chord segmentation or tonality estimation and
F .29–.41 on half cadences, **which is exactly why only the cues live at L1.**

**(c) ★ The chain-level finding, and it is the pass's sharpest result about L2.** *"No chain the read
material exhibits can express what the ratified frame requires L2 to publish"* — specifically **rivals
with mass including segmentation-differing rivals**, and **chord-tone assignment decided together with
chord identity**. The one composable chain in the whole read set (row 1 → row 2 → row 6) does neither

express — was NOT found.**

**What that is and is not.** It is evidence the frame is **not a re-description of an existing
pipeline**. **It is not evidence the frame is right.** The honest statement is the surface's own: *the
frame asks for something the field has not built, for reasons the field has not tested.*

**(d) ★ AND THE POINT THAT CUTS AGAINST "DO THE HARD ONE FIRST" — the pass already did that job.** The
usual reason to derive the riskiest piece first is to find the blockers early. **The blockers are
already found: fact (b) names them and fact (c) names the gap.** The reading pass was commissioned
precisely to survey where the frame is unsupported, and it did. **So deriving L2 first would not
discover its blockers; it would arrive at them.** That is a materially weaker benefit than it was
before the pass ran, and it is the single biggest change the pass makes to the 2026-08-30 surface.

**(e) The dependency argument for L1-first is REAL BUT WEAKER THAN IT LOOKS, and the pass is why.**
L2 *"Consumes: everything L0 and L1 publish. Nothing else."* The reason to fix an input contract first
is that it might move. **The pass tested it and it did not move** — L1 drew support and no falsifier
anywhere. **A stable input contract is a weaker reason to go first, not a stronger one.**

**(f) ★ AND THE ORDERING CANNOT STARVE L2, because §5's interface is already ratified.** **What L1
publishes is charter-level and settled**: the slices, the metric strength class per change point, the
notated boundary evidence, the local cadence cues. **A detail specification derives HOW, not WHAT.**
So if L2's derivation later finds it needs something L1's charter does not publish, that is an
**amendment**, and the amendment route is identical whichever order is taken. **Order changes when we
learn. It does not change the precision ceiling.** *(This is the fact that dissolves what would
otherwise be a genuine conflict between the objective and the principles — see §6.)*

**(g) A fact about L1's specification that makes it thinner than its citation count suggests.**
Established this same day, at the V4 surface's §6a: **L1 publishes a class and does not judge**; the

**(h) The phase's method has never been run.** Derive-before-compare, witnesses read only after each
derived statement is written, five recorded dispositions, complete by arithmetic. **This is a new
procedure and its first exercise is also its first test.** Where a new procedure is first exercised on

**(i) The user's own joint-evaluation ruling, quoted because it cuts against pure dependency

**(j) What is NOT a candidate, so the enumeration is honest.** **L3 first** — it consumes L2's

## 5. The options, in plain language

### Option A — Rule §9.0 first (what is a unit), then take the deriving subject

One ruling, on evidence the pass has already assembled, before any derivation begins.

- **Against the objective (#4).** **It is the only option that removes a reshaping risk from every
  charter rather than from one.** By §9.0's own words the reconciliation reading makes *every charter
  two-tiered* and the factor reading rewrites the boundary contracts as independence claims — and
  **independence claims about our own system are exactly the #18 Class A objects we may not assert
  unchecked.** Ruling it settles what kind of thing every subsequent derivation is producing.
- **Against the principles.** **#19** — it stops an unsettled question being treated as settled by
  drift, which is what happens to a "put to the user, not settled" item that nobody puts. **#5** — the
  investigation is done; the facts are assembled and there is nothing cheaper left to learn. **#6** —
  one home for the question. **#1** — the evidence is on the table and it is one-directional.
- **Cost and risk.** One sitting. **The likely outcome is that it confirms the reading the document
  already works at**, which is a small return — but the *cost of not ruling it* is carried by every
  specification derived afterward. **Risk: none identified.** It authorises no derivation by itself,
  so it is not sufficient on its own — it must be followed by a deriving-subject choice.

### Option B — Derive L0+L1 first

The 2026-08-30 recommendation, now standing on different ground.

- **Against the objective.** Produces the phase's first ratifiable specification quickly, on the layer
  the pass most strongly corroborated, **and pilots an unexercised method on bounded material where a
  procedural defect is distinguishable from a material difficulty** (fact h). **It does not raise the
  precision ceiling by itself** — L1 decides nothing about the music — but by fact (f) it does not
  lower it either.
- **Against the principles.** **#5** — L1's facts are not scarce; they are the best-evidenced in the
  frame, so this is the one place the phase can proceed without investigation. **#20/#96** — L1
  publishes a class and fits nothing, so nothing here can leak fit into evaluation. **Nothing weighs
  against it on principle**, and it touches **no underived design point**.
- **Cost and risk.** Small — fact (g) makes L1 thin. **The honest cost is that it spends the phase's
  first and freshest effort where uncertainty is lowest**, which under a strict reading of #4 is where
  effort buys least. **Residual risk: if L2's derivation later needs something L1's charter does not
  publish, an amendment follows** — and by fact (f) that is true in either order.

### Option C — Derive L2 first

Go straight at the entangled decision: segmentation, tonality, chord-tone assignment and chord
identity decided together, with rivals and their mass.

- **Against the objective.** **The strongest claim available to any option, and the pass has partly
  spent it.** L2 is where the precision lives and where fact (c) says the frame asks for something
  nobody has built. Deriving it first would test the frame's distinctive requirements soonest, and
  fact (i) is the user's own argument for it. **But fact (d) is the answer: the pass has already
  surveyed for those blockers and found them.** What L2-first would add is learning about *our own
  derivation*, and that learning is delayed by taking L1 first, not lost.
- **Against the principles.** **#5 cuts against it here, not for it**: three of the four underived
  design points sit in its path (fact b) and the pass established the reachable literature settles
  **none** of them, so an L2 derivation runs into questions that need **a ruling or new research**, not
  more derivation. **#19** — a derivation that has to assume an answer to DP-N, DP-O or DP-Q in order
  to proceed would be establishing by assumption exactly where the record says nothing is established.
  **And #18** — the frame's own §9.0 consequences bite hardest here: it is L2 that the factor reading
  decomposes into roughly ten units.
- **Cost and risk.** The largest subject, first, with an unexercised method (fact h) and three open
  theory questions in its path. **The real risk is not failure; it is a stall that looks like a
  finding.**

## 6. Objective against principles — do they conflict? *(worked, not asserted)*

**On the face of it, yes, and it is worth showing rather than waving away.** A strict reading of the
ultimate objective — maximum-precision inference — says go where the uncertainty is, and that is
**L2** (option C). The principles — #5, #19, #18 — point at **A then B**. **That is the shape of a
conflict, and the standing ruling says the objective would win it.**

**It does not survive contact with fact (f), and here is the working.** The objective concerns the
**precision of the end state**, not the information gained along the way. Ordering could only cost
precision if going second locked something worse in — and it cannot, because **what L1 publishes to L2
is fixed in §5's ratified boundary contract, not in either detail specification.** A detail
specification derives *how*, not *what crosses*. So if L2's derivation later needs something L1 does
not publish, that is an amendment, on an identical route in either order.

**Therefore ordering changes WHEN we learn, not WHAT the analysis can ultimately achieve.** With the
precision ceiling untouched by the order, the objective stops discriminating between the options, and
the principles decide. **The conflict dissolves on a fact rather than on a preference — and if that
fact were wrong, C would win.** *(Stated that way deliberately: it names what would overturn the
recommendation.)*

**The ratings.** **A is first on both axes.** **B is second on both.** **C is third on both.**

## 7. Recommendation

**A, then B: rule §9.0 first, then derive L0+L1.**

**Why A first.** It is one ruling on evidence already gathered, and it is the only item on the table
that reaches *every* charter. §9.0's own text says the factor reading rewrites the boundary contracts
as independence claims and the reconciliation reading makes every charter two-tiered. **Deriving
anything before that is deriving into a shape a pending ruling can change**, and the pass has already
done the work that would inform it. **The most likely outcome — that it confirms the decisions reading
the document already works at — is not a reason to skip it; it is what makes it cheap.**

**Why B second.** It touches **no underived design point**, it is the layer the pass most strongly
corroborated, it is genuinely small (fact g), and it exercises an untested method where a procedural
defect can still be told apart from a hard problem. **Its weakness is real and named: it spends first
effort where uncertainty is lowest** — and fact (f) is why that weakness does not cost precision.

**Why not C, said plainly.** Not because L2 is hard, and not because dependency order forbids it —
**the user's own joint-evaluation ruling is a serious argument for going at L2 first, and I am not
dismissing it.** The reason is fact (d): **the pass has already done C's job.** Its value was to find
the frame's unsupported places early, and they are found — DP-N, DP-O and DP-Q, with the reachable
literature settling none of the three, and a chain-level gap at exactly L2's two distinctive
requirements. **What remains at L2 is not discovery; it is derivation into three open theory questions
that need your rulings or new research.** That work is better done with the phase's method already
proven and the unit question already answered.

**★ And one thing this surface will not pretend.** The 2026-08-30 surface recommended L0+L1 too. **The
answer is the same; the reasons are not.** Then it was dependency order — L2 consumes L1, so settle L1
first. **The pass has since weakened that argument (fact e) and the user's own joint-evaluation ruling
cuts against it (fact i).** What carries the recommendation now is the asymmetry of open design points
(fact b), the method-pilot argument (fact h), and the ceiling argument (fact f). **If you rejected the
dependency reasoning in August, you are not being shown it again in a new coat.**
