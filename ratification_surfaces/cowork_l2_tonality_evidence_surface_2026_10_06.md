## §A.1 What this family is

The derived statement, whole (*checked*, `cowork_blind_derivation_l2_2026_09_27.md`, its lines 528–553):

> **L2-S20. The tonality terms read the chords the reading proposes (degree, function, cadential
> progressions) and a recency-weighted account of which spelled scale degrees have sounded. They do not
> read a global pitch-class profile of the span.**

In plain words: when L2 weighs which key a passage is in, it looks at two things. First, the chords its own
reading proposes, each understood as a degree with a function in that key, including cadences. Second, which
spelled scale degrees have sounded recently, with recent notes counting more than older ones. It does **not**
judge the key from one overall count of pitch classes over a stretch of music.

Its defense (*checked*): music that dwells on its tonic hardly sounds the tones that tell keys apart, so a
model that rates keys by those tones rates the true key lowest exactly where it is most strongly prolonged
(ledger C36); a published key finder argues that a few C♯s can change what we hear "no matter how many C♮
there were before" (Feisthauer et al. 2020, *relayed*); and "reading the proposed chords is what the joint
decision is for" — the charter, with Rocher et al. 2010's ablation as its measured ground (*relayed* — note
that entry 169 records a doubt about that paper's chord-axis figure; it says nothing of the tonality figure). Its
status: "Settled that the global profile is excluded. The exact recency form is *measured* (OQ-L2-3)."

**The rows.** Section 7 of `ratification_surfaces/cowork_comparison_l2_reading.md` lists 50 row-parts for
L2-S20: 18 AGREES, 32 DIFFERS (*checked*, its line 71144).
