Claude Code, written by Cowork sittings between 2026-11-08 and 2026-11-12 and not yet released. When it is released and

**What "Part B" is.** While writing the dispatch, the writing side (the Cowork sittings) had to settle a number of things

**Why they come to you.** Two Cowork entries planned it: entry 604 (§3 step 4) plans *"the user's overrule points … put

put to you so far. Under D-599 (*"apply the standing principles FIRST, and only where they do not decide is there a

gives the settlement as written, the principles it rests on, and the alternative an overrule would bring. Where the
principles leave no real alternative, it says so.

**Documentation only, or an algorithm change?** This sitting judges, from each settlement as read at its dispatch lines,

## B1 — Where the statements the rulings add go in the successor, and that they get no L3-S number

**What it is.** Some L3 ruling records do not just amend existing statements L3-S1 to L3-S42; they add new statements
(for example, the display record adds four, the architecture-rules record six). The records do not say where in the
successor these go or how they are numbered.

**The settlement as written** (dispatch lines 144–149, form (a)): the added statements go in one section of the
successor headed `## Statements added by the L3 family rulings`, after the last derived statement. Each is headed
`### Added — <record file>, Ruling N, <part as the record names it>` and carries the record's text quoted whole. No L3-S
number is given to any of them, and no existing statement is renumbered. Where a record gives only a subject and no
text, that addition is a STOP-and-report.

**The principles it rests on.** Not renumbering keeps every citation of an L3-S number elsewhere in the record pointing
at the same statement. Quoting the record whole means the act places the user's words and invents none (the standing
bar at dispatch lines 201–202: *"an amendment is the record's words placed, never this batch's"*). The dispatch says the
L2 act's form allowed no added statements at all; the L3 records order them, so a form had to be made.

**What an overrule would mean.** The main alternative is to give the added statements numbers (L3-S43 onward), so they
read as ordinary statements. That makes them easier to cite later, but the numbering order would be the writing side's
choice and not a ruling, and it would have to be decided now. Another alternative is placing each added statement next to
the statement it relates to; the records do not say which statement that is, so the act would have to judge it, which
the standing bars exclude.

**Towards the objective and the principles.** The settlement keeps the ruled text exact and adds no new judgment.
Numbering can be done later, at ratification, without losing anything. I see no reason from the principles to prefer an
alternative.

**Documentation only.**

## B2 — Where the lists the records leave "not ruled" go in the successor

**What it is.** Some records place lists in the successor's notes and mark them *"the writing side's proposal as put …
not a ruled act"* (carry list §5).

**The settlement as written** (dispatch lines 150–153): written in a section headed
`## Notes — lists the records place here, not ruled`, each list under its record's name, quoted, carrying the record's
own *"not a ruled act"* words.

**The principles it rests on.** Keeping unruled text visibly apart from ruled text, with the record's own warning beside
it, stops a later reader taking it as ruled.

**What an overrule would mean.** Either leave the lists out of the successor (they stay at their records), or put them
next to the statements they concern. Leaving them out loses nothing ruled, but the records place them in the successor,
so leaving them out departs from the records. Putting them next to statements needs the act to judge where they go.

**Towards the objective and the principles.** The settlement follows the records and labels the status. No principle
points to an alternative that I can see.

**Documentation only.**

**What it is.** Several records order changes to `FRAMEWORK.md` (the charter) and to the ratified interface contracts.

uses the charter's own *★ AMENDED … THE FORMER WORDING WAS* note form instead, as its L1 section already does).

**Towards the objective and the principles.** The settlement keeps the charter carrying only ruled words. Where a record
gives none, the change waits for you; it is listed, not lost.

## B4 — No decisions-register entry and no new D-number from this act

**What it is.** Several L3 records say their rulings supersede entries in the decisions register (`DECISIONS.md`) and
send those supersessions *"through the application act and the register route"*. The records do not say what the act
itself writes in the register.

**The settlement as written** (the standing bars, dispatch lines 204–207; lines 244–246; Task 5 at lines 5515–5517): the
batch creates no register entry and allocates no D-number. Each record's supersessions are listed in its ledger entry
under *Register* as a list, not an instruction. What the act writes for them is left *[AT RELEASE]*, to be fixed by the
releasing sitting. If the releasing sitting fixes nothing, the close writes nothing into `DECISIONS.md` and lists the
supersessions in its close.

**The principles it rests on.** The register records your decisions. An act that runs without you writing register
entries would be the act ruling on the register. Earlier batches carried the same bar: `STATUS.md`'s entry for the
2026-11-08 language-model batch says *"no new decisions-register entry, no D-NNN"*, and its 2026-09-02 entry says *"no
decisions-register entry and no D-NNN allocated"*.

**What an overrule would mean.** You could have the releasing sitting fix the supersessions' wording now, so Claude Code
writes them in this batch. That would mean a further surface: each supersession's wording read at its record and put to
you. Under the settlement as written, that work happens later by the register route and nothing is lost meanwhile,
because the close lists every supersession.

**Towards the objective and the principles.** Settled by the principle that the register is yours. What remains is
the *[AT RELEASE]* question of whether the releasing sitting writes anything for the supersessions. That question is a
Part C fact-taking step at release; this point asks only that the bar stands.

**Documentation only.**

**B6 item 2, at the record.** Ruling 1 of the pivot-label record (record lines 48–73) decided an algorithm change: L3

tonality L2 decided for a span". The re-writing is the application dispatch's act."* The check correction (2) (line 93)

1. **Writing the falsifier alone leaves L3-S7 half amended.** L3-S7's body (derivation lines 300–303: *"L3 does not

no alternative short of inventing text.
