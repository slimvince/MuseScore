## 1. The question, in plain words

The old design had a **catalog of named progressions** (its *Harmonic Vocabulary*): entries such as ii–V–I, the
turnarounds, the circle-of-fifths and other sequences, the lament bass and other bass-line patterns, the galant
schemata, and a list of substitution operations (a chord standing in for another of the same function, the tritone
substitution being the standard case). A separate **recognition** step, run over chords already decided, was to find
those entries in the music, report each found one with its name, and mark the stretch of music it covers as a span.
The same catalog was also meant to serve a future tool that suggests chords to a composer.

**The question:** does that catalog and its recognition belong to the harmonic analysis as the framework defines it
— and if not, where are the rows that describe it filed?

**Two uses, kept apart (added 2026-11-06 on the user's objection, §8).** The catalog can serve the analysis in two
ways. (a) **As evidence for choosing chords:** where a chord is in doubt, a candidate that makes the passage form a
known progression is evidence for that candidate. That use can only act inside L2, where the chord and key are
decided (item 3 below); the record has already placed it there as an open measurement question (item 4). It is
not put to the user on this surface. (b) **As a published label:** the analysis reporting "this passage is a
ii–V–I" over chords already decided. Positions A, B and C below are about (b) and about where the catalog's
rows are filed; none of them removes or weakens (a).
