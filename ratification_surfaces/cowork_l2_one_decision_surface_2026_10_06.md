## §A. The surface

### 1. What this is about, and the cut

The chord-and-key layer (L2) answers four questions about a stretch of music: what the key is at each
moment, where one harmony gives way to the next, which sounding notes are chord notes and which are
passing notes, suspensions and so on, and what chord is heard over each stretch. The ratified framework
says these four are answered **together, as one decision** (*checked*, `FRAMEWORK.md` §4.2 and the L2
charter). The older design documents of this project describe a different arrangement: a key layer
decides the key first, a chord layer then names chords under that key and leaves the hard cases open,
and a later "function" layer settles what was left open by picking among the readings the earlier
layers carried forward. **This family decides what happens to those older texts in L2's specification.**

The derived statement, whole (*checked*, `cowork_blind_derivation_l2_2026_09_27.md` §3b):

- **L2-S11:** "A boundary is one of L1's change points, and where the boundaries fall is decided together
  with the tonality, the chord and the assignments, in the one decision. It is never decided before the
  chord or after it." Source class: "Given — charter (repeated by the input contract)". Status: "Settled".
  Its falsifier: "falsified if a boundary lies at a position that is not a change point or span edge, or
  if the boundary set is computed by a pass that does not see candidate chords."
