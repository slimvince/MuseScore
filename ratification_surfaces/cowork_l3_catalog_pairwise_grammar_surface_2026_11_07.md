## 1. What this surface does, in plain words

### 1.1 The subject

The old design had two places that knew which chord may follow which. The **catalog** (the *Harmonic Vocabulary*)
held the named progressions and substitutions. The old function layer held a **pairwise licensing grammar**: a yes/no
test of whether the root motion between two chords is one of the "standard functional successions". The old documents
state a rule for how the two relate, which they call the D5 dependency map: neither is derived from the other; a change
to one never needs an edit of the other; the only link is a **consistency test** that runs one way, from the catalog
to the grammar (every adjacent pair inside a catalog entry must be licensed, and a failure is either a mis-encoded
entry or a gap in the grammar, to be escalated); and a licensed pair that no entry contains is not a gap of the
catalog. A separate passage of `ARCHITECTURE.md` §7 says that whether the grammar folds into the catalog or stays a
second store is a decision owed, to be made explicitly when the recognition consumer is built, never by drift.

The 15 row-parts are those sentences. The L3 reading file graded every one THE DERIVATION IS SILENT and proposed
every one UNPLACED. The grounds it gives: *"read as at Row 57.398"* (Rows 57.493 to 57.496), *"as at Row 7.85"* (Rows
57.396 (ii), 57.398), *"as at Row 44.39"* (Row 57.451), *"as at Row 19.38 (vi)"* (Row 38.79 (ii)), Row 24.1's finding
(Rows 24.4, 24.6), or travelling with another row (57.405). The common ground: no charter of `FRAMEWORK.md` §5 names a
catalog of named progressions, so none names its relation to the grammar **[checked at each entry, L3 reading file
