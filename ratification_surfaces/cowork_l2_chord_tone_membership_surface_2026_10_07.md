## §A.1 What this family is

The derived statement, whole (*checked*, `cowork_blind_derivation_l2_2026_09_27.md`, its lines 308–327):

> **L2-S10. An event is a chord tone of a span only if its spelled pitch class is a member of the span's
> chord, spelled. Membership does not imply chord tone, since a consonant elaboration is admitted (a
> consonant passing tone, for example). A chord is admissible over a span whose sounding set lacks its
> root, or lacks its third.**

In plain words, three rules:

1. **A note can be a chord note only if its written spelling belongs to the chord as spelled.** Over a
   chord spelled G♯–B–D–F, a written A♭ is not the chord's G♯; over a German sixth A♭–C–E♭–F♯, a written
   G♭ is not its F♯.
2. **Belonging to the chord does not make a note a chord note.** A note that is a member of the chord can
   still be read as a passing note or another elaboration — a consonant passing note, for example.
3. **A chord may be read over a stretch where its root does not sound, or where its third does not
   sound.** A missing root or a missing third does not by itself forbid the reading; how well the sounding
   notes fill the chord is weighed, not required (L2-S32: "a cost, never a requirement"). *(§B item 1.)*

Its defense (*checked*, same lines): spelled membership "because L0 supplies spelling and a chord is
spelled (L2-S1)"; the published rule set lists "can there be consonant non-chord tones?" among its four
sources of analytic ambiguity (relayed by the derivation from Condit-Schultz, Ju & Fujinaga, §1.3); the
derivation's ledger: a reading whose root does not sound "is not thereby wrong, and the published analysis
makes such readings" (ledger C1), and where the third does not sound "no signal at that moment separates
the readings" (ledger C34); so requiring the root or the third "would therefore exclude annotated
readings, which fails L2-S2". Source class: "Derived". Status: "Settled".

Its premise and known gap (*checked*): "Spelling is correct in the record." The false-negative path it
names: "a respelled passing note (a ♯4 written as ♭5 for voice-leading reasons) whose spelling differs from
the chord's member. It would never be a chord tone." The note-layer contract "publishes the spelling as
written and grades nothing" (relayed by the derivation: input-contract Ruling 58 (i)), and "An
enharmonic-equivalence allowance at L2 is part of OQ-L2-1". Its falsifier: falsified "if an event whose
spelled pitch class is not in the chord is assigned chord tone, or if a chord is refused over a span for
lacking its root"; "Not falsified by: a chord member assigned elaboration."

**OQ-L2-1**, the derivation's open question that holds L2-S10's share (*checked*, its line 1201): "the
closure measurement. Convert every annotated span of the grading corpora … into L2's four fields, and list
every annotated reading the admission rule does not reach. This decides the vocabulary's completeness
(…; an enharmonic allowance for chord-tone membership). *A measurement, not a ruling.* UNESTABLISHED." The
chord-vocabulary surface of 2026-10-05 left L2-S10's share of it to this family (*checked*, that surface's
lines 53–55). It is a measurement and is not put to you.

**The rows.** Section 7 of `ratification_surfaces/cowork_comparison_l2_reading.md` lists 29 row-parts for

### §A.4.1 Decision 1 — Row 6.45: is the written spelling a condition for a chord note, or one weighed signal?

**The row, whole** (*checked*, comparison line 9947), from `cowork_layer4_chordsymbol_design.md` §2, the
design of the old per-slice chord engine: the notated spelling "is a strong-but-fallible signal
(engraving can be expedient; a MIDI import may carry arbitrary spelling), so it is weighed, not trusted
blindly, by the precedence ladder in §5."

**The question, in plain words.** The written spelling can be wrong. An engraver may write a note in the
easier spelling, and a score imported from MIDI gets whatever spelling the import program guessed. The old
design treated the spelling as one piece of evidence among others. L2-S10 makes it a condition: a note
whose written spelling is not in the chord can never be that chord's note. The derivation saw the risk and
named it (a ♯4 written as ♭5), and routed the cure — an "enharmonic allowance", meaning a note may count as
a chord member it sounds like though it is spelled differently — to the closure measurement OQ-L2-1.

**A reading that bears on it (my reading of OQ-L2-1's own words, not checked at any corpus).** OQ-L2-1 runs
on the grading corpora it names — exemplar 1's two annotation traditions and exemplar 5's. It can show how
often their spelling leaves an annotated chord unreachable. It is not designed to show how often an
imported score's guessed spelling does; whether any grading corpus was itself imported from MIDI was not
checked. No measurement this sitting found on the record is aimed at that half of Row 6.45's concern.
*(§B item 4.)*

**Alternative A — file Row 6.45 HISTORICAL; L2-S10 stands; the concern carried.**
- What it does: the row is filed as a superseded design of the old engine. L2-S10 is unchanged: spelled
  membership stays the condition. Carried with it, so nothing is dropped (#12): the enharmonic allowance is
  decided by OQ-L2-1's measurement, as the derivation already says; and it is recorded that OQ-L2-1 cannot
  show the imported-score case, which stays a named, unmeasured gap with no route chosen. **Files only; L2's
  algorithm unchanged.**
- Towards the objective (#4): keeps the spelled distinctions the graded annotations write — the German
  sixth against the dominant seventh by spelling (Row 50.26), the diminished seventh's root from its
  spelling (Row 6.101), the secondary leading tone read from the spelling (Row 5.178). Cost: on a badly
  spelled score, a chord whose notes are misspelled cannot be read with those notes as chord notes; the
  analysis would then prefer another reading or read the notes as elaborations (*my reading*).
- Towards the principles: D-254 and #5 (the allowance is measured, not guessed); #18 (no design rests on
  the unchecked premise that misspelling is common enough to matter); #20 and #21 (no fitted cost for a
  spelling mismatch, which no ground truth could fit); #12 (the concern carried, the gap named).
- Meta level: the analysis's own specification.

**Alternative B — hold Row 6.45 UNPLACED, travelling with OQ-L2-1.**
- What it does: the row waits for the measurement's result. **Files nothing now; L2's algorithm
  unchanged.**
- Towards the objective: the same as A for now.
- Towards the principles: #12 met as in A. Against keeping it open: the measurement it waits for is not
  aimed at its imported-score half (above), so the row could wait on a result that never addresses it.
  *(§B item 5.)*
- Meta level: the analysis's own specification.

**Alternative C — amend L2-S10 now: the spelling becomes a weighed signal.**
- What it does: a note that sounds like a chord member but is spelled differently may count as that chord
  member, at a cost in the candidate score. **Changes L2's algorithm.**
- Towards the objective: more robust on badly spelled input. Cost: the spelled distinctions in A are
  softened, since a misspelled-looking note can now be read either way and the cost decides.
- Towards the principles: the cost has no ground truth to be fitted on (#20, #21); it rests on an
  unchecked premise about how often spelling is wrong (#18); it settles from this surface what the
  derivation routed to a measurement (D-254).
- Meta level: the analysis's own specification.

**Recommendation: A.** It keeps the spelled precision the graded repertoire uses, sends the allowance to
the measurement the derivation already names, and writes down the one half of the concern no measurement
covers instead of leaving it open behind a measurement that cannot close it. B keeps a row open on a
result that cannot answer half of it. C builds on an unchecked premise and a cost nobody can fit. This is
the same shape you chose for the harmonic-sequence passages on 2026-10-07 (L2-S49 Ruling 1).

### §A.4.2 Decision 2 — Row 24.39 claim (i): when a chord's root sounds only in the bass part, must the analysis know which part is the bass?

*(Corrected 2026-10-07 at the user's challenge "How can anybody play a missing note?"; see §C. Former heading,
preserved (#12): "Decision 2 — Row 24.39 claim (i): must the analysis know which instrument supplies a missing
root?")*

**The row, whole** (*checked*, comparison lines 39144–39160), from `ARCHITECTURE.md` §5.8, *Known Analyzer
Limitations*, *Rootless voicings*: "Correct handling requires knowing that a bass instrument is providing
the root, which is context the current analyzer does not have." Two claims: (i) reading such a chord
correctly requires knowing that a bass instrument supplies the root; (ii) the old analyzer lacks that
knowledge. Claim (ii) is proposed QUARANTINED with Row 23.41; claim (i) was left UNPLACED, because "neither
text names a layer that publishes which instrument supplies a root, so a disposition would decide whether
L2 needs that fact."

**The question, in plain words.** In jazz ensemble writing the piano often plays a voicing without the
root, and the bass player plays the root. The old texts say the old analyzer misread such chords (Rows 24.37
and 24.38: it promoted the lowest note to root); that is the old texts' claim, not checked (§C). L2-S10 does not need the
root to sound: a rootless chord is an admissible reading, weighed against the others. So the question is
whether L2 should also be told which part is the bass instrument, as evidence.

**Facts and readings that bear on it.** Which staves feed the analysis is decided at the fact adapter, not
by a later filter (D-013, *checked* at the register index). When the bass part is among the staves fed,
its notes are among the sounding notes L2 reads, and the root then sounds (*my reading*). Whether the note
layers publish which instrument each part is was **not checked** this sitting and is UNSETTLED. Jazz
chords are a named increment with its own premise ledger and desk simulation (chord-vocabulary Ruling 2,
*checked*).

**Alternative A — file claim (i) HISTORICAL for L2; the idea carried to the jazz increment.**
- What it does: the requirement is superseded for L2, since L2-S10 admits a chord whose root does not
  sound. The idea — that the role of an ensemble part, such as a bass instrument, is evidence about the
  root — is carried into the jazz named increment's premise ledger as a candidate to check (#12). If there
  is reason to suspect it would improve the reading, it is measured (your condition under L2-S49 Ruling 3).
  **Files only; L2's algorithm unchanged.**
- Towards the objective: no reading is lost now; the case where it could matter (jazz ensembles) is where
  it will be examined.
- Towards the principles: #18 (that L2 misreads rootless voicings without instrument knowledge is
  unchecked, and L2's rule differs from the old analyzer's); D-254 (measured when suspected); #12; #6 (the
  jazz questions in one place).
- Meta level: the analysis's own specification.

**Alternative B — RELOCATE claim (i) to *L1 — Change points, candidates and notated evidence*: the note
layer publishes each part's instrument as evidence.**
- What it does: L1 is asked to publish the instrument of each part, and L2 may later weigh it. **No change
  to L2's algorithm now; it adds an obligation to L1's specification.**
- Towards the objective: makes the fact available early. Cost: decides an L1 publication from an L2
  surface, with no premise ledger.
- Towards the principles: the evidence corollary (publish evidence broadly, even without a named consumer)
  supports it; #17 and #18 weigh against deciding it here; whether L1 already publishes the instrument is
  unchecked.
- Meta level: the analysis's own specification (L1's).

**Alternative C — hold claim (i) UNPLACED, travelling with the jazz increment.**
- **Files nothing now; L2's algorithm unchanged.** The same as A in effect, with the row left open until
  that increment is taken up.

**Recommendation: A.** L2-S10 already removes the old analyzer's need for a sounding root, so the
requirement as written no longer applies to L2. Whether instrument roles help is a fair question, and the
jazz increment is where its premise is examined and, if suspected to help, measured. B decides an L1
obligation without a premise; C leaves an open row for the same outcome as A.
