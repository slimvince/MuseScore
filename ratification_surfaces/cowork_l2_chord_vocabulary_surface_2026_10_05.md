- *The reading file* is `ratification_surfaces/cowork_comparison_l2_reading.md`. It puts every sentence of

  DERIVATION IS SILENT, and *proposes* a fate for each row, among them: ADOPTED (carried into L2's specification),

  written for the audit phase), HISTORICAL, or UNPLACED (the comparer could not place it without choosing
  between the two texts). Nothing in it is applied yet.
- *L2* is the layer the ratified charter (`FRAMEWORK.md` §5) calls the one entangled decision: the tonality
  at each moment, where each harmony gives way to the next, which sounding notes belong to the harmony, and
  the chord over each stretch. L2 decides a chord as four fields: *degree* (the scale step of the root
  against the tonality, with any alteration, such as ♭II), *quality*, *figure* (which chord member is in the
  bass, with the seventh or other added members) and *applied target* (for V/V, the degree it is applied to).
- *The admission rule* decides which readings the search may consider at all. *The chord vocabulary* is the
  list of chord kinds that rule ranges over. A *state* here means one of the values the search can choose;
  a *table estimate* is a probability the analysis counts from annotated music for one such value.
- *Ground truth* is the published human harmonic analyses this project grades against. *An elaboration* is a
  sounding note that does not belong to the chord, with its named relation (passing, neighbour, suspension,
  and the others ruled this morning).

## 5. Question 1 — is a rare chord kind its own state, or merged into one pooled class? (a real choice)

**What is being decided.** The derivation fixes the vocabulary by music theory and a test: every chord kind
an annotator writes must be one the search can choose (L2-S2), and the list is at least the standard
common-practice classes (L2-S4). The incumbent fixes it by counting: the chord kinds observed in the
annotated corpus, with every kind seen fewer than twenty times merged into one pooled "rare" class (Row
10.10), which is your ratified capacity rule applied to the state itself (D-271's last sentence). The two
agree on common chords. They part on rare ones. The record shows one case: "the fitted vocabulary collapsed the
family to Italian pitch content (2 corpus tokens)" (Row 49.18, *relayed* from the row), and the output
contract then reads the sub-type back from the sounding notes at the display (Row 49.18(i)). That this
collapse is the pooling rule of Row 10.10 at work, rather than some other step of the fit, is *my reading*;
the row does not name its cause.

**Position one — every chord kind the vocabulary lists is its own state; rarity is handled in the
estimates, not in the vocabulary (L2-S2 and L2-S4 as derived; the recommendation).** A rare kind (the
German sixth, a chain such as V7/IV/III) stays a value the search can choose and publish. Its table
estimates, where its count is below the threshold, borrow from its declared parent class, which is what
D-271's item 2 already does for every table cell; and D-533's rule keeps a rare continuation from ever
scoring as impossible. D-271's last sentence — the same pooling applied to the state space — is not
carried into L2's specification; its register status is settled at the ratification act, not here.

- *Towards the objective (#4).* Every kind on the list, and every kind the closure measurement adds to
  it, is a reading the analysis can choose and publish, however rare. The German sixth and the
  French sixth are told apart by their notes (the German has the lowered third), so the emission can choose
  between them even when their transition estimates are borrowed (*my reading*). The fullest-label rows the
  comparer proposes to carry (5.41 "never a reduced stand-in", 5.47 "never a generic `+6`") become
  reachable rather than restored at the display.
- *Towards the principles.* #12: the merge is a collapse that cannot be got back from what is kept, which is
  why the display has to re-derive the sub-type from the notes; position one removes that. The
  fact-publication corollary ("consumers read, never re-derive"): Row 49.18(i)'s display read is a consumer
  re-deriving a chord fact; under position one the chord is L2's, published once (#6). #20 and D-271's own
  ground (no overfitting on a 326-piece corpus): kept, because the rare kind fits no estimate of its own.
  D-329's principle (complete admission by default; any prune ratified): met.

  state is larger, which adds to the tractability question OQ-L2-8 ★ (its own surface). A rare kind's

- *Meta level:* the analysis's own specification.

**Position two — L2-S2 and L2-S4 stand, but D-271's state-space pooling is kept as a ruled exclusion.** The

## 6. Question 2 — is a sounding ninth, eleventh or added sixth part of the chord, or an elaboration? (a real choice)

**What is being decided.** The dormant chord decoder's design, and the rule in `ARCHITECTURE.md` §2.15,
treat any note above the basic triad or seventh as a chord tone, so the chord is named with it (`C6`,
`Cmaj9`, `G7♭9`; Rows 6.34, 6.140(ii), 21.77). The derivation reads a sounding ninth or eleventh as an
elaboration of a basic chord, as the annotation tradition it read does (`iv7(9)`, `ii%65(119)`, *relayed*),
and leaves an added-sixth class to the measurement. In common-practice music the two mostly coincide in what
is published, because under L2 every sounding note carries its assignment: a ninth published as a
suspension over `iv7` is still published, and a display can write the fuller name from the chord and the
assignments (*my reading*). Where they part is jazz, where a ninth is a member of the chord and does not
resolve. Your ruling of 2026-10-04 says jazz is a core use case and the question is when, not if.

**Position one — the common-practice vocabulary in the first L2 specification, with extended chords written
in as a named increment (the recommendation).** In the first specification a sounding ninth, eleventh or
thirteenth is an elaboration assignment, with its named relation or the unclassified elaboration; whether an
added-sixth class is needed is decided by the closure measurement (OQ-L2-1; the derivation's ledger point
that an added-sixth chord and a seventh chord on the related root share their notes, *relayed*). **The
extended chords that jazz treats as chord members — the ninth, eleventh and thirteenth chords with their altered
forms (♭9, ♯9, ♯11, ♭13), and the power chord D-334 keeps — are written into that specification as a named increment, in
the shape your mode ruling set:** theory-built tables whose source is read on our side (#1), a
premise-ledger entry with the near-neighbour hazard enumerated (a ninth chord against a seventh chord with
a ninth suspension, #17e), a desk simulation (#17c), a grading convention, and held-out jazz ground truth
for the evaluation (#20, D-500). Its trigger is the premise-ledger entry, as for the modes, so it may land
with a declared bound before jazz ground truth exists; that reads D-526's "jazz-specific classes enter only
under the OI-7 jazz-ground-truth gate" as gating the increment's measurement rather than its landing (*my
reading*, which you may rule otherwise).

- *Towards the objective (#4).* On the repertoire the project grades, the chord matches the annotation's
  chord (`iv7`, not `iv9`), and the ninth keeps its relation, which says whether it resolves. Jazz extensions
  get a dated route instead of none.
- *Towards the principles.* #12: no note is dropped; each carries its assignment. #6: one vocabulary, the
  elaboration relations ruled this morning reused rather than a second class for the same note. #17 and #8:
  no extended state built before its premise ledger. #14: the increment is its own ratified change. Your
  2026-10-04 principle: the extensions are sequenced, not excluded for lack of ground truth.
- *The cost.* The first L2 text does not read a jazz `Cmaj9` as one chord; until the increment lands it
  reads `Cmaj7` with the ninth as an elaboration, most likely the unclassified one (*my reading*). Row 21.77's rule is narrowed for L2: L2 states everything it found, but
  as chord plus assignments rather than in the chord's name.
- *Meta level:* the analysis's own specification.

**Position two — added notes are chord members now: the vocabulary is every quality by every root by
whatever added notes membership finds (the incumbent's design, Rows 6.34 and 21.77).**

- *Towards the objective.* Jazz extensions read correctly from the first text. On the graded repertoire, a
  suspended ninth becomes part of the chord's name, against the annotation's chord; and the ninth loses its
  relation (*my reading*).
- *Towards the principles.* #17: extended states enter before any premise ledger. #6: a ninth could then be
  read two ways, as a chord member and as a suspension, each a separate kind of state. Row 21.77's rule kept
  as written.
- *Meta level:* the analysis's own specification.

**Position three — both readings admitted now as rivals: a ninth chord and a seventh chord with a ninth
elaboration.** This is position two with the elaboration reading kept beside it, scored by the decision.

- *Towards the objective.* Neither reading excluded. The extended states enter with tables nobody has
  built or tested.
- *Towards the principles.* #17 as in position two; the state grows by the extended classes (OQ-L2-8).
- *Meta level:* the analysis's own specification.

**Recommendation: position one.** Towards the objective: the graded repertoire is read as its annotators
