## 3. The facts, as the pass established them

**Every figure below was read AT THE OBJECT unless marked otherwise.**

**(a) V12 VERIFIES.** Koops, de Haas, Burgoyne, Bransen & Volk, Utrecht technical report
UU-CS-2017-018, read at the object: the 2017 best MIREX chord-estimation scores on data intersecting
their dual-annotated song set — **.86 / .86 / .83 / .63 / .61** at root / majmin / majmin-inv /
sevenths / sevenths-inv — lie **beyond** their measured annotator-pairwise agreement of
**.76 / .73 / .67 / .60 / .54**. Their own words: *"the state-of-the-art ACE algorithms perform beyond
the 'subjectivity ceiling' found in our dataset"*, and in the abstract, *"by about 10 percent"*.
**Domain: popular-music audio.**

**(b) A second study in the same domain reports the opposite sign on its own data.** Humphrey & Bello
2015 (population row 21, read at both passes and cross-checked): annotator-against-annotator **0.835**
at tetrads, against the two evaluated systems at **0.590** and **0.540**. Asked directly whether the
paper anywhere claims machines match or exceed human agreement: **no**.

**(c) ★ BUT (a) AND (b) DO NOT CONTRADICT EACH OTHER, AND SAYING THEY DO WOULD BE THE SAME ERROR THIS
SURFACE EXISTS TO CORRECT.** They differ in **year** (2015 systems against 2017 systems), in
**corpus** (the Rock Corpus against Koops's dual-annotated set), and in **which systems** were
evaluated. **Systems improving between 2015 and 2017 reconciles both results without either being
wrong.** *No source read by this pass compares them, and this side has not established that they are
in tension — only that the literature does not report ONE uniform finding.*

**(d) The framework already declares the domain bound globally.** **R-1**: *"Every axis-level
agreement figure this document cites comes from popular music or from figured bass."* **So the
framework is not concealing this; #24's requirement that an unestablishable condition be DECLARED as
a bound is already met at R-1.**

**(e) ★ AND D-474's PROHIBITION DOES NOT REACH THIS USE.** D-474 and principle #21 forbid off-domain
agreement figures being used **as a ceiling for this repertoire** — *"they are NOT a ceiling for this
repertoire and may not be used as one, however convenient the invariant they share."* **DP-K does not
use the figure as a ceiling.** It uses it as evidence about **what a single-answer target measures**.
That is a different use, and the prohibition as written does not bite. *This is the second place my
earlier framing was too strong.*

**(f) The pass found a stronger claim in the same paper, which needs no ceiling comparison at all.**
Humphrey & Bello, on "I Saw Her Standing There" across four human references and two systems:
*"no two reference annotations correspond to greater than a 65% agreement, with the DNN and kHMM
scoring 28% and 52% against the ground truth Isophonics reference … Despite this low score, the DNN
and kHMM estimations agree with at least one of the four human annotations **89.1%** and **92.3%** of
the song, respectively."* **A threefold movement in the score produced by nothing but the choice of
what to compare against.** *(The paper reports these numbers and does not itself draw the conclusion;
the inference is the reader's, and travels labelled as such.)*

**(g) And it found the same shape twice on OUR domain — classical symbolic music.**
- **Row 13** (Nápoles López, Feisthauer, Levé & Fujinaga 2020, DLfM) [RELAYED]: the same music yields
  **two defensible onset-level key ground truths** — a modulation column and a tonicization column —
  and annotation traditions differ enormously in how much they tonicize (**41.63%** of onsets in one
  textbook against **15.97%** in another). A dual-annotated dataset is released.
- **Row 16** (the Lille thesis) [DECLARED PARTIAL, chapter-level]: the dual-tonality reading stated as
  a principle — at every moment the score carries two defensible key labels — with the thesis's
  corpus annotating both.

## 4. What the defect actually is, stated precisely

**Not** that ground 2 is false: V12 verifies. **Not** that it is forbidden: (e). **Not** that it is
undeclared: (d).

**The defect is that a finding one study reports is written as a general one.** The framework's
wording — *"the finding that automatic systems already score above human-human agreement on the same
data"* — reads as a settled property of the field. **What the pass established is that the literature
it reached does not report it uniformly**, and the framework's `[FACT]` tag does not distinguish
*"this paper measured this"* from *"this is where the field stands"*.

**This is the same shape as V4, one degree milder.** V4 misstated its primary. **Ground 2 states its
primary correctly and generalises beyond it.** #1 and the theory-grounding corollary reach both:
a load-bearing claim is FACT only as *stated or measured in a paper actually read*.

## 5. The options

Rated per the 2026-08-31 ruling on **both** axes, with the principle behind each pro and con named.

### Option A — Qualify ground 2 in place; add nothing

Narrow the wording to what the primary supports — *one study measured systems scoring above the
annotator agreement it measured, in popular-music audio* — and record beside it that another study in
that domain reports the opposite sign on different data and a different system generation, **without
asserting a contradiction (fact (c))**. Former wording preserved (#12).

- **Objective:** ground 2 stops carrying more weight than its evidence. **No effect on what is built**
  — DP-K is chosen and its shape decision stands on ground 1 regardless. **Indirect value: a
  consequential shape decision (§7) becomes harder to unsettle by a later challenge to an
  over-generalised citation.**
- **Principles:** #1 and the theory-grounding corollary (a FACT is what a read paper states); **#12
  (the second study is information, and carrying it beats dropping it)**; #24 (the bound is declared —
  already met at R-1, and this makes it local as well as global).
- **Cost and risk:** one clause, one added sentence. **Risk: none identified.**

### Option B — Qualify ground 2 *and* add the on-domain findings as further grounds

As A, plus rows 13 and 16 — two defensible ground-truth columns for the same music, in **classical
symbolic** repertoire — and fact (f), as additional recorded grounds for DP-K.

- **Objective:** DP-K would then rest on **three** grounds, two of them **on this project's own
  repertoire and annotation kind** rather than on popular-music audio. **That is the strongest
  available footing for the most consequential shape decision in §7**, and it is the footing the pass
  was commissioned to find.
- **Principles:** #1/#2 (specific research over general — rows 13 and 16 are *this* repertoire);
  #12; #24. **And it answers R-1 locally**: R-1's complaint is that every axis-level agreement figure
  the document cites is off-domain — **B adds on-domain evidence for this point**, which is a partial
  answer to R-1 rather than merely a declaration under it.
- **Cost and risk:** more text in a ratified document, and **it adds content the framework did not
  previously carry — which is your word to give, not a session's**. Row 16 is at DECLARED PARTIAL
  grade (chapter-level, French text) and row 13 is RELAYED; **both bounds must travel with the
  citations**, or B imports the very over-generalisation A removes.

### Option C — Add the on-domain findings; leave ground 2 as written

Additive only: no change to the existing clause.

- **Objective:** the footing improves, but **the over-generalisation stays in the text**, and a later
  reader still meets a general claim the literature does not uniformly support.
- **Principles:** fails #1's point — an unearned generality is not repaired by putting better evidence
  next to it. **#12 is satisfied; #1 is not.**
- **Cost and risk:** same as B's cost, without A's repair.

### Option D — Leave it entirely; R-1 already carries the bound

- **Objective:** nothing changes. **The honest case for D is real and should not be dismissed:** DP-K
  is chosen, ground 1 is on-domain and untouched, R-1 declares the domain bound, and D-474's
  prohibition does not reach this use. **On the objective axis the difference between D and A is
  small.**
- **Principles:** **#10's worth test is the question.** Its (b) limb — a specification that
  misdescribes is not correct and complete — catches an over-generalisation only if you judge that a
  reader would be misled about the analysis. **This side judges yes**, because the clause's own
  concluding words — *"a single-answer target has stopped measuring harmony and started measuring one
  annotator"* — are a strong general claim resting on it.
- **Cost and risk:** nothing spent. **Risk: the pass read this literature precisely to check such
  claims, found this one, and left it as written.**

## 7. Recommendation, with its reason

**Option B — qualify ground 2 in place and add the two on-domain findings, with their grades attached.**

**The reason is what this pass was for.** Ruling 2 of 2026-08-30 commissioned it on your own words —
*"We should act knowledge-based, right? Knowing we have research that is not yet read brakes that
rule"* — and widened it because *"it is the entire architecture as a whole that is evaluated against
the ultimate objective."* **The pass then found, on this project's own repertoire, evidence for a
chosen point that the framework was resting on popular-music audio to support.** Declining to record
it would leave the framework's most consequential shape decision standing on off-domain evidence when
on-domain evidence is now in hand and read.

**A is the safe subset and would be a sound ruling.** It repairs the defect and adds nothing. **The
difference between A and B is whether the pass's on-domain yield reaches the document it was run for.**

**C is declined** on #1: better evidence beside an unearned generality does not repair the generality.
**D is declined**, but on the narrowest ground — not because it is unprincipled, but because #10's (b)
limb is met in this side's judgement, and because leaving a found defect unfixed is the one outcome
that makes the pass's finding worthless.
