**What every item here is.** **Documentation only. No algorithm change.** Each item decides which disposition word, and
which wording, the release writes into the reading file for one old-document passage (a "row"), and which *Open* point
of the dispatch is pointed at the answer. No item changes what L3 computes or publishes. For each item, the method as
currently decided is stated beside it.

**Words used.** A *row* is one passage of the older design documents, numbered in the reading file. A *disposition word*
says what happens to that passage under the new specification: **ADOPTED — proposed** (the new specification takes the
content, and no derived statement already carries it); **ADOPTED — carried (L3-Sn)** (the derived statement L3-Sn
already carries it in substance); **RELOCATED** (it belongs to another layer's or charter's specification);
**HISTORICAL** (decided against or superseded). *As proposed* in a line means the record kept the reading file's own
proposal for that row. *Form (b)* is the dispatch's line for a row a ruling places; *form (d)* is its line for a row a
record places as *"this side's derivation, not a ruling"* (dispatch lines 155–177) [checked].

**Ruling 9's paragraph, in short** (the paragraph's own text governs; part 2 record lines 388–404 [checked]): for a row
a record places ADOPTED, the word is (1) the word the record names in full; else (2) **ADOPTED — carried** with the L3-S
number the record names as carrying the content; else (3) or (4) as the paragraph sets out. *As proposed* is written only
where the word and number are the row's own proposed ones and the record adopts the content as proposed; under the
paragraph, a row proposed UNPLACED does not get *as proposed*. It replaced the list's own rules (i) to (v), which the list's derivations below cite.
