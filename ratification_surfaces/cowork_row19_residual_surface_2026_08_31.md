## 3. The facts

**(a) What the condition is, and how close we are.** Eight central rows: 1, 2, 3, 5, 17, 18, 19, 21.
**Seven carry cross-checked double extracts.** Row 19 carries one. Every other §8 condition is met —
every row read, a verdict per load-bearing figure, the findings surface delivered. **This one row is
the whole of the gap.**

**(b) The commission offers TWO routes to a second pass, not one.** Its own words, above: *"a fresh
session, **or a cleanly separated re-read that does not consult the first extract**."* **So the
condition can be met inside this session** by a reader that is given the paper and not the extract.
That fact is what makes option A cheap enough to be worth putting to you at all; without it, A would
mean another session.

**(c) ★ WHAT THE SEVEN CROSS-CHECKS ACTUALLY CAUGHT, sorted by whether reading at the object would
have prevented it.** This is the load-bearing fact of the whole surface.

| What was caught | Class | Would an at-the-object read be immune? |
|---|---|---|
| **Row 17** — a second-pass claim downgraded from fact to conjecture: the paper does not state whether its window sizes are computed independently | *Does the paper say X?* — the relay tool's measured weak spot | **Largely yes** |

**So: of the five catch-classes measured in this pass, THREE are reader-class, and reading at the

**(d) The single most consequential catch of the whole exercise was an omission.** Row 21's second
pass missed the 89.1% / 92.3% figures; the first pass held them; **those figures are what carries
V12's negative establishment**, one of the pass's load-bearing verification results. **The pair
recovered a load-bearing item that one reader alone had dropped.** That is the strongest single fact
for doubling, and it says nothing about relays.

**(e) What row 19 carries, so the load is not guessed at.** It bears on **DP-O** (read into and left
unmoved, for a sharper reason than "no measurement" — GTTM's one harmony-bearing subtheory, the
prolongational reduction, is stated by its own authors as the one they did not implement, and the

musicological meaning — for **F ≈ 0.49** on monophonic eight-bar excerpts. **This is a load-bearing
row, not a courtesy one.**

**(f) A second extraction of row 19 would be the STRONGEST pair in the pass, not the weakest.** The

**(g) The residual that option A does NOT remove, stated so it is not discovered later.** A separated

worth having is a real question and not a rhetorical one; the seven existing pairs have exactly the
same limitation and were accepted.

**(h) Cost, stated honestly and not minimised.** One reader, thirty pages of page images, plus a
cross-check written against the existing extract. **Comparable to one of the seven second-pass slices
already performed** — those seven took a working session between them. This is roughly a seventh of

## 4. The options, in plain language

### Option A — Do the second extraction now, by the commission's own second route

Give a cleanly separated reader the paper and **not** the existing extract; have it produce an
independent extraction; cross-check the two; resolve any disagreement at the paper or record it
unresolved. **Then §8 is met literally for all eight central rows and there is nothing to rule.**

- **Against the objective (#4).** **The only option that can change what we hold.** What it plausibly
  catches is exactly what the pair caught twice elsewhere — an omission or a mis-attached number — in a
  row that bears on **R-7's discharge**. **Bounded by fact (e):** row 19's contributions are already
  conservative, so this is a check on a load-bearing row, not a hunt for a new finding.
- **Against the principles.** **#5** — the one remaining cheap investigation, and the commission itself
  names the route. **#19** — settles the condition by doing the thing rather than by declaring the
  thing unnecessary. **#12** — whatever it finds is added, nothing is lost. **Nothing weighs against it
  on principle.**
- **Cost and risk.** Fact (h)'s slice. **Risk: none to the record.** The honest limit is fact (g) — it
  buys less independence than the word "independent" suggests, and that limit must be written on the
  cross-check's face.

### Option B — Rule that the at-the-object whole read substitutes, for this row

Declare row 19's single read sufficient because it was taken at the object, whole, and close §8.

- **Against the objective.** **Adds nothing.** If the extract has an omission of the row-21 kind, it
  stays, and it stays in a row that bears on whether R-7 can be discharged. **Nothing detects it
  later** — an extract that misses something looks exactly like an extract with nothing to miss.
- **Against the principles.** **This is the option that rests on the premise §2 just retracted.** With
  that premise gone, the argument for B is: *one careful reading is enough because it was a good
  reading* — which is **#19's exact prohibition**, merely-unfalsified treated as established. **And
  fact (c) is not silent on it: three of the five measured catch-classes are ones an at-the-object
  read does not prevent.**
- **★ And we have a measured price for taking this shape of option, from three hours ago.** Row 7's
  Option B — dissolve row 7 into row 6 on a strong resemblance — was declined on #19 over this side's
  own good lead. **The lead was wrong. Taking it would have dropped a real primary from the
  population, silently.** That is not an argument that B is wrong here; it is a measured instance of
  what this shape of reasoning costs when it fails, **on this project, today.**
- **Cost and risk.** Free, and wrong-if-wrong with no mechanism to find out.

### Option C — Rule the substitution as a STANDING rule: at-the-object whole reads never need doubling

B, generalised to every future pass.

- **Against the objective.** Adds nothing now and **removes a check from every future reading**,
  including future rows where the load is heavier than row 19's.
- **Against the principles.** **Worst of the four.** It installs a standing rule on the strength of a
  premise that §2 retracted and fact (c) partly falsifies. **#18** — it is a causal claim about our own
  reading process with no measurement behind it. **#19** — it makes an unestablished belief permanent.
  **This side declines to recommend it and would ask you not to take it**, on the same footing that
  row 7's B was declined.
- **Cost and risk.** Free now, compounding later, and the least visible of the four when it goes wrong.

### Option D — Record the shortfall as a stated bound and take no position

Leave §8 recorded as **met for seven of eight**, say so plainly in the findings surface's bound where
it already is, and rule nothing about substitution either way.

- **Against the objective.** Adds nothing, **but claims nothing either.** The coverage claim stays
  exactly as honest as the coverage is — which is what the bound section is for (DT-26).
- **Against the principles.** **#12 is satisfied** — a recorded gap is information about the gap, not
  information loss. **#13** — the shortfall stays visible rather than being ruled away. **But #5 is not
  exhausted while option A is available and costs a seventh of a slice.**
- **Cost and risk.** Free. The cost is the foregone check of fact (d)'s kind, on the row of fact (e).

## 6. Recommendation

**Option A. Option D is the sound ruling if you would rather spend the slice elsewhere.**

The reason is fact (d) against §2. **The strongest thing the double pass did all pass was recover a
load-bearing figure that one reader had simply dropped** — not a relay error, an omission — and
**reading at the object is no protection against that.** Row 19 is a load-bearing row on the question
of whether R-7 can be discharged. The commission's own second route makes the check cheap, and by
fact (f) it would be the cleanest pair in the pass.

**D is honest and I would not argue with it.** It costs the check and keeps the record accurate about
what it is; the bound is already written.

**B I now argue against, having myself argued for it three times in this pass's files** — see §2.
**C I decline to recommend at all.**
