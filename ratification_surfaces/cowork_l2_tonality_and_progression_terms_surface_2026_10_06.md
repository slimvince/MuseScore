## §A. The surface

### 1. What this is about

When the chord-and-key layer (L2) weighs one complete reading of a passage against another, it adds up a
score. Some parts of that score look at one stretch of music at a time: how well the notes fit the chord, for
example. The family here is about the parts that look **across neighbouring stretches**: what it costs to
change key, how well a chord sits in its key, and how well one chord follows the one before it.

The derived statement, whole (*checked*, `cowork_blind_derivation_l2_2026_09_27.md` §3b, its lines 844–872):

- **L2-S34:** "The candidate score carries three families of terms relating the four fields across spans.
  (i) Tonality persistence and distance: a cost for a tonality change, graded by the distance between the two
  tonalities in a published tonal space. (ii) The tonality–chord cost, of L2-S3. (iii) Progression: a term on
  the pair of adjacent chords *read as degrees in their tonalities*, including the cadential progressions that
  confirm a tonality. Each family's weights are fitted."
- Its defense, in short. Published systems grade key changes by a tonal-space distance (Lerdahl's tonal pitch
  space; Weber's table). The progression term reads scale degrees, not root intervals, because "a V-I
  transition has a different distribution than a I-IV transition, even though the root distance is the same"
  (Masada & Bunescu). A reward for the previous root carrying on "suppresses wrong root changes and correct ones
  alike". A V→I detector used on its own as key evidence had low precision. Hence: "confirmation is carried by
  the progression term over *proposed* chords, not by a separate detector".
- Status: "Settled in shape. **Open** in value and in the choice of tonal space (OQ-L2-3)". OQ-L2-3 is the
  derivation's list of measured shapes and values, among them "the choice between Lerdahl's and Weber's tonal
  spaces", all to be measured on held-out music (*checked*, line 1210).
- The two neighbouring statements this surface relies on (*checked*). **L2-S38:** every weight is fitted from
  annotated music, never set by hand. **L2-S39:** a distance from published theory enters the score as one
  feature with one fitted weight, and its internal constants are left exactly as published.

#### 4.4 The first chord after a change of key (3 parts)

new key, given the new key alone, "fit from the ground truth's modulation points" (Rows 10.14(vii), 10.30).

**L2-S34:** the progression term is on the pair of adjacent chords "*read as degrees in their tonalities*".
Across a change of key, the chord before is read in the old key and the chord after in the new one.

**What follows.**

- An entry factor on the new key alone loses what the old key says about the first chord. Its own premise
  table records that loss (#12, no information loss).

  (*checked*).
- L2-S34's pair across the change already reads the old key. That is the "pivot-aware" remedy the old text
  itself names (my reading).
- Whether a separate term on the new chord given the new key adds anything beyond the pair term is
  measurable on held-out music, so it is measured, not asked (D-254).

**Proposed placement:** ADOPTED — proposed, Rows 10.14(vii), 10.30, 10.43. **The proposal:**

- The progression term covers the pair across a change of key, each chord read in its own key.
- A separate entry term is one more held-out comparison, added to OQ-L2-3's list.

**Towards the objective:** the old key's information about the first new chord is kept. **Towards the
principles:** #12, #17(a) (the outgoing text's own labelled assumption and falsifier), D-254. **Meta
level:** the analysis's own specification. **Cost:** chord pairs across a change of key are rarer than pairs
within one key, so their counts are thin. D-533's proportional share is what covers that (my reading).

**Recommendation:** confirm.
