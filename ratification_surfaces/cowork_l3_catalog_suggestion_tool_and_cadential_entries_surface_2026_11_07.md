## 4. Part A.2 — the tool's own working: ONE DECISION for the user

**What is to be decided, from scratch.** The old design described not only that a chord-suggestion tool might share
the catalog, but also how that tool would work. Seven items say this:

- 38.81 (ii): knowledge of longer progressions would be core to generating chords;
- 38.82, second part, and 57.107 (ii), predictive half: the tool reads the catalog *predictively*;
- 57.445 (ii) and 57.433 (ii)'s second half: the tool reads a stretch of chords to *suggest* what could follow, come
  before or replace it;
- 46.27, composing half: the tool *offers* a substitution;
- 46.37: every entry is usable both for analysis and for composition, and new entries are added *"with both readings in
  mind"*.

None of the five dispositions fits them cleanly. RELOCATED points only to one of the four layer charters, and the tool
belongs to none. ADOPTED would add the tool's design to the analysis specification. HISTORICAL fits a *superseded* plan,
and this sitting found no ruling that supersedes this one; key Ruling 1 lists the tool among the consumers the
amendment is to name. QUARANTINED is for how the implementation works, which these items do not state. DISCARDED needs
a finding under the worth test, and no such finding is on the record found by this sitting. Under D-599 the record
does not settle the question, so it comes to the user.

**Whatever is chosen: documentation only. No algorithm change.** The method that stands: L2 decides key and chord
together, its progression term a table of chord-to-chord pairs counted once from annotated music; L3 publishes the
cadence at each phrase end with its type, the phrase and section grouping, the chord symbol, the figured bass, the
harmonic rhythm and, once the application act amends `FRAMEWORK.md`, the catalog matches (key Ruling 1). The catalog is
reference knowledge beside the layers. Nothing on L3's *Publishes* line, as the key record quotes it and as key Ruling 1
extends it, suggests chords.

**Alternative A — HISTORICAL.** The seven items are recorded as a plan of the old design. Nothing of the tool's working
enters the L3 specification. The old texts and the reading file keep the words.
- *Towards the objective:* no effect on the analysis.
- *Towards the principles:* #6 (the analysis specification holds only analysis). Against: the label does not fit its
  definition; a later reader would take *historical* to mean given up, of a tool key Ruling 1 lists among the consumers
  the amendment is to name; the label would not say where the rows went.

**Alternative B — ADOPTED — proposed, as dormant notes.** The L3 specification records how the future tool would use
the catalog, and that entries are curated for both readings.
- *Towards the objective:* no effect on the analysis.
- *Towards the principles:* #12 (nothing lost; the ideas sit next to the catalog they concern). Against #6: product
  design would live in the analysis specification; and Row 46.37's curation rule would bind the catalog L3 reads to the
  needs of a tool not yet specified.

**Alternative C — placed outside every charter, carried for the tool (the writing side's recommendation).** The user
places the seven items with this placement: *outside the analysis — the design of a future chord-suggestion tool,
carried for that tool's own specification*. The writing side proposes **[my reading]** that the application act
records them in one list in the amended L3 specification's notes, beside the boundary statement of Part A.1, without
adding them to the specification itself.
- *Towards the objective:* no effect on the analysis.
- *Towards the principles:* #6 (the tool's design kept out of the analysis specification, findable for its own); #12
  (nothing lost); the label says where the rows went; the UNPLACED definition (*"a row the user places"*). Against:
  it is a placement outside the five dispositions, so the application act carries one extra list. Family 7 (a language
  model reading the analysis) may meet the same question; C would be a precedent there **[my reading]**.

**Why C is recommended [my reading]:** A stretches HISTORICAL past its definition; B puts product design into the
analysis specification; C keeps the five definitions as they are and says plainly where the rows went. The cost is one
list.
