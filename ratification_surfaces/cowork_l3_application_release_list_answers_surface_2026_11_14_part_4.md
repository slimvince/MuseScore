**Words used.** A *row* is one passage of the older design documents, numbered in the reading file; a row with several
claims is split into parts (i), (ii) and so on. A *disposition word* says what happens to that passage under the new
specification. **UNPLACED** is the reading file's honest default, the user placing the row later (as the Part 3 surface
relays it). **HISTORICAL** is given by the reading file's §5 to what is *"not a rule"* (as the list relays §5, line 753
[checked at the list]). **ADOPTED — carried (L3-S*n*)** is the word for content an existing derived statement, numbered
L3-S*n*, already carries; **ADOPTED — proposed** is the word for content the specification takes in that no numbered
statement carries [this side's description, from the list's grounds at lines 730 and 792–793]. *As proposed* is a
qualifier a line adds where the record adopts the content as proposed (O18, line 749 [checked]). *STOP-and-report* is the
dispatch's instruction to Claude Code to stop at a row and report it rather than write anything [this side's
description, as in Parts 2 and 3].

**Ruling 9's paragraph, as O18 relays it.** It opens with a STOP that, O18 records, does not fire for a row with a
*PROPOSED DISPOSITION.* paragraph (each row here has one). Its (1): where the record names the word, that word. Its (2):
where the record names an L3-S number as carrying the content, ADOPTED — carried with that number. Its (3): an added
statement has no L3-S number and is not a derived statement; its STOP does not fire where the reading file's axis names
no derived statement. Its (4): the row's own ADOPTED word; the axis, as (4) reads it, gives the same. It writes *as
proposed* only where the record adopts the content as proposed; **a row-part proposed UNPLACED gets no *as proposed***
(entry 872 §2 item 2 attributes this to the paragraph's last sentence) [all relayed from O18, lines 744–750, 788–796
