## §A.1 The statement, whole, and what it means

From `cowork_blind_derivation_l2_2026_09_27.md`, its lines 397–422 (*checked*):

> **L2-S14. Two adjacent spans never carry the same tonality and the same chord (degree, quality,
> figure and applied target all equal). A change of figure alone, such as V6 to V65, is a change of
> chord and may be a boundary. A change in which notes are elaborations, with the chord unchanged, is
> not a boundary.**
> - *Defense.* The published enumerate-then-filter system discards any interpretation in which "the
>   same chord is identified in two successive slices", because another sub-segmentation covers it [FACT
>   (relayed, 08) — Condit-Schultz, Ju & Fujinaga, §3.2.2, step 7]. Allowing two identical adjacent spans
>   would make one musical reading appear as several rivals, which spreads its mass and breaks L2-S40.
>   Figure is a field of the charter's chord [RULED — charter], and the two published readings of
>   exemplar 1's piece disagree at bar 4 on exactly this. One writes `V6 … V65 … I` and the other
>   `V6/5 b3 I` [FACT at the files — exemplars 1 and 2]. So a figure-only boundary is a real rival. The
>   elaboration clause follows from the charter placing elaborations on events, not in the chord
>   (L2-S1). **A consequence recorded rather than hidden:** exemplar 3's analysis writes a new label
>   where a suspension resolves (`V7(4)/iv` then `V7/iv`). In L2's publication that is one span with a
>   suspension assignment, so boundary grading against that annotation must map one onto the other
>   (OQ-L2-7).
> - *Source class.* Derived.
> - *Status.* Settled.
> - *Premise.* A phrase boundary with a restated chord is L3's to publish (DP-J), so L2 loses nothing by
>   merging. **False-negative path:** a restatement an analyst writes as a new harmonic event, the
>   `|| b4 I` shape. L2 merges it and L3's phrase grouping carries the division. If the measurement layer
>   grades L2's boundaries against the annotation's label positions, it will count this as a miss
>   (OQ-L2-7).
> - *Falsifier.* CODE. **Observable:** consecutive spans. **Decision rule:** falsified if two adjacent
>   published spans are identical in all five fields, or if a figure change is inadmissible as a
>   boundary. **Not falsified by:** adjacent spans equal in chord and different in tonality.

**In plain words.** L2 cuts the music into *spans* — stretches over each of which one chord is read, in one
tonality. L2-S14 says three things about where one span may end and the next begin:

1. **Two neighbouring spans are never the same reading.** If the tonality, the scale degree, the chord's quality,
   its inversion ("figure") and any applied target (the "of V" in V/V) are all the same on both sides, there is no
   boundary there: it is one span. So a span in L2 is always the longest run of one reading — there is nothing left
   for a later step to "merge".
2. **A change of inversion alone is a change of chord.** V6 followed by V6/5 over the same bass motion may be two
   spans. The two published analyses of one exemplar piece disagree on exactly this, so both readings must be
   available as rivals.
3. **A change only in which notes are passing notes, suspensions and the like is not a change of chord.** Those
   notes get an *assignment* (chord tone, or an elaboration with its relation — L2-S1, L2-S9), not a new span.

The reason given is about the inference, not about display: if two identical neighbouring spans were allowed, the
same musical reading could be written in several ways (one span, or two equal halves, or three thirds …), each would
be a separate rival in the search, and the probability weight ("mass", L2-S40) of that one reading would be split
among them.

**What L2-S14 does not do.** It does not decide how an annotator's extra labels are graded — a new label where a
suspension resolves (`V7(4)/iv` then `V7/iv`), or the same chord written again after a phrase end (`|| b4 I`).
Both are recorded for the measurement-design stage as OQ-L2-7 (lines 1223–1227, *checked*).

## §A.7 The method that stands — unchanged by the confirmation

Quoted or summarised from the derivation, *checked* this sitting:
- **L2-S14:** two adjacent spans never carry the same tonality and the same chord (degree, quality, figure, applied
  target); a figure-only change is a change of chord and may be a boundary; a change only in which notes are
  elaborations is not a boundary.
- **L2-S1 and L2-S9:** a reading is an ordered partition into harmonic spans, each with a tonality and a chord, and
  per event per span an assignment (chord tone, or elaboration with its relation); an event that crosses a boundary
  has one assignment in each span.
- **L2-S27:** the chord is decided as degree, quality, figure and applied target against the span's tonality; the
  chord symbol (root, quality, bass) is L3's read-off.
- **L2-S31:** span terms read the evidence at and inside the span; pair terms read both spans' fields and the
  evidence at their shared boundary.
- **L2-S40:** a span-rival's mass is the total mass of the whole readings that contain it.
- **L2-S13** (placed earlier today): notated marks are fitted terms; no mark forces or forbids a boundary.
