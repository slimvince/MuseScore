## 1. What the user is being asked, and what the two verdicts mean

**One verdict per ruling**, and for every ruling proposed as a DECISION, one proposed home.

- **DECISION** — the ruling establishes something that BINDS A FUTURE SESSION beyond the act it
  licensed: a standing rule, a method, a status change, or a commissioning. The register carries
  it, and rule (e) puts its text in the owning specification with the register pointing.
- **EXERCISE** — the ruling APPLIES a decision the register already holds to one occasion: a
  scoped licence to perform an act the record already requires, a timing choice a row reserved to
  the user, a status flip performed under the register's own rules. Nothing new binds; the ruling
  is fully recorded in the provenance of the act it licensed, which is where every one of them
  already is.

**The distinction is not about importance.** Several EXERCISE rulings below unblocked more work
than several DECISION ones. It is about whether a session that reads only the register would be
missing a rule.

**A verdict the user disagrees with costs one line here** — no entry has been written, so nothing
has to be unwritten.

**Where a DECISION's proposed home is a section of a document rather than a layer specification**,
that is rule (e)'s *"wherever possible"* doing its work: a rule about how the record itself is kept
has no owning LAYER, and every comparable entry the register already holds is homed in a section of
`cowork_audit_protocol.md`'s dispatch-protocol block or in `CLAUDE.md`'s own governing text. Where
the proposed home is `CLAUDE.md`, **only the user writes it** — that is flagged on each such row.
