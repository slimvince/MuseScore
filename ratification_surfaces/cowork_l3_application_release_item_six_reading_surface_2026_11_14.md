**Words used.**
- A *row* is one passage of the older design documents, numbered in the reading file (Row 38.43, say). A row may be
  split into *claims*, marked (i), (ii) and so on.
- The *transfer list* (*§10*) is the reading file's list of rows proposed to move to another layer's text. Its entries
  name rows, and often name only some claims of a row (*"Row 19.38 (i), (ii) and (iv)"*).
- A *ruled line* is the line the dispatch writes at a row in the reading file, saying how a ruling placed it (form (b),
  dispatch lines 155–160). Where a ruling places only one claim of a split row, *"the line names that claim"* (line 160
  [checked]).
- A *LEAVES L3* line is the line Task 3 (c) item 6 adds beneath a transfer-list entry, keeping the entry, to record that
  a ruling took that row off the transfer list (dispatch lines 5210–5216 [checked]).

## The question, in plain terms

Item 6 says (dispatch lines 5212–5215 [checked]): *"Beneath each §10 entry whose row carries a ruled line from one of
those four records under (a), ONE line is ADDED … Where one §10 entry lists several rows, the line names the rows it
covers."*

When a ruling places claim (iii) of a row, and the transfer list's entry for that row names only claim (i):
- **Read by row:** the entry's *row* carries a ruled line, so item 6 adds a *LEAVES L3* line beneath it. The line then
  says that a ruling took claim (i) off the transfer list, though no ruling placed claim (i).
- **Read by claim:** the entry names claim (i), and claim (i) carries no ruled line, so item 6 adds nothing there. Claim
  (i) stays on the transfer list, proposed as before.

## The derived answer: by claim

**Item 6 acts by claim:** a *LEAVES L3* line goes beneath a transfer-list entry only where a claim the entry names
carries a ruled line from one of the four records, and the line names the claims it covers. An entry naming only claims
no ruling placed gets no line. Where an entry names a row whole and a ruling places one claim of it, the line names that
claim; this follows form (b)'s rule for ruled lines (line 160) by analogy and is this side's derivation. Whether such an
entry exists among the eight above or elsewhere was not checked.

**Grounds, under D-599** (the standing principles first; `DECISIONS.md` line 714, LIVE [checked]):
1. **A line must not say more than the ruling it names.** Read by row, the line beneath the line-79311 entry would say
   that claims (i), (ii) and (iv) of Row 19.38 leave the transfer list, when the ruling placed claim (v) only. Ruling 9
   was put with the dispatch's rule that the record governs where its words differ (third Part B record lines 69–70
   [checked]).
2. **The ruled item next to it already settles the same case by claim.** Item 7, added after item 6 by Part 2 Ruling 7
   on the user's word "A", adds its line beneath each §11 entry *"whose row-part"* a §L entry places otherwise, and says
   *"A §11 entry naming another claim of the row is not touched"* (part 2 record lines 293–296 [checked]). §11 is the
   reading file's audit list, the transfer list's neighbour.
3. **The dispatch works by claim at its neighbouring rules.** Form (b) has a ruled line name the one claim a ruling places
   (line 160 [checked]); item 5 speaks of a *"row-part"* (line 5205 [checked]). Among items 5 to 7, item 6's *"row"* is
   the odd one out.

Both readings delete and edit no entry, so #12 does not decide between them.

**What weighs the other way.** Item 6 says *"row"*, and Ruling 9 ratified it *"as written"*. The records item 6 quotes
speak of rows leaving *"row by row"* (dispatch lines 5210–5212 [checked], quoting §L.21 and §L.24; the records
[relayed]). Read by row, the act would follow item 6's word literally. This side's reading: *"row by row"* in those
records says that the rows leave one by one rather than as a block, and does not say that a claim no ruling placed
leaves with its row. The records were not opened to confirm this.

**This side's reading under D-599:** the grounds above decide it, so no genuine choice is left. What remains is whether
the user ratifies the derivation.
