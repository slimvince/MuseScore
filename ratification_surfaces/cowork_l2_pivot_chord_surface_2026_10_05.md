## 2. The derived statement

**L2-S19**, quoted (*checked*): "A pivot chord, read by an analyst in two tonalities at once, is published
as one span with one tonality. The other tonality's reading of the same span is published as a rival with
its mass. Whether L2 should instead be able to publish a span as a pivot, carrying both tonalities in the
principal reading, is a question for the user (OQ-L2-5 ★)."

- *Its defence.* The charter publishes "per span, the tonality", in the singular. The exemplar analyses the
  deriving session read write pivots: exemplar 2 at bar 41 writes `N6 F: IV6` (one chord, a Neapolitan in
  A minor and IV6 in F), and exemplar 5 writes `V2/IV F: V2` at bar 1 and `VII C: I` at bar 29. Exemplar 1,
  which is the other published analysis of exemplar 2's piece, writes the same bar 41 as `IV.IV6`: a change
  to F at that chord, with no pivot. "So one analyst writes a pivot and the other does not." It adds: rivals
  express "either" and cannot express "both at once"; the pivot is a real theoretical claim; and "the
  charter's singular may not have been meant to exclude it". *(The exemplar facts are relayed from the
  derivation; I did not open the exemplars.)*
- *Its status.* Open (OQ-L2-5 ★).
- *Its premise and how it could fail.* "Rivals suffice." It fails for "a consumer (L3's cadence reading, or a
  display for the user) that needs the pivot as one fact. It would see two readings splitting the mass and
  no statement that they hold together."
- *Its falsifier.* "RESIDUAL until ruled."

**OQ-L2-5 ★**, quoted (*checked*): "may L2 publish a span as a pivot carrying two tonalities in its
principal reading, or is the charter's 'the tonality' per span singular, so that a pivot is expressed only
as two rivals?"

## 5. The question — may L2's principal reading carry two tonalities on one span? (a real choice)

**What is being decided.** Whether L2's specification keeps one tonality per span and expresses a pivot as
two rivals (the change before the chord, the change after it), or lets one span carry two tonalities in the
principal reading.

**Position one — one tonality per span; a pivot is two rivals with their mass (L2-S19 as derived; the
recommendation).** OQ-L2-5 is answered: the charter's singular stands. The span is published in one
tonality, the other reading of it as a rival with its mass. The label "a chord in both tonalities", where a
consumer wants it, is the decided chord re-expressed in the neighbouring span's tonality; where that label
is published (at L3, or as a display view) is **not decided here** and is carried to L3's own specification.

- *Towards the objective (#4).* The two published analyses of exemplar 2's piece disagree on whether bar 41
  is a pivot (*relayed*). So whether to write a pivot is partly a habit of the annotating tradition, and the
  rivals with their masses carry what the music supports: that the change can fall on either side of that
  chord. Nothing about the chord itself is lost, because its degree and quality are published in one
  tonality and its reading in the other is a fixed re-expression (my reading).
- *Towards the principles.* #12: the second label is recomputable from what L2 publishes (the chord, and
  the tonality of the next or previous span), so not publishing it at L2 is not a loss under #12's
  recomputable clause (my reading). #6 and the charter's own chord-symbol reasoning: a derivable label is a
  view, not a second decision. No charter amendment.
- *The cost.* The derivation's premise failure stands: a consumer that wants "this chord is a hinge" as one
  fact must compute it, and the masses of the two rivals express "either", not "both" (*checked*, L2-S19's
  premise). Which chord an analyst calls the pivot is a judgment, and a fixed rule may not reproduce it: the
  old detector's rule, "diatonic to both", would not pick exemplar 2's Neapolitan, which is chromatic in
  A minor (my reading, from the relayed label and standard theory).
- *Meta level:* the analysis's own specification.

**Position two — a span may be a pivot, carrying two tonalities in the principal reading.** L2-S19 is
rewritten; L2-S16 gains an exception ("exactly one tonality, except a pivot span"); the charter's three
singular sentences (L2's publication list, §3.2, §7) are amended at the ratification act.

- *Towards the objective (#4).* The principal reading then matches literally the traditions that write
  pivots (exemplars 2 and 5, *relayed*), and a consumer gets the hinge as one fact.
- *Towards the principles.* The pivot reading becomes a third kind of reading in the search, beside "change
  before" and "change after", and L2-S38 requires every weight to be fitted from annotated music. Only some
  annotations write pivots, and the census records no public deposit of one pivot source (*relayed*), so its
  weight would be learned from one tradition's labelling habit (my reading; #21). Against #6: the
  second tonality's label is a re-expression of the same chord, so publishing it as part of L2's decision
  makes a second home for a derivable fact, which is the reason the charter gives for making the chord
  symbol a view (my reading).
- *The cost.* Three charter sentences and two derived statements amended; a new reading type to score, fit
  and grade; Row 43.94(ii) would then agree and be carried rather than historical.
- *Meta level:* the analysis's own specification and its charter.

**Position three — position one at L2, and L3's charter list gains the pivot as a read-off fact now.** L2 as
in position one; L3 is told, in this ruling, that it publishes the pivot label.

- *Towards the objective.* A consumer is guaranteed the hinge as one fact, without L2 deciding it.
- *Towards the principles.* It amends L3's charter from an L2 surface, before L3's own derivation has been
  written or compared (my reading: an act out of the route's order). The rule that would pick
  the pivot chord is not derived anywhere on the record.
- *Meta level:* the analysis's specification, one layer downstream.

**Recommendation: position one.** Towards the objective, the annotators' own disagreement on bar 41 says the
rival pair with its mass is the honest publication, and nothing about the chord is lost. Towards the
principles, it keeps one home for one fact (#6) and loses nothing (#12), with no charter amendment; and it
leaves the pivot label to the layer that reads facts off a settled reading, where L3's own specification
can decide it with the evidence in front of it. The cost is that "both at once" is not one published L2
fact.

*If position one is ruled:* L2-S19 stands as derived; its status closes; its falsifier, "RESIDUAL until
ruled", becomes the code falsifier L2-S16 already states ("falsified if two tonalities are published for
one span as its principal reading", *checked*), which is my reading for the application act. Row 43.94(ii)
goes HISTORICAL as proposed. The question of where a pivot label is published is carried, unsettled, to
L3's specification.
