**The currently decided method for face (a), in plain words.** A reading of the passage splits it into harmonic
spans, each starting at the passage's edge or where the sounding notes change; any set of such points may be
the boundaries, and no habit of harmonic rhythm is a rule. Each span gets a tonality — a tonic spelled with one
of seven letters, natural, sharp or flat, in major or composite minor (42 tonalities) — and a chord: a degree
with any alteration, its quality, its inversion, and a chain of applied targets such as V7/IV/III. Every note
sounding in a span is either a chord tone or an elaboration of one of eight kinds: passing, neighbour,
suspension (an upward-resolving one included), anticipation, appoggiatura, escape tone, pedal point, or
unclassified; a note held across a boundary gets one such assignment in each span. A note is a chord tone only
if its spelled pitch is a spelled member of the chord, and a chord may be read without its root or third
sounding. The search weighs whole readings, never span by span. No chord is ruled out under any tonality — a
foreign one costs more. The vocabulary holds every triad and seventh-chord quality on every degree, the
Neapolitan and the three augmented sixths, each its own choosable kind however rare, a rare kind borrowing its
estimates from its parent class. Ninths, elevenths and thirteenths are elaborations, not chord members. Every
reading an analyst writes must be reachable unless a ruling excludes it by name with its cost; two are
excluded so far — the cadential six-four as its own category, and "no chord" over sounding music. Still open,
for the closure measurement OQ-L2-1: whether the vocabulary is complete, how deep an applied chain may go,
tonics with double accidentals, and an enharmonic allowance for chord tones. Church modes and jazz extended
chords enter later, each as a named increment through its own premise gate.
