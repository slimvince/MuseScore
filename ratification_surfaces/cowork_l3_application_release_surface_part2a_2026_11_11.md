## Question A5 — Items a ruling places outside every charter on purpose: what is written beside the row?

### What the question is

Several of the user's L3 rulings placed some rows *outside every charter* on purpose: the content is not the analysis's
business at all, but belongs to the specification of something else (a future chord-suggestion tool, a display, the
application, the build, the framework). Such a ruling gives the row none of the five disposition words, because none
fits. The question is what Claude Code writes beside such a row: nothing at all, or a line of some other form.

### Where it stands in the record

The working list sorted every site this question was found at, one record at a time, and found at each that the record
settles it **[relayed: working list, A5 heading, lines 908–912, and items (xxxii) to (xxxvi), lines 921–1161, read by
this sitting]**. The sites and their records:

- the suggestion-tool and cadential-entries record, seven items (item (xxxii));
- the display-family record, thirteen items, among them Rows 4.6, 4.18 and 4.19 (item (xxxiii));
- the code-interfaces record, twelve items (item (xxxiv));
- the modal-counts record, Row 57.373 part (i) (item (xxxv));
- the decomposition-rules record, nine row-parts (item (xxxvi)).

The derivation is the same at each site, each tested at its own record:

1. The reading file's §5 defines UNPLACED as *"the honest default and it is NOT a sixth disposition — it is a row the
   user places"* [relayed: working list (xxxiii), quoting the reading file lines 395–396]. The ruling is that placement.
   So writing nothing would leave the row marked as still waiting on the user after he has placed it.
2. The ruling gives no disposition word on purpose (at each site the surface weighed the five words and found none fits,
   or the user declined the word offered). So the ordinary line form, which needs a word, cannot be used.
3. The dispatch already has a ruled line with no disposition word, form (c): *"No disposition word is written, and the
   row is not counted in the ruled distribution"* (dispatch line 171) [relayed: working list (xxxii)]. The new line
   takes that shape.

### The line, as the writing side worded it (the working list's item (xxxii))

One line beneath the row's `*PROPOSED DISPOSITION.*` paragraph:

> `*RULED PLACEMENT (<record file>, Ruling N).* Outside every charter — <the record's placement in its words>; listed in
> the successor's notes.`

It names the part, where the ruling places a part only. No disposition word is written; the row is not counted in the
ruled distribution; the report lists it as placed outside every charter. **This wording is the writing side's**, as the
dispatch's other line forms (a) to (e) are, and the user may overrule it before Task 0 [relayed: working list (xxxii)].

### What Ruling 3 adds

Ruling 3 (part 1's Question 3, the user's word "ratified") placed the lead-sheet, Nashville and Roman-numeral items of
Row 4.17 *"as Rows 4.18 and 4.19 are: outside every charter, carried for a display's own specification"*, and said that
until A5 is ruled they get no line [checked: the ruling record, lines 115–118]. The working list's item (xxxiii), written
before Ruling 3, gives Rows 4.18 and 4.19 the line above, with the placement *"Outside every charter, carried for a
display's own specification"*. Applying D-599 first (the standing principles first; only what they leave is the user's
choice): the three items of Row 4.17 now carry the same placement by ruling, so they take the same line, naming the
three items as the part placed. **[This sitting's derivation from Ruling 3 and item (xxxiii).]** The working list's
sentence that Row 4.17's items *"stay a question at (xvii)"* (line 912) is overtaken by Ruling 3.

### The currently decided method

Unchanged. L2 decides key and chord together and publishes the chord as a degree in its tonality; L3 publishes facts
read off it; L3 publishes no tonic / subdominant / dominant summary and writes nothing into the score; *"a display turns
published facts into text"* [relayed: working list (xxxiii), quoting the display-family record lines 47–51]. The
placements say only that certain old statements belong to the specification of something outside the analysis.

### The alternatives, weighed towards the ultimate objective and the principles

- **A — Ratify the derivation and the line as worded.** Every row a ruling placed outside every charter gets the line
  above, Row 4.17's three items included. *Towards the objective:* neutral; the analysis is untouched. *Towards the
  principles:* each placement is written once, at its row, in the ruling's own words; no row is left marked as waiting
  on the user after he has placed it; no word is invented that the user did not give.
- **B — Ratify the derivation, but change the line's wording.** The same rows get a line, in words the user gives. Only
  needed if something in the wording above reads wrongly to him.
- **C — Write nothing beside these rows; the notes list in the amended specification is their only place.** The working
  list weighed this and did not take it: the row would keep an UNPLACED that by §5's own definition still waits on the
  user, and the reading file would not say where the row went. *Towards the principles:* this leaves the record saying
  something false about these rows.

**The record already settles the substance (A or B); C goes against §5's definition.** What is the user's is whether
the wording above stands.

## Question A6 — RELOCATED to a destination that is not one of the six framework places

### What the question is

RELOCATED means *"the content belongs to another layer or area"*, and the reading file's §5 says the row names that
place *"in `FRAMEWORK.md` §5's own words"* [checked: part 1's background, quoting reading file lines 382–396]. The six
places are L0, L1, L2, the second axis (voice leading), the uncertainty surface, and the measurement of the analysis
[checked: part 1, lines 42–44]. The question is whether a RELOCATED line may name a home that is not one of the six.

### Where it stands in the record

The working list sorted the four sites as derivations [relayed: working list, A6 heading, lines 1163–1169, and items
(xxxvii) to (xl), lines 1176–1352, read by this sitting]:

- **Item (xxxvii) — seven row-parts of member 23 (Rows 23.30 (i), 23.31, 23.36 (i), 23.37, 23.39, 23.40, 23.41).** §5
  alone does not admit the home. But the presets-scope record's Ruling 1 (the user's word "yes") gives RELOCATED and
  stretches it in terms: *"RELOCATED — outside the framework, to `ARCHITECTURE.md` §6.8, the user-facing preset layer,
  recorded and deferred product work"*, and *"Recorded beside the rows … RELOCATED stretched to a home outside the
  framework; the transfer list's entry "outside the framework — the style system""* [relayed: working list (xxxvii)].
  The user declined, for these rows, the label *"outside every charter"*. So the line carries the stretch in the
  record's words.
- **Item (xxxviii) — Row 40.12 part (iv), tempo and character markings as style hints.** The phrase-end-evidence record
  places it *"RELOCATED — outside the framework, to `ARCHITECTURE.md` §6.8"*, and its surface, ratified with it, records
  the stretch *"as family 6 records part 2A"* [relayed: working list (xxxviii)].
- **Items (xxxix) and (xl) — voice-leading parts of Rows 57.118, 53.7, 53.12, 53.21.** Their destination, the second
  axis, is one of the six, so the question does not arise for them.

So the record as it stands: **the user has twice ratified RELOCATED to a home outside the six**, each time with the home
named (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §6.8) and the stretch recorded beside the row. Applying D-599 first, the general answer to A6
follows from those two rulings: yes, where a ruling gives the word, names the home, and the stretch is written beside
the row. **[The working list's derivation; the two rulings themselves relayed, not opened by this sitting.]**

### What Ruling 2 adds — the part that is not settled

The working list's A6 heading says A6 leaves no question of its own. It was written on 2026-11-10, before Ruling 2.
Ruling 2 (part 1's Question 2, the user's word *"B, falling back to A if A6 does not admit display destinations"*)
reads [checked: the ruling record, lines 57–60]:

> The remainder of Row 3.5 (iii) is RELOCATED to a display destination. If the ruling on A6 (whether RELOCATED may name
> a destination that is not a framework layer) does not admit display destinations, the remainder is instead RELOCATED
> to L2, naming L2-S27. The line is written when A6 is ruled; until then the remainder gets no line and is listed in the
> report.

The remainder of Row 3.5 (iii) is, in plain words, the old design's idea of showing a chord's Roman numeral relative to
an enclosing key area rather than only to its own local key; L2's degree stands whatever a display shows [checked: the
ruling record, lines 46–49, quoting the L2 chord-as-degree record].

**By the derivation above, A6 admits a display destination**, so Ruling 2's fallback to L2 does not come into play and
its first branch (B) stands. **But B's line cannot yet be written, because no home is named.** The two rulings A6 rests
on each named a home (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §6.8). Ruling 2 says only *"a display destination"*.

This sitting looked for a named display home [checked]:

- `ARCHITECTURE.md` has no section for displaying analysis results in the way this remainder needs, at the headings
  containing "display" or "presentation" (a heading search only): §2.3 *"Analysis Layer Is Display-Agnostic"* is a
  rule for the code (*"Analysis components produce structured data — they never produce display strings"*); §12.1a
  *"Harmonic Analysis Display Preference"* is the on/off preference for the status bar; the "UI Presentation" heading
  near line 6856 is the intonation preference's radio buttons.
- The L3 rulings already keep one place for display matters: the **display list** in the amended L3 specification's
  notes, *"carried for a display's own specification"* (the display-family record's Ruling 1, record lines 37–39
  [relayed: working list (xxxiii)]), which the code-interfaces record (§2B) and the modal-counts record also add to
  [relayed: working list (xxxiv), (xxxv)].

### The currently decided method

Unchanged. A key area closes at every change of tonality in L2's principal reading; each span's governing key is its
own tonality in L2's reading, and a Roman numeral is L2's degree against it [checked: the ruling record, lines 63–65].
None of the alternatives below changes that; they decide only where the old display idea is recorded.

### The alternatives, weighed towards the ultimate objective and the principles

- **A — RELOCATED to the display list.** The line, on the model of item (xxxvii)'s:
  `*RULED DISPOSITION (cowork_rulings_2026_11_11_l3_application_release_part1_sitting.md, Ruling 2).* **RELOCATED —
  outside the framework** — to a display's own specification, listed with the display list in the amended L3
  specification's notes; recorded beside the row: RELOCATED stretched to a home outside the framework.`
  *Towards the objective:* neutral; the idea is kept for the display that may want it, as the L2 record wished
  (HISTORICAL was declined there because *"it would drop a ratified display idea L3 may still want"* [checked: the
  ruling record, lines 48–49]). *Towards the principles:* one home for display matters, the one the L3 rulings already
  use; RELOCATED is stretched exactly as the user has already ratified twice. *Cost:* the "home" is a list in L3's own
  notes waiting for a display specification, not a section of another document. It is a holding place, as §6.8 is
  *"recorded and deferred product work"*. Whether a §10 transfer-list entry is also made for it is the same open
  point as item (xxxvii)'s *To do at release* (read §10's headings first).
- **B — RELOCATED to a new home in `ARCHITECTURE.md`.** A display section would have to be created first; no ruling
  orders that, and creating it is beyond this act. *Towards the principles:* it would add a second home for display
  matters beside the display list.
- **C — Treat Ruling 2's condition as met the other way: RELOCATED to L2, naming L2-S27.** This reads A6 as not
  admitting display destinations, against the two rulings above. *Towards the principles:* it goes against the record.

**The record settles the general answer to A6.** What is the user's is the home Ruling 2's display destination names:
A is the one place the records read by this sitting give for display matters, and the writing side leans to it.
