### 1.3 The test against D-599 and the records (entry 291 §3)

D-599 **[checked at its `DECISIONS.md` row]**: *"apply the standing principles FIRST, and only where they do not decide
is there a genuine user choice — what remains for the user is ratifying the derivation, not picking an option"*.

**What the old design says the operation is.** Row 46.27 **[checked at the row]**: *"Each operation below is over the
skeleton — analysis *inverts* it (recognises the substituted surface as the underlying function); composition *offers*
it (proposes the substituted form)"*. In plain words: a named progression such as ii–V–I can appear with one of its
chords replaced (the V by a chord a tritone away, `♭II7`); analysis recognises the replaced form as the same named
progression and says which chord stands in for which.

**What the records already fix.**
1. **L3 may not revise L2** — the L3 charter, *"It may not consume anything L2 did not publish, and it may not revise
   L2"* (`FRAMEWORK.md`, L3 block, line 477) **[checked]**. So the Roman numeral L2 decided (`♭II7`) is never rewritten
   by a recognition. That is D-506's first half (*"the literal Roman numeral is never rewritten"*), which follows from
   the charter without carrying the old decision over. Part 1 already notes Row 44.37 (ii) proposed RELOCATED to L2 by
   the reading file **[checked at part 1]**.
2. **The replaced chords are L2's chords.** L2-S4 (`cowork_blind_derivation_l2_2026_09_27.md`) **[checked]**: the
   vocabulary contains *"the four triad qualities on every diatonic and chromatically altered degree"* and *"the five
   seventh-chord qualities … on every degree"* — so `♭II7`, a parallel-mode chord, a same-function diatonic chord are
   all chords L2 can decide. L2-S3 **[checked]**: *"No chord is excluded under any tonality"*, a foreign chord *"admitted
   and costs more"*. L2-S5 **[checked]**: an applied chord's target is a chain (`V7/IV/III`). The L2 chord-vocabulary
   Ruling 1 **[checked, whole]**: every listed chord kind *"is its own state, choosable and publishable however rare"*.
   So L3 meets these chords already decided; recognising a substitution adds a relation between a decided chord and a
   catalog member, and adds no chord, since L3 may not revise L2 (record 1) **[my reading]**. *(Former wording, kept
   (#12): "…and a catalog member, never a chord.", unmarked.)*
3. **The fact-publication corollary** (`CLAUDE.md`, *Guiding principles*, as key Ruling 1 states it **[relayed from the
   key record]**): a derived analytical fact is published once, *"on the producing layer's output surface"* — the
   quoted words are the corollary's as the record quotes them; *"published once"* is the record's own wording.
   *(Former wording, kept (#12): "as key Ruling 1 quotes it", without the sentence after the dash.)* Key Ruling
   1 names the fact's consumers, *"presentation; a future chord-suggestion tool"*.
4. **D-506** (`decisions/group_M.md`) **[checked, whole entry]**: *"When the recogniser sees that a chord is standing in
   for another — a tritone substitute doing a dominant's job — it says so in the annotation and leaves the Roman numeral

   queue"*, which on this side's reading describes the entry when it was entered, not now **[my reading; not traced]**.
   *(Former wording, kept (#12): the item without the two sentences on the provenance line.)* Its carrying over into L3

**The positions, and how two fall away.**
- **A — no substitution in the fact:** L3 matches each entry only as written; a substitution entry is never applied.
  *Towards the objective (#4):* a ii–`♭II7`–I is then not recognised as a ii–V–I unless the catalog lists that variant
  as an entry of its own — and a catalog that lists the variants publishes the substitution all the same, in the
  variant's name. So A either loses what the analysis would say about the passage or reproduces the next position by
  writing the variants out; the difference is then how the catalog stores its content, which is detail design
  **[my reading]**.
- **B — the substitution is part of the match:** a progression entry is matched through the catalog's substitution
  mappings, under the same exact test (part 2's Row 57.401 (i): members *"in order and contiguously"*; that a member
  filled by its mapped substitute still meets that test is **[my reading]**: no threshold is set, but the member's degree
  and quality are the substitute's, not the entry's as written), and the
  published match states, beside the entry's name and the stretch, which member was filled by a substitute and by
  which operation. L2's chords and numerals are untouched (record 1).
- **C — a separate per-chord fact:** beside the matches, L3 publishes for each substitute chord the function it stands
  for. *Towards the principles:* it states twice what the match already carries (#6; the corollary's *"published
  once"* — the key record's wording, see record 3), and a function label on a chord outside L2's decided degree reads as
  a second reading of that chord, which the charter's *"may not revise L2"* stands against **[my reading]**. *(Former
  wording, kept (#12): "…(#6; the corollary's "published once"), … which the charter's "may not revise L2" rules out".)*

**Result, on this side's derivation: the alternatives collapse to B.** *(Former wording, kept (#12): "Result: the
alternatives collapse to B.")* It is what key Ruling 1's own grounds give when applied to the open point:
the relation is established from L2's settled chords and the catalog before any presentation decision (the user's
point 3, as key Ruling 1 records it), and publishing it once at L3 is what keeps presentation and the suggestion tool
from re-deriving it (the corollary). It is also D-506's second half (*"a substitution is recorded only in the
annotation"*) read for L3: the annotation is the published match **[my reading of "annotation"]**.

**What B does not settle, named:**

  of the amended L3 specification"* (lead-rows record, as the further-rows surface quotes it) **[relayed]**;
- **exact or tolerant matching** — key Ruling 1's point, unchanged. B is exact: a member replaced by its mapped

  an inserted `V7/x`, 46.29, 46.33) breaks *"contiguously"*, so matching through it would be a tolerance **[my

- **the generative slots** (`subV7/x`, `V7/x` at any degree) — the further rows' Rows 57.392 (ii), 57.434 (ii) and 57.402,

- **the carrier form** — part 1 §3.2's (per match, or a non-tiling span kind);
- **the composition direction** — the chord-suggestion-tool surface (as lead-rows Row 46.37).

**Documentation or algorithm: B is an algorithm change.** The method that stands, as key Ruling 1 defined it: L3
publishes, per match, the entry's name and the stretch of decided chords it covers, the catalog's entries matched as
written, with whether substitutions are part of the fact left open. B adds two things to it: matches made through a
substitution mapping, and, in each such match, the substituted member and its operation. It changes no part of L2
(record 1) and adds nothing to the evidence L2 weighs (that is Ruling 2's candidate, unmeasured). If B is ratified, the
application act states it with key Ruling 1's amendment to the L3 *Publishes* line and §3.2 **[my reading of where it
is written]**.

## 2. The vocabulary, and terms used below

**Dispositions** as part 1 §2, the lead-rows surface §2 and the further-rows surface §2: **ADOPTED — proposed**,
**RELOCATED**, **UNPLACED**, and *"decided against in so far as …"*, the re-marking left to the application act.

In plain words **[checked at the rows quoted]**:
- **a substitution entry** — a catalog record of an operation that replaces a chord with another filling the same role
  (the further-rows surface §2);
- **a substitution mapping** — the rule a substitution entry states, from the replaced member to its substitute (Row
  44.4's *"the catalog's substitution mappings"*);
- **through a substitution** — a progression entry's member filled by a mapped substitute instead of the member as
  written, the rest of the entry matched as written.
