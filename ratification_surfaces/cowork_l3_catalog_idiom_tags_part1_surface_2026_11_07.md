## 1. What this surface does, in plain words

### 1.1 The subject

The old design gave every entry of the catalog of named progressions an **idiom tag**: the style or styles the entry
belongs to, drawn from the project's five-idiom set (diatonic-functional, chromatic-functional, seventh-functional,
triadic-modal, chromatic-coloristic). It also gave every entry two further labels, **mode** and **chromaticism**, which
it calls cross-attributes. In the old design the recogniser used these tags: a preset chose which entries were active,
and an idiom mixture weighted each match as evidence. This surface places the 26 row-parts that state those things.

## 2. The vocabulary, and terms used below

**Dispositions** as the substitution surfaces': **ADOPTED — proposed**, **RELOCATED**, **UNPLACED**, and *decided
against in so far as …* (the lead-rows form); the re-marking is the application act's. Plain words:
- **idiom tag** — the set of idioms (from the five) an entry is labelled with; **multi-valued** means one entry may
  carry several;
- **cross-attributes** — two further labels on an entry, *mode* (major, minor, …) and *chromaticism* (diatonic,
  chromatic, …), set independently of the idioms;
- **the catalog's final content** — which entries the catalog holds and what each carries; the lead-rows record leaves
  it to *"the ratification act of the amended L3 specification"* **[checked]**;
- **the presentation decision** — key Ruling 1's *"decided later and separately"*.
