## §A.1 What this family is

The derived statement, whole (*checked*, `cowork_blind_derivation_l2_2026_09_27.md`, its lines 478–507):

> **L2-S18. Wherever a span can be read as an applied chord in the prevailing tonality, it can also be
> read as a diatonic chord in a new tonality, and the reverse (V/V in C against V in G). Both readings
> are admitted, and both are carried as rivals with their mass. Which one is principal is decided by the
> candidate score over the whole sequence, not by a rule.**

In plain words: take a D major chord going to a G major chord in a piece in C major. One analyst writes
"V/V – V" (the piece stays in C; the D major chord is the dominant of the dominant, borrowed for a moment).
Another writes "V – I in G" (the music has moved to G major). L2 keeps both readings. Each gets its share of
the weight, and the one with more weight is published as the principal reading. Which one gets more weight
is decided by how well each whole reading fits the music, not by a rule such as "stay in the home key unless
a cadence confirms the new one".

Its defense (*checked*, same lines): the charter publishes both a tonality and an applied target per span,
so "neither is a spelling of the other"; two published systems collapse applied chords into brief key
changes, which the charter's output forbids; "The line between modulation and tonicization is not clearly
defined in tonal music" (Kostka & Payne, as quoted, relayed by the derivation); exemplars 1 and 2 place the
move to D minor at different bars; exemplar 3 keeps a passage in D minor as chords applied to III
("`V7/III`, `I/III`, `IVM7/III`") where another analyst might write F major; a leading tone at a cadence
"does not separate" real from passing changes (ledger C42). Status: "Settled". Premise: the candidate score
has terms that can tell the two apart over a stretch — "the tonality-persistence and progression terms of
L2-S34". False-negative path: where no term separates them, "The two then receive near-equal mass, which is
the honest publication."

**Where the work is actually done (*checked*, the derivation's line 844):** L2-S34 — "(i) Tonality
persistence and distance: a cost for a tonality change, graded by the distance between the two tonalities
in a published tonal space. (ii) The tonality–chord cost, of L2-S3. (iii) Progression: a term on the pair
of adjacent chords *read as degrees in their tonalities*, including the cadential progressions that confirm
a tonality. Each family's weights are fitted." So a brief lean pays the change cost twice (out and back)
for little gain in fit, and tends to stay an applied chord; a long, well-fitting stretch in the new
tonality repays the cost and tends to become a change of tonality; a cadence in the new tonality is
evidence through the progression term, not a condition (*my reading* of how the terms combine; the same
arithmetic D-348 describes for the old key layer, *checked* at Row 7.77).

### §A.4.2 Confirmation 2 — Row 5.177: "a chord fully diatonic to the home key is never applied"

**The row, whole** (*checked*, comparison line 6520; `cowork_layer5_function_design.md` §5.6): "A chord
fully diatonic to the home key is never applied (the natural-minor `bVII7→III`, all-diatonic, stays the
diatonic numeral — it is **not** `V7/III`)."

**The music, in plain words.** In D minor, the chord C–E–G–B♭ going to F major. Every note belongs to D
minor's natural-minor scale. One analyst writes it "♭VII7 – III" (two chords of D minor). Another writes
"V7/III – III" (the dominant seventh of F major, the relative major, borrowed for a moment while staying
in D minor). A third could write "V7 – I in F" (a move to F major). The old rule forbade the second reading
whenever every note is diatonic.

**What L2 says** (*checked*, the derivation): L2-S2 — "Every reading a published analysis writes for a
passage of the grading repertoire must be admissible … A reading type that some analyst writes and the
rule does not admit is excluded only by a named ruling that carries the ceiling cost it imposes." L2-S5 —
"A chord may be applied to a degree …". And L2-S18's defense cites exemplar 3, which "keeps a whole passage
in D minor as chords applied to III (`V7/III`, `I/III`, `IVM7/III`)" (*relayed* — the derivation's "FACT at
the file"; exemplar 3's file was not opened this sitting). So an annotation writes the reading the old rule
forbids.

**Why the record already answers it.** Chord-vocabulary Ruling 1 of 2026-10-05 (*checked*, its lines
39–47): "L2-S2, L2-S4 and L2-S5 stand as derived", "every chord kind the vocabulary lists is its own state,
choosable and publishable however rare — … an applied chain such as V7/IV/III." Excluding V7/III over a
fully diatonic chord would be an exclusion with no named ruling and no stated ceiling cost. *(My reading:
this row is as much an admission question, L2-S2 and L2-S5, as an L2-S18 one; the comparison filed it under
L2-S18.)*

**What is proposed: Row 5.177 HISTORICAL — a superseded design rule.**

**Algorithm.** Nothing changes. *Description, not change:* under L2 the three readings above are all

**Towards the objective (#4):** an annotated reading stays reachable (L2-S2's ceiling). **Towards the
