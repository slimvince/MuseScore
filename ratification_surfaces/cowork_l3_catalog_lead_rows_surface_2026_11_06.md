## 1. What this surface does, in plain words

On 2026-11-06 the user gave two rulings on the old catalog of named progressions (ii–V–I, turnarounds, sequences,
bass-line patterns, galant schemata, substitutions) — `records/cowork/rulings/cowork_rulings_2026_11_06_l3_catalog_key_sitting.md`
**[checked, whole]**:

- **Ruling 1 (algorithm change):** L3 publishes, as a read-off fact, which catalog entries fit which stretches of L2's
  decided chords — per match the entry's name and the stretch it covers, overlapping matches all published. How a
  match is shown, and whether a preset filters what is shown, is presentation, decided later. The catalog is to be
  named in L3's *Consumes* line as fixed reference knowledge L3 reads, not input about the piece.
- **Ruling 2 (documentation only):** using the catalog as evidence when choosing chords ("use (a)") is a named
  candidate at DP-O, L2's open question on longer-range memory; unmeasured, unadopted.

The key surface (`ratification_surfaces/cowork_l3_catalog_key_surface_2026_11_06.md`) reached 25 "lead" row-parts of
the L3 reading file directly. This surface says, row-part by row-part, what disposition follows from the two rulings,
and where a row says more than the rulings settle, which later surface the rest goes to. **Nothing here asks a new
choice of scope or method**; the splits of §3.2 and the moves of §3.4 are the writing side's proposals under the route
ruling, which the user may change. *(Former wording, kept (#12): "**Nothing here is a new choice.**")* What is put to the user is whether to ratify these derivations (D-599).

**Documentation or algorithm: documentation only.** Placing a row changes no part of the method. **The method that
stands** is the one the two rulings left: L2 decides key and chord together, its progression term a table of
chord-to-chord pairs, DP-O open with the catalog term among its unmeasured candidates; L3 publishes the cadence at each
phrase end with its type, the phrase and section grouping, the chord symbol, the figured bass, the harmonic rhythm —
and, by Ruling 1, the catalog matches, once the application act amends `FRAMEWORK.md`.

## 2. The vocabulary used below

The reading file's closed disposition axis (`ratification_surfaces/cowork_comparison_l3_reading.md` §5) **[checked]**:
**ADOPTED — proposed** means no derived statement carries the content yet, it is not implementation-describing, not
another charter's and not historical, and it is proposed for addition to the derived specification (a proposal the
ratification act later confirms or not). **RELOCATED** means the content belongs to another charter, named in
`FRAMEWORK.md` §5's words. **UNPLACED** is the default for a row the writing side cannot place. Where a ruling decides
against part of a row, earlier L3 records write *"decided against in so far as …"*, the re-marking left to the
application act (route record §2 step 2; the inventory's tables of rows reached, for example its §1.1 and §1A.1) **[checked]**.

**Why most of these rows become ADOPTED — proposed.** Most were UNPLACED on one ground: *"`FRAMEWORK.md` §5 names no
charter whose subject is a catalog of named progressions"* (Row 7.85's ground, or Row 44.1's, which states the same
gap); the knowledge-base rows (38.20, 44.21, and those travelling with 38.20 (i)) on the parallel ground that no
charter, nor a NOT A LAYER item, names a knowledge-base component **[checked at the rows]**. *(Former wording, kept
(#12): "Each was UNPLACED on one ground: … (Row 7.85's ground, which the others cite)".)* Ruling 1 removes both
grounds: L3 now reads the catalog and publishes its matches. A row that states what the
catalog is or holds then has a charter, and on the vocabulary's own definitions it is ADOPTED — proposed **[my reading,
derived from §5's definitions and Ruling 1]**. Which entries the catalog finally holds is still the user's at the
ratification act; this surface only says where the rows are filed.
