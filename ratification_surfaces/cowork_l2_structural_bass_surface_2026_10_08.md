## §A.1 What this family is

The derived statement, whole (*checked*, `cowork_blind_derivation_l2_2026_09_27.md`, its lines 709–734):

> **L2-S28. The figure is decided in the reading. It is the chord member the reading takes as the
> span's structural bass, which is not necessarily the lowest pitch sounding at the span's first slice.
> The candidate score has a term relating the figure to the lowest sounding pitch of each slice of the
> span, weighted by duration and metric strength. The lowest sounding pitch per slice is not published
> by L1 for every slice, which is OQ-L2-11, naming IC S-44 and S-50.**
> - *Defense.* In arpeggiated and Alberti textures the lowest pitch of a short slice is not the harmony's
>   bass [FACT — ledger C32, C38; IC S-44 declares the same hazard]. Alberti writing requires carrying a
>   root across a moving, re-inverted bass, and one continuity rule cannot serve both that and sustained
>   arpeggiation [FACT — ledger C32, the conflict half]. So "the bass at the first event" is wrong as a
>   rule. One published system uses two definitions of a segment's bass, the lowest note of the first
>   event and the lowest note of the whole segment, as separate features whose weights are fitted [FACT
>   (relayed, 08) — Masada & Bunescu, Appendix C.3, p. 18]. Inversion accuracy falls steeply away from
>   root position in a published large-vocabulary system (71.5 / 54.4 / 38.3 / 40.5 for root, first,
>   second and third) [FACT (relayed, 08) — McLeod & Rohrmeier 2021, §results]. That is where the figure
>   decision is weakest, so the figure must be carried in the rivals (L2-S14). L1 publishes the lowest
>   sounding pitch as a cue anchor, at onset change points and inside cue witnesses [RULED — IC S-44,
>   S-49, S-50], not for every slice. Computing it from the sounding set would re-derive an L1-defined
>   quantity [RULED — D-100], so L2 writes the want instead.
> - *Source class.* Derived.
> - *Status.* Settled in shape. **Open** on the per-slice input (OQ-L2-11).
> - *Premise.* The structural bass is a reading decision, not a notated fact. **False-negative path:** a
>   figure analysts read from the first bass note regardless. The term weights would then fit toward the
>   first slice, which the fit shows.
> - *Falsifier.* CODE. **Observable:** figures over an Alberti span. **Decision rule:** falsified if the
>   figure is set by a fixed rule from one slice's lowest pitch. **Not falsified by:** the fitted term
>   preferring the first slice.

And its open question, whole (*checked*, the derivation's lines 1240–1243):

> **OQ-L2-11 (face (e); L2-S28) — lowest sounding pitch per slice.** L1 publishes the lowest sounding
> pitch as a cue anchor at onset change points and in cue witnesses (IC S-44, S-49), not for every
> slice. The figure term wants it for every slice. *The input-contract statements concerned:* S-44 and
> S-50. Would S-50's list gain the lowest sounding pitch per slice?

**In plain words.** The *figure* is the inversion part of a Roman numeral: whether the chord stands in root
position, first inversion, and so on — in other words, which member of the chord is its bass. L2 does not read
that off the lowest note at the start of the chord. It decides it as part of the reading, because in an Alberti
bass or a broken-chord accompaniment the lowest note of a short moment is often not the chord's real bass. The
evidence is one term of the candidate score: how well the chosen figure fits the lowest sounding pitch of each
slice inside the span, each slice counted by its length and by how strong a beat it starts on. (A *slice* is
L1's smallest stretch of music: a stretch during which the same notes sound.)

**One wording point the reader needs from the start.** Under L1's ratified wording the word *bass* is kept for the
chord's bass note, and L1's object is called *the lowest sounding pitch* of a slice (Ruling 42 of the 2026-08-31
record, *checked*, its lines 3523–3567; L0/L1 derived specification §0 and S-44, *checked*). This surface follows
that: *structural bass* and *figure* are the chord's; *the lowest sounding pitch* is the fact on the page.

## §A.2 The research, searched before writing (entry 319 §2)

**At the derivation (*checked*).** L2-S28 cites two published systems, both *relayed* from the reading pass:

**At `docs/research_papers/BIBLIOGRAPHY.md` (*checked*, whole).** Two rows bear on the subject by their titles:
Masada & Bunescu (line 24, held) and Ju, Margot, McKay, Dahn & Fujinaga 2020, "Automatic Figured Bass Annotation…
(BCFB)" (line 50, held). No source for the Rule of the Octave or the partimento tradition is listed.

**At the reading-pass extracts (*checked*, each at the lines named).** I searched the twelve extracts staged this
sitting for "inversion", "bass" and "lowest note". I read the hit lines in nine of them; in three — Chen & Su 2018,
Karystinaios & Widmer 2023 and Sailor's RNBERT 2024, each of which has inversion among its outputs — I took the hit
counts only and read no line *(§B item 3; former wording, preserved (#12): "and read the hits that bear on the
figure:" — which implied every extract's hits were read)*. The ones that bear on the figure:
- **Masada & Bunescu 2019** (extract lines 124–131, 155–163): the label set carries **no inversion**; the authors
  say inversion "can be done in a straightforward post-processing step, as a function of the bass note in the
  chord". Their bass features come in two definitions — "the lowest note of the first event in the segment" and
  "the lowest note in the entire segment" — plus, in the extract's own words, "duration-weighted forms over
  events" *(§B item 4; former wording, preserved (#12): "… as a function of the bass note". … — "plus
  duration-weighted forms over events" — a quotation cut without a mark, and the extract's words quoted as the
  authors')*. *My reading:* a duration-weighted lowest
  note over the events of a segment is the published form nearest to L2-S28's per-slice term.
- **McLeod & Rohrmeier 2021** (extract line 40): inversion accuracy 71.5 / 54.4 / 38.3 / 40.5 for root position
  and first, second and third inversion — the figures the derivation relays, confirmed at the extract.
- **Micchi, Gotham & Giraud 2020** (extract lines 215–220, 326–347): one of their input encodings gives the network,
  per time frame, "one [vector] with the lowest note and another with the total pitch content", which the authors
  call a compromise "reflecting the special role of the bass in tonal harmony in defining both chordal inversion
  and other important matters for harmonic progression" *(§B item 5; former wording, preserved (#12), cut the
  quotation at "matters" without a mark)*. Against encodings carrying every octave, or pitch classes only, it measured
  "markedly higher performance not only in identifying the correct inversions, but for all of the tasks (again,
  p-value < 10⁻²)" — inversion 70.1, against −3.5 for full register and −4.7 for pitch classes only (Table 6, as
  the extract transcribes it). *Relayed from the extract; I did not open the paper.* *My reading:* this is a
  measured reason to give the lowest sounding pitch to the chord decision as an input of its own, at a fine time
  grain — their grain is a fixed frame, not a slice.
- **AugmentedNet 2021** (extract lines 275–280, 413–419): the bass has its own input block and its own output task,
  and the authors texturise their synthetic training data with three patterns, *Alberti bass* among them, because
  plain block chords were found "to be only slightly beneficial for the model, possibly because it did not capture
  the complex texture of real keyboard music". The extract records that the bass's contribution was not measured
  there. *(§B item 6; former wording, preserved (#12): "with an "Alberti bass" pattern, because block chords did not
  capture real keyboard texture" — the authors' "possibly" stated as a cause.)*

**What follows, in plain words.** The literature I read supports the statement as derived: the lowest sounding
pitch is measured to help the inversion decision as an input of its own, at a fine grain (Micchi et al.), and
published systems use it as fitted evidence, not as a rule (the one that takes the first event's lowest note also
takes the whole segment's) — that it should reach L2 per slice is *my reading* of those results. In what I read I
found nothing that suggests L2-S28's design rule costs precision, so nothing in Part 1 goes to a measurement (the
user's rule of 2026-10-07); the three extracts whose hits I did not read bound that negative. *(§B item 7; former
wording, preserved (#12): "the lowest sounding pitch is strong evidence for the inversion, it should reach the
decision at a fine grain, and it is not a rule (…). I found nothing that suggests …" — a reading stated as the
literature's, and a negative not bounded by what was not read.)* The figured-bass paper (BCFB) and the Rule of the Octave were not read; the second has no held
source. Both bear on Part 3 and are left to the research iteration ruled on 2026-10-07.
