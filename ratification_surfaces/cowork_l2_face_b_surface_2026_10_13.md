## 2. The five statements, checked against the rulings' words

- **L2-S11 — a boundary is one of L1's change points, decided in the one decision.** Where the boundaries fall is
  decided together with the tonality, the chord and the note assignments, never before the chord or after it.
  *Ruling:* the one-decision record of 2026-10-06. Its three rulings ("yes", "confirmed", "confirmed") place rows
  only. Its §1d says "L2-S11 stands as derived; no wording change is proposed", and the successor's recorded line
  quotes that sentence word for word. The dispatch marks it as a record statement, not a ruling, because it sits
  in §1d and under no ruling heading. **Matches.**
- **L2-S12 — how strong the beat is where a chord changes.** The beat-strength class of a change point is a term on
  putting a boundary there. No metric level forbids a boundary, and the weights are fitted per class.
  *Ruling:* metric strength Ruling 1 ("confirmed"): "L2's algorithm as its statements define it is unchanged; no
  statement's wording changes." Not shown in the successor. **Matches.** Two pointers are missing (correction 2).
- **L2-S13 — notated phrase marks are evidence for a boundary, never a rule.** L1's flags with their scope, its
  positioned marks and its per-voice relations are terms on a boundary. An absent flag is never read as an absent
  boundary, and no flag forces or forbids one. *Rulings:* notated boundary evidence Ruling 1 ("confirmed": "no
  statement's wording changes") and Ruling 2 ("confirmed": "L2-S13's, L2-S31's and L2-S33's wordings are
  unchanged", together with one new item under OQ-L2-3). The successor's line beside L2-S13 points to that item, and

  is unchanged — in the paragraph stated with the question you confirmed, not in its list of what is ruled. That is
  not shown. **Matches.**
- **L2-S14 — two neighbouring stretches never carry the same reading.** Two adjacent spans never have the same
  tonality and the same chord (degree, quality, figure, applied target). A change of figure alone, such as V6 to
  V65, is a change of chord. A change only in which notes are elaborations is not a boundary. *Ruling:*
  adjacent identical spans Ruling 1 ("confirmed"): "L2's algorithm as its statements define it is unchanged;
  L2-S14's wording does not change." Not shown. **Matches.**
- **L2-S15 — rivals that differ only in where a boundary falls are kept as rivals, with their weight.** *Ruling:*
  four agreeing statements Ruling 1 ("yes"): "no wording of L2-S3, L2-S9, L2-S15 or L2-S21 is changed by this

- **Algorithm:** **nothing changes.** Correction 1 adds lines that quote rulings already given. Correction 2 adds

  changes no behaviour. It names the same safeguard (L2-S42) by its ruled name.

**The currently decided method for face (b), in plain words.** Where one harmony gives way to the next is decided
at the same moment as the tonality, the chord and the role of each note, in one decision over whole readings, never
in a separate step before or after. A boundary can only fall where the sounding notes change (one of L1's change
points). Any such point may be a boundary. How strong the beat is there makes a boundary more or less likely
through a fitted weight per beat-strength class, but no beat level forbids one. Notated phrase marks — fermatas,
double bars, and the like, with their scope — and L1's other notated boundary evidence pull towards or away from a
boundary as fitted terms. A mark never forces or forbids a boundary, and a missing mark is not evidence that there
is no boundary. Two neighbouring stretches never carry the same tonality and the same chord. A change of figure
alone (V6 to V65) is a change of chord and may be a boundary. A change only in which notes are passing or
suspended is not: a suspension resolving inside one chord stays one stretch, with the suspension recorded on the
note. Rival readings that differ only in where a boundary falls are kept, each with its weight, exactly like
rivals that differ in label. At the top effort setting none is dropped. Below it, a rival may be withheld under
the cut-off only on L2-S42's conditions. Still open: the fitted weights and whether the three-level beat gradient
holds on this music (OQ-L2-3); whether a notated flag should also weigh on elaborations that cross it (OQ-L2-3);
and how boundary grading maps an annotation's suspension labels and restated chords onto L2's stretches (OQ-L2-7).
