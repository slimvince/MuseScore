## §A.1 What this family is

The derived statement, whole (*checked*, `cowork_blind_derivation_l2_2026_09_27.md`, its lines 752–772):

> **L2-S30. No chord term decides a chord from the pitch-class content of the span alone. The chord
> terms also read the neighbouring spans' readings (the progression), the assignments (which notes
> count), and the voice-leading evidence of L2-S24.**
> - *Defense.* Several ledger entries record that pitch-class content under-determines the chord: an
>   added-sixth chord and a seventh chord on the related root have the same content [FACT — ledger C6];
>   a root-position major triad and the first inversion of its submediant are near-indistinguishable
>   vertically [FACT — ledger C8]; a sonority and the chord a third above it are separated only by the
>   surrounding music [FACT — ledger C2]; three pitch classes with a tritone do not determine the
>   rotation [FACT — ledger C35]; and where a carried root is wrong, the separating evidence arrives after
>   the moment [FACT — ledger C41]. A residual disagreement between a vertical reading and a reading by
>   role "is a legitimate divergence between two readings, not an analyzer defect" [FACT — ledger C7].
>   In one published segment-then-label system, 12% of the labelling errors were "an incomplete chord
>   identifiable only through tonal function in context" [RULED — charter, DP-C, carrying Pardo and
>   Birmingham 2002].
> - *Source class.* Derived.
> - *Status.* Settled.
> - *Premise.* Context terms add separating information where content cannot. **False-negative path:**
>   passages where neither content nor context separates. Near-equal mass is the honest output.
> - *Falsifier.* CODE. **Observable:** the inputs of the chord terms. **Decision rule:** falsified if the
>   candidate score's preference between two chords with identical span content is independent of the
>   adjacent spans' readings. **Not falsified by:** the preference being weak.

**In plain words.** Some chords cannot be told apart by the notes sounding in them. D–F–A–B is both a D minor chord
with an added sixth and a half-diminished seventh on B; the ledger records (*relayed*) that "a root-position major
triad and the first inversion of its submediant are near-indistinguishable vertically" (for C major, *my example*:
C–E–G against C–E–A); and a diminished seventh chord could have any of its four notes as its root. *(§B item 1;
former wording, preserved (#12): "the ledger records (relayed) that C–E–G in root position and the A minor chord in
first inversion, C–E–A, are hard to tell apart from the notes alone" — this side's example put in the ledger's
mouth.)* L2-S30 says that L2 never picks between such readings from the notes of the
stretch alone: the choice also depends on what comes before and after (the progression), on which notes the reading
counts as chord tones and which as passing or neighbour notes (the assignments), and on how the voices move (the
voice-leading evidence L1 publishes, read under L2-S24). Where none of that separates two readings, L2 keeps both
with near-equal weight rather than guessing.

**The derivation names no open question for this statement** (*checked*: a search of the derivation for "S30" finds
only the statement and its row in the source table, line 1355).

**So the family is one confirmation, with one row placed by derivation.** 27 row-parts are filed under L2-S30 at §7
of `ratification_surfaces/cowork_comparison_l2_reading.md` (*checked*, its line 71196–71197: 18 AGREES, 9 DIFFERS).
26 carry a proposal from the comparison; one, Row 23.112, was left UNPLACED. §A.4 shows that the principles and the
record leave one placement for it. None of the 27 is proposed as an addition to L2 (ADOPTED — proposed), so no row
asks for a change to L2's algorithm. *(§B item 2; former wording, preserved (#12): "No row asks for a change to L2's
algorithm." — the ground was not stated.)*

## §A.2 The research, searched before writing (entry 319 §2)

**At the derivation (*checked*, lines 752–772).** L2-S30 cites one published paper, Pardo and Birmingham 2002,
through the charter, and otherwise ledger entries C2, C6, C7, C8, C35 and C41 (the ledger is *relayed*; I did not

**At the charter (*checked*, `FRAMEWORK.md` lines 700–717).** The charter's account of Pardo and Birmingham, already
narrowed on the user's ruling of 2026-09-20 (its lines 719–729, *checked*): with segmentation taken from the answer
key, perfect tie-breaking would remove at most 26% of the labelling errors; of the rest, the authors attribute one
class (12% of errors) to "an incomplete chord identifiable only through tonal function in context", say of another
(15%) that it "might perhaps be resolved through a deep understanding of structural voice leading", and name other
causes or remedies for the others. *(§B item 3; former wording, preserved (#12): "and one (15%) to structural voice
leading, and name other remedies for the others" — the charter's "might perhaps" dropped, which made a hedged remark
an attribution.)* The charter also records the authors' own remark
that using adjacent chord labels would make the calculation "much more difficult".

**At `docs/research_papers/BIBLIOGRAPHY.md` (*checked*, the lines a search for the named authors returned).** Pardo &
Birmingham (line 18, held), Temperley & Sleator (line 21, held) and Raphael & Stoddard 2003 (line 15, held) bear on
the subject by their content as the extracts record it.

**At the reading-pass extracts (*checked* at the lines named; none read whole):**
- **Pardo and Birmingham 2002** (extract lines 102–111, 121–126, 153–177): the labeller reads "no notion of tonal
  context, beat, absolute pitch height, or dynamics" (p. 31); segments are scored "without reference to their
  context" by a stated assumption made to limit the search (p. 36); its one contextual rule is a tie-break for
  diminished seventh chords, choosing the root a semitone below the root of the following segment's chord (p. 30).
  Table 4 (p. 32): mean label score 84.54% with no tie-breaking, 86.11% with that rule alone, 88.66% with all three
  tie-break rules; standard deviations 10.7 to 13.0 over 45 excerpts. *My reading:* the table gives the spread of
  the score across excerpts, not the uncertainty of the difference between two regimes on the same excerpts, so
  the size of the gain from the contextual rule cannot be read from it (#24); I do not cite it as evidence that
  context helps. The paper's error classes are what bear on L2-S30, as the charter already carries. *(§B item 4;
  former wording, preserved (#12): "a difference of under two points against a spread of about thirteen is not a
  finding (#24)" — it took the spread across excerpts for the uncertainty of a paired difference, and asserted a
  verdict the table cannot give either way.)*
- **Temperley and Sleator 1999** (extract lines 93–101): the harmony program's preference rules read context — the
  **harmonic variance rule**, "prefer roots such that roots of nearby chord spans are close together on the line of
  fifths", and the **ornamental dissonance rule**, which reads whether an event is "closely followed by another
  event a half-step or whole-step away in pitch" and is metrically weak. *My reading:* a published system in which
  the choice of root reads the neighbouring spans' roots, and the choice of which notes count as ornamental reads the
  melodic motion and the metre — L2-S30's shape. *(§B item 5; former wording, preserved (#12): "in which the choice
  of root reads the neighbouring spans' roots and the melodic motion" — the ornamental rule labels events, not
  roots.)*
- **Raphael and Stoddard 2003**: I saw only search-result lines (73–81), which describe a chord-transition
  probability within a key; I did not read the extract, and I rest nothing on it.

**What the research does not give.** No source I read measures the gain from adding context to a content-only chord
choice on the same data with its uncertainty. L2-S30's status as Settled rests on the ledger's cases and the error
analysis above, and the size of the gain is what the fitted weights (L2-S38) will show.

**Algorithm: unchanged.** L2-S30's wording does not change, and nothing is added to or taken from the candidate score.

**The method that stands, as the ruled statements define it** (each *checked* at the derivation):
- Where the boundaries fall "is decided together with the tonality, the chord and the assignments, in the one
  decision" (L2-S11, lines 331–333). *(§B item 13; former wording, preserved (#12): "L2 decides the chord inside one
  decision with the boundaries, the tonality and the assignments (L2-S11)." — L2-S11 had not been read at the
  derivation when "each checked" was written; it is now, and the line quotes it.)*
- The candidate score is "a sum over its spans of span terms, plus a sum over adjacent span pairs of pair terms"; a
  pair term "reads two adjacent spans' fields and the evidence at their shared boundary" (L2-S31, lines 776–780).
- One family of pair terms is the progression: "a term on the pair of adjacent chords *read as degrees in their
  tonalities*, including the cadential progressions that confirm a tonality", its weights fitted (L2-S34, lines
  844–848).
- The assignments — which notes are chord tones — are made "inside the candidate score, relative to each candidate
  reading's chord, and never by a detector that runs first" (L2-S23, lines 601–602).
- The voice-leading evidence is read from L1's per-voice relations (L2-S24, line 623 onward), and an elaboration
  whose preparing or resolving note lies in the next or previous span reads that span's candidate reading (L2-S33,
  lines 822–825).
- Every weight is fitted from annotated music on held-out folds (L2-S38, lines 938–941).
- So, between two chords with the same notes in a span, the preference moves with the neighbouring chords through the
  progression and elaboration terms; where nothing separates them, both are kept with near-equal mass.
