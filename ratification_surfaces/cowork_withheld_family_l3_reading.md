## 1. The words used here, explained first

- **A deriving session** — a session that writes what the analysis *should* do for one unit,
  from the domain and from your ratified design intent, without reading what the current code or
  the current specifications say it *does*.
- **The boot pack** — the self-contained directory such a session opens at boot, and nothing
  else.  L3's pack does not exist yet; its members are a later ruling.
- **The withheld family** — the recorded decisions, documents and passages cut out of that pack
  for one subject, so that the answer the session is chartered to derive does not reach it.
- **A candidate** — a decisions-register entry the ruled criterion picked as possibly disclosing
  some part of that answer.  Being a candidate is not a judgment; the verdict is.
- **A verdict** — one of three words written against one candidate: **IN** (withhold it), **OUT**
  (admit it), **UNPLACED** (the entry's own text does not settle it).  Each carries a *finding* —
  what the entry's own text says — and a *reason* — why that makes it IN, OUT or UNPLACED.
- **The register group** — the letter under which `DECISIONS.md` lists an entry.  It is a
  property of the entry and decided no verdict.  Note that register group **F** is titled
  *Layer 3 — key and mode* while this subject is called **L3**, and that register group **H**,
  *Layer 5 and Layer 6 — function, cadence, grouping*, carries much of what the framework puts
  at L3: they are different units in two different numbering schemes (`ARCHITECTURE.md`'s
  Layer 1–6 and the framework's L0 to L3).

## 2. The subject the deriving session will derive, stated from scratch

> **The read-off facts: given a settled tonal reading, where does each phrase end and how do phrases group into sections, what cadence stands at each phrase end and of what type, what is the chord symbol, what is the figured bass, and what is the harmonic rhythm?**

That is L3's charter question, located on this run at `FRAMEWORK.md` under the heading
`### L3 — The read-off facts` — *"given a settled tonal reading, what else does a harmonic analysis of this music say?"* — and the same block's **Publishes** line names the FIVE
facts this layer reads off that settled reading: the cadence at each phrase end, with its type;
the phrase and section grouping; the chord symbol — root pitch class, quality and bass note; the
figured bass; and the harmonic rhythm.  L3 may not revise L2: the tonality, where one harmony
gives way to the next, which sounding notes are chord tones, and the chord read over each span
reach it settled.  A verdict here is IN if the entry discloses the ruled answer to how L3 reads
off ANY of the five, in whole or in part.
