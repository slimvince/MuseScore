### L2-S3 (its lines 159–179)

> **L2-S3. No chord is excluded under any tonality. A chord whose pitches are foreign to the span's tonality is
> admitted and costs more in the candidate score.**
> - *Defense.* The charter fixes the coupling between tonality and chord as a cost, never a veto, and
>   generalises it: "a reading-shaped evidence producer is a score and never a constraint" [RULED — charter,
>   L2]. … "adding a compatibility between chords and keys has led to a decrease of accuracy" … [FACT (relayed,
>   08) — Rocher et al. 2010, p. 143] *(§B item 1)*. … Exemplar 5's bar 28 reads a major tonic in D minor (`I ||`, a Picardy close) …
> - *Source class.* Given — charter. *Status.* Settled.
> - *Premise.* A finite cost is always enough to express "unlikely here". **False-negative path:** a cost so
>   large that it acts as a veto in practice. It would be caught by L2-S2's closure test …
> - *Falsifier.* … falsified if any chord of the vocabulary is absent from it for some tonality, or if the
>   tonality–chord cost is infinite for any pair. **Not falsified by:** a large finite cost.

In plain words: under any key, every chord stays possible. A chord that does not fit the key (a chord with a
borrowed or chromatic note) is not ruled out; it just scores worse. The same rule covers every piece of
evidence shaped like a reading: it may lean, never forbid.

**What the record has already said.** The rule is the charter's own (source class "Given — charter", *relayed*
from the derivation; `FRAMEWORK.md` not opened). One-decision Ruling 1 of 2026-10-06 put it in its ground in the
charter's words: "The coupling between tonality and chord is a cost, never a veto" (that record's lines 70–71,
*checked*).

### L2-S9 (its lines 287–306)

> **L2-S9. An assignment is made per event per span, not per event. An event that sounds across a span boundary
> receives one assignment in each span it sounds in. A suspension, for example, is a chord tone in the span
> where it is prepared and a suspension in the span where it dissonates.**
> - *Defense.* A suspension is by definition a note held from a harmony in which it is a chord tone into a
>   harmony in which it is not [THEORY]. … L1 publishes events whole across slices, with a tied group as one
>   event [RULED — IC S-23]. So one event can meet two harmonies, and a single per-event assignment could not
>   express the suspension.
> - *Source class.* Derived. *Status.* Settled.
> - *Premise.* A span-level assignment is fine-grained enough. **False-negative path:** an event whose role
>   changes within one span … It is expressed only if a boundary is placed there, which L2-S14 permits when the
>   figure changes.
> - *Falsifier.* … falsified if such an event carries one assignment only, or if it is ever an elaboration in
>   an earlier span and a chord tone in a later one because it *became* consonant …

In plain words: a held note (including a tied note, which counts as one note) is labelled once in each chord it
sounds under. The textbook case is the suspension: the held note is a chord tone under the first chord and a
suspension under the next.

**What the record has already said.** Score-decomposition Ruling 3 of 2026-10-07 placed D-330's "never a
pooled recompute" rows HISTORICAL and said their substance "is carried by L2-S23, L2-S9, L2-S32 and the L0/L1
reading's S-33" (that record's lines 67–69, *checked*). The elaboration record of 2026-10-05 declined a
"cambiata" name partly because "a cambiata spans several notes, which the per-event assignment (L2-S9) does not
carry — the last a reading" (its lines 86–88, *checked*). Neither ruled L2-S9 itself.

### L2-S15 (its lines 424–436)

> **L2-S15. Rivals that differ only in where a boundary falls are carried as rivals, with their mass, exactly as
> rivals that differ in label are.**
> - *Defense.* The charter: "per span, the rivals with their mass — including rivals that differ in where the
>   boundaries fall and not only in the label" [RULED — charter, L2; DP-K]. …
> - *Source class.* Given — charter. *Status.* Settled. The form is L2-S41.
> - *Falsifier.* … falsified if two readings differing only in a boundary position cannot both appear. **Not
>   falsified by:** the boundary rival falling below a publication threshold that obeys L2-S42.

In plain words: if two readings name the same chords but disagree about where one chord ends and the next
begins, both are kept, each with its weight, just as two readings with different chord names are.

**What the record has already said.** The rule is the charter's own (*relayed* from the derivation).
Rival-publication Ruling 1 of 2026-10-05 changed L2-S42 (at the top effort setting nothing is withheld; below

threshold of L2-S42" to "down to the bound the effort setting sets" (that record's lines 31–46, *checked*). See
§A.4 item 1 for what that means for L2-S15's own last clause.

### L2-S21 (its lines 555–573)

> **L2-S21. The minor mode is one mode whose sixth and seventh degrees may each be raised or not. A reading in
> minor may use either form of each without a change of tonality. The tonic chord in major quality at a
> minor-mode close (the Picardy third) is a chord of the minor tonality, not a change to the major.**
> - *Defense.* The minor mode's variable sixth and seventh degrees (the natural, harmonic and melodic
>   collections) are standard theory [THEORY]. … I♯-PA1 final cadences outnumbering i-PA1 about ten to one in
>   the Bach chorales [FACT (relayed, 08) — de Clercq, pp. 196–197] *(§B item 1)*. … It is admitted as a rival (L2-S3) …
> - *Source class.* Derived. *Status.* Settled.
> - *Premise.* Analysts read the Picardy close in the minor tonality. **False-negative path:** an analyst who
>   writes a change to the parallel major. It is admitted as a rival.
> - *Falsifier.* … falsified if raised 6̂ or 7̂ chords, or the major tonic, are admissible only through a
>   change of tonality. **Not falsified by:** a rival reading in the parallel major.

In plain words: minor is one key whose sixth and seventh notes can each be raised or not (natural, harmonic and
melodic minor are one key, not three). Using a raised seventh does not change the key. A major tonic chord at
the end of a minor piece (the Picardy third) is read as a chord of the minor key, not as a move to major — while
the move-to-major reading still stays in play as a rival.

**What the record has already said.** Tonality-vocabulary Ruling 1 of 2026-10-04 ruled the first
specification's modes "two modes, major and composite minor (the composite minor practice: natural, harmonic
and melodic as one tonality with variable sixth and seventh degrees, as D-524 states it)" (its lines 81–83,
*checked*). That is L2-S21's first two sentences. The Picardy sentence is derived and follows from L2-S3: the
major tonic is admitted under minor at a cost, and the change-of-key reading is a rival (*my reading*).

## §A.6 The method that stands — no algorithm change is proposed anywhere in this surface

Quoted from the derivation and the records, *checked* this sitting:
- **L2-S3:** every chord of the vocabulary is admissible under every tonality; a chord foreign to the span's
  tonality is admitted at a higher cost in the candidate score; the tonality–chord cost is finite for every
  pair; every reading-shaped evidence producer is a score, never a constraint.
- **L2-S9:** assignments are made per event per span; an event sounding across a span boundary (a tied group
  is one event, IC S-23 as relayed by the derivation) receives one assignment in each span it sounds in, so a
  suspension is a chord tone where prepared and a suspension where it dissonates; the assignment is made inside
  the candidate score, relative to each candidate chord (L2-S23, elaboration-relations Ruling 2, *relayed* from
  the L2-S1 surface) *(§B item 10)*.
- **L2-S15:** readings that differ only in where a boundary falls are carried and published as rivals with
  their mass, in the form L2-S41 gives (one rival object over consecutive spans, with its extent and
  whole-reading mass), down to the bound the effort setting sets; at the top effort setting no span-rival is
  withheld (rival-publication Ruling 1).
- **L2-S21:** the tonality vocabulary is major and composite minor (tonality-vocabulary Ruling 1, D-524); a
  minor reading uses raised or natural sixth and seventh degrees without a change of tonality; the Picardy
  tonic is a chord of the minor tonality, and the change-to-major reading stays a rival.
