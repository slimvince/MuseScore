## 2. The derived statement and the open question

**L2-S46**, quoted (*checked*): "L2 works over the record's own positions, in notated order. At each repeat
junction L1 publishes, the span following the junction has more than one predecessor, one per pass, and
the pair terms are taken over each. Which order the published ground truth is aligned to, and therefore
which order is graded, is a question for the user (OQ-L2-16 ★)."

*(A pair term is a part of the candidate score that reads two neighbouring spans together, such as how
likely one chord is to follow another.)*

- *Its defence.* The input contract settled that L1 works in notated order and publishes the junction
  adjacencies, and handed "the order the analysis as a whole decides over, and the ground truth's
  alignment" to L2's surface. Unfolding is a performance reading; L2 can honour both passes without
  choosing one, by scoring each junction's predecessors. A published corpus reports that its harmony labels
  "had to be unfolded according to the repeat structures", so graded ground truth may be in either order.
  Exemplar 5 notates a repeat (`m8 I :|| b4 V`) whose analysis writes one reading across the junction.
  *(The exemplar fact is relayed from the derivation; I did not open the exemplars.)*
- *Its source class.* Derived.
- *Its status.* Settled for L2's internal order. Open for the graded order (OQ-L2-16 ★).
- *Its premise and how it could fail.* "One reading per span serves both passes." It fails for "a span
  *before* the junction whose reading changes because of what follows on the second pass". A first ending
  whose harmony differs by pass does not make it fail: the two endings are different spans.
- *Its falsifier.* CODE. "**Observable:** pair terms at a junction. **Decision rule:** falsified if the span
  after an end-repeat is scored against one predecessor only. **Not falsified by:** the two predecessors'
  contributions being combined by a declared rule."

**OQ-L2-16 ★**, quoted (*checked*): "is the ground truth this project grades against aligned to notated
order or to unfolded order? Is the working span a stretch of notated bars, or of the performed sequence?
(This is the remainder of the input contract's OQ-1.)"

## 5. The question — does L2 decide one reading per notated position, or one per pass? (a real choice, close to collapsed)

**What is being decided.** When a passage is repeated, L2 can either read each notated bar once, scoring
both passes' neighbours at each junction (position one), or read the passage once per pass, so that the
same notated bars can carry different readings on the first and second time through (position two).

**Position one — one reading per notated position (L2-S46 as derived; the recommendation).** L2 decides
over notated order. At each junction the span on either side has one neighbour per pass, and the pair
terms are taken over each, combined by a declared rule. OQ-L2-16 is answered as follows: the working span
is a stretch of notated positions (already ruled, Ruling 46 (i)); L2 decides over notated order; how a
ground truth is aligned is a fact established per corpus where the grading is designed, not ruled here.

- *Towards the objective (#4).* The case the derivation names where the passes differ in the notation, a
  first and second ending, is already two different spans, so it is fully expressed. How often passes
  differ in any other way was not measured by anything I read. What position one cannot say is the
  derivation's own failure case: a bar before a junction heard differently because of what follows on the
  second pass. There, both readings remain as rivals with their masses, but nothing says which pass favours
  which (*checked*, L2-S46's premise; my reading for the rivals). The annotation files the record
  describes write the repeat mark in place (*relayed*, §3), and nothing I read shows an annotator writing a
  pass difference that position one would fail to match.
- *Towards the principles.* The charter's partition stands unamended, read with Ruling 46 (i). One reading
  per position is one home per fact (#6). #12: a per-pass reading position one never computes is, under
  D-297, not a loss; the cost of pass attribution not being published is real and is stated, not hidden.
  It decides no unfolding, which the ratified L0/L1 text calls a performance reading. L2-S38 asks every
  weight to be fitted from annotated music; with labels written once per position (*relayed*), a per-pass
  reading would have nothing independent to be fitted against (#21; my reading).
- *The cost.* Pass attribution is not published: where a bar before a junction is read two ways, a
  consumer sees two rivals with their masses, not "this on the first pass, that on the second". And where a
  graded corpus is unfolded and its two passes carry different labels on one notated bar, the grader has
  two labels for one span; how that is graded is a measurement-design question (the same kind as OQ-L2-7),
  carried and not decided here. How the two neighbours' contributions are combined is the "declared rule"
  L2-S46's falsifier names; it is part of the candidate score's detail and not decided here.
- *Meta level:* the analysis's own specification.

**Position two — one reading per pass (unfolded order).** L2 expands the repeat structure and reads each
pass as its own stretch; a repeated passage is published once per pass, each span carrying which pass it
belongs to.

- *Towards the objective (#4).* A bar heard differently on its second pass gets its own reading, attributed
  to the pass, and an unfolded ground truth is matched pass for pass.
- *Towards the principles.* The charter's "partition of the working span" is amended, because one notated
  position would then lie in two spans; L2-S1 (a reading is a partition of the working span) and L2-S46
  itself change with it (my reading). L2 would choose an unfolding,
  including how to treat da capo and segno structures, which the record places with the junction list only
  as a challengeable reading (OQ-9's answer). The same notated material is published twice where the passes
  agree, a second home for one fact (#6). The extra field, pass attribution, has no evidence to be fitted or
  graded against on the record (#21; my reading, from the relayed annotation facts).
- *The cost.* At least three texts amended (the charter's L2 list, L2-S1, L2-S46); a pass index on every published
  span; the publication over repeated music doubled; an unfolding rule to derive.
- *Meta level:* the analysis's own specification and its charter.

**Why I call the choice close to collapsed, and still put it.** On the facts read, position two buys one
thing, pass attribution, which no annotation I read about writes; and it costs a charter amendment and a
duplicated publication. Position one costs that one thing, stated. It is not wholly collapsed because the
corpus fact behind it is relayed and unestablished: **if a grading corpus were found whose annotators write
different labels on two passes of the same bars, that would be evidence for position two**, and the
question should come back.

**Recommendation: position one.** Towards the objective: the pass difference the notation itself writes,
a first and second ending, is expressible; nothing I read shows an annotator writing another; and the one
case position one cannot attribute still keeps both readings as rivals. Towards the principles: no charter
amendment, one reading per position (#6), no reading that is computed is discarded (#12, D-297), and no
performance reading is decided. The cost is that a pass-dependent reading
before a junction is published as "either", not as "this pass, that pass".

*If position one is ruled:* L2-S46 stands as derived; its status "Open for the graded order" closes, with
the ground truth's alignment recorded as a fact to be established per corpus at measurement design, not as
an L2 question; its falsifier is unchanged. OQ-L2-16 is answered; with it the last of the input contract's
OQ-1 is answered. No row is placed.
