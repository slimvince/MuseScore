## 7. Documentation and algorithm, kept apart

**Documentation:** everything above. Ratifying this surface marks §0, §1, §4 (and, on this side's proposal, §2 and §5)
ratified, and orders the listed lines written.

**Algorithm: unchanged.** No item changes what L2 computes. The spelled tonic (R7), the eight relations (F2), the
lowest sounding pitch in L1 (F1), the publication cut-off (R5) and the effort setting's place (R3) were each ruled
earlier; these lines only make the file's terms and open questions say so.

**The currently decided method, in plain words.** For the stretch of music asked about (plus music loaded around it
as evidence only), L2 considers every reading its admission rule allows: a division into harmonic spans, each
starting at a point where some note starts or stops; for each span a tonality — a spelled tonic, major or minor —
and a chord given by degree, quality, figure and applied target; and for every sounding note, whether it is a chord
tone or an elaboration, with one of eight relations. It gives each reading a candidate score, a sum of terms each
multiplied by a fitted term weight. At the top effort setting it searches exactly, or prunes only by a bound that
provably cannot discard a reading that could win (L2-S36); no reading is dropped on partial scores. *(Corrected at
the check; former wording: "searches exactly and never drops a reading before the whole stretch is scored" —
L2-S36 and tractability Ruling 1 allow a provably safe bound.)* It publishes the best reading, one reading per notated position, together with its
rivals and each rival's mass — the model's own distribution, not a calibrated probability — withholding nothing at
the top setting; below it, a cut-off whose depth is decided when the effort setting is built, with the cut-off and
the withheld mass published.
