# The L3 application dispatch's surface — two derivations to confirm, one statement of what the record leaves open, 2026-11-08

**What this file is.** The surface that must come before the next Claude Code dispatch (the application act, step 2 of
the L3 route ruling), written by the Cowork sitting booted on handoff entry 601, under its §3 steps 3 and 4. It is the
"dispatch's surface" the step-one remainder record names. **Both items put here are derivations under D-599, not
choices**: the record already settles them, and what is asked of the user is to confirm them. **Documentation only; no
algorithm change is decided by anything in this file.** It writes no dispatch.

**Words used here, explained first.**

- **The reading file** — `ratification_surfaces/cowork_comparison_l3_reading.md`: the table that compares every
  statement of the old L3 design documents ("outgoing statements", one per row) against the blind derivation of L3.
  Each row carries one *disposition*: a word saying what happens to that statement.
- **The derivation** — the blind derivation of L3: the L3 specification written by a session that had not seen the
  old documents. Its statements are numbered L3-S1 to L3-S42.
- **The application act** — the next Claude Code dispatch. Under the route ruling it (a) writes on each ruled row of
  the reading file the disposition the ruling gave it, and (b) writes the L3 specification as amended by the rulings as
  a NEW file beside the derivation.
- **The carry list** — `ratification_surfaces/cowork_l3_application_carry_list_2026_11_08.md`: what the L3 ruling
  records of 2026-11-02 to 2026-11-08 hand to that act, as found by its §0 search.
- **D-599** — the standing method: apply the principles and the record first; only where they do not decide is there a
  user choice, and otherwise the user ratifies the derivation.

## 1. Derivation A — the word written on rows a ruling "decided against": HISTORICAL

**The question, from the record.** Step-one remainder record (`records/cowork/rulings/
cowork_rulings_2026_11_08_l3_step_one_remainder_sitting.md`), Ruling 3, lines 84–86:

> "for Rows 3.5 (iii), 4.17, 4.24 (ii) and 4.26, as for the 27 method rows of Ruling 2, the records say "decided
> against" and name no disposition word; the reading file's vocabulary (§5) has none for it. Which word the application
> dispatch writes is for that dispatch's surface to state."

The same wording, "decided against", is used by at least two other records for rows they name: the sections record
lines 112–113 (*"the ten rows that call the grouping flat are recorded as decided against — 3.1(i), 3.3, 8.21, 8.22,
8.25, 8.34, 8.96(ii), 19.38(iii), 57.315(i), 57.315(ii)"*) and the phrase-ends record lines 86–87 *(★ CORRECTED at the
check. Former wording, kept (#12): "line 87")* (*"The method rows
(peak-picking, forcing markers, the primitive's worked examples) are the rows Ruling 1 decides against"*). **This
surface did not search every record for the phrase**; the application act finds the rows by searching the L3 ruling
records for "decided against" / "decides against", and the word below applies to each row so found.

**What the record says that settles it.**

1. **The vocabulary is closed and every row gets exactly one word.** The reading file §5 (lines 368 and 379): *"Two axes,
   both closed … No other verdict word appears in this file"*; the disposition axis is *"exactly one per outgoing
   statement"*. So no new word may be coined, and a ruled row cannot be left with no word.
2. **Of the seven entries on §5's list, one fits.** The reading file §5, lines 382–396, defines them; the seventh,
   UNPLACED, is by §5's own words *"NOT a sixth disposition"* but the default for a row not yet placed. *(★ CORRECTED at
   the check. Former wording, kept (#12): "Of the seven words, one fits. The reading file §5, lines 382–396, defines
   them.")* Taking each in turn:
   - *ADOPTED — carried* and *ADOPTED — proposed* keep the content in the specification. The ruling decided against the
     content. They do not fit.
   - *RELOCATED* means the content belongs to another layer; *QUARANTINED* means it describes how the code works now.
     Neither is the ground the rulings give.
   - *DISCARDED* needs *"principle #10's worth test, with the finding, the date the row is written and the reason — all
     three, or the disposition does not reach the row"*. A ruling deciding against a design is not that test.
   - *UNPLACED* is *"a row the user places"*; these rows the user has placed, by ruling.
   - *HISTORICAL* — *"it records an event, a build state, a superseded plan, or a status; not a rule."* A design
     position that a ruling has decided against is a superseded plan.
3. **The L2 application act did the same with rows a ruling declined or overtook.** In
   `records/cc/instructions/cc_instruction_l2_application_2026_10_10.md`, for example: line 704, Row 6.90 (a position the
   ruling declined), *"Ruling 1: **HISTORICAL**, against its proposal — "not adopted, for position three's reason""*;
   line 1530 (rows overtaken by the position the ruling took), *"HISTORICAL rather than ADOPTED because Ruling 1 took
   Position B, not C"*; line 576, Row 7.110 (a design choice overtaken by a decision, not by a declined position), *"a
   design choice … overtaken by D-524"*. These are examples found by a search for the word HISTORICAL; whether every
   declined row in L2 was so marked was not checked. *(★ CORRECTED at the check. Former wording, kept (#12): "The L2
   application act did exactly this. In … rows whose position an L2 ruling declined were written HISTORICAL: line 704 …;
   line 1530 …; line 576, Row 7.110 …" — Row 7.110 was overtaken by D-524, not declined, and the sentence read as
   covering every such L2 row.)* The
   difference: there the L2 records named the word themselves; the L3 records do not, which is why the step-one
   remainder record sends it here.

**The derivation.** Each row an L3 ruling record says it decides against is re-marked **HISTORICAL**, its line quoting
the record's own placement in the record's words (the L2 dispatch's line form (b): *"\*RULED DISPOSITION (<record
file>, Ruling N).\* **<disposition>** — <placement in the record's words>"*). Where a record decides against only a part
of a row (Rows 3.5 (iii), 4.17, 4.24 (ii), 4.26 are worded *"decided against where …"*), the line names that part
only, as the L2 form does for a split row; the rest of the row keeps what the record says of it.

**The alternatives, and why they have collapsed.** Writing no word (the L2 form (c), used there only for rows that
carried no proposed disposition) would leave a ruled row without the one word §5 requires. Coining a word such as
"DECLINED" would break the closed vocabulary. Each of the other five words fails at its own definition, as item 2
shows. **No alternative remains among those weighed here.** *(★ CORRECTED at the check. Former wording, kept (#12):
"No alternative remains.")*

**Documentation only. Algorithm: no change.** This decides only which word is written in the comparison table. What
those rows describe was already decided against by the rulings named; nothing here changes what L3 does.

## 2. Derivation B — every change the records ascribe to "the derivation" is made in the new file; the derivation's body is not touched

**The tension, as the carry list §10 states it.** The route record lines 59–61:

> "One Claude Code dispatch, the application act, re-marks the ruled rows in the reading file and writes the amended
> derived L3 specification as a NEW file beside the derivation, whose body is not rewritten and which gains D-674's top
> banner only."

Several later records speak of changing the derivation [the four quotations below relayed from the carry list §10, not
re-read at their records by this sitting]: the chord-symbol record line 88 (*"A stale pointer in the
derivation, fixed by the application act"*); the key-areas record line 60 and the modal-counts record line 42 (*"the
derivation to gain a statement for them"*); the thematic-parallelism record lines 117–118 (*"nor the derivation (those
are the application act's)"*).

**What the record says that settles it.**

1. The route ruling is the user's ruling ("A", 2026-11-02) and states the derivation's treatment in terms: body not
   rewritten, top banner only. None of the record lines quoted above says it amends that ruling; those records were
   not read whole for this. *(★ CORRECTED at the check. Former wording, kept (#12): "None of the later records says it
   amends that ruling.")*
2. D-674 (`DECISIONS.md` line 550, quoted at the carry list §1): *"a dated report is re-bannered and never rewritten"*.
3. The L2 precedent, on the identically worded L2 route ruling: the L2 application dispatch's Task 2 is headed *"the
   derivation's top banner under D-674, and nothing else"*, and its check requires *"exactly ONE changed passage, at
   the file's head, insertions only, zero deletions"*. The same dispatch's forms section (lines 219–241) writes the
   amendments *"in the successor"*, the new file. *(★ CORRECTED at the check. Former wording, kept (#12): "Every L2
   amendment went into the new file." — wider than the sections read.)*

**The derivation.** Where an L3 ruling record says "the derivation" is to be fixed, rewritten or to gain a statement,
the application act makes that change in the NEW file (the amended derived L3 specification), and gives the derivation
itself the top banner only.

**The alternatives, and why they have collapsed.** Editing the derivation's body would contradict the route ruling's
own words and D-674; no record line quoted here asks for that ruling to be amended. **No alternative remains among
those weighed here.** *(★ CORRECTED at the check. Former wording, kept (#12): "no record asks for that ruling to be
amended. No alternative remains.")*

**Documentation only. Algorithm: no change.** This decides only which file receives the changes the rulings already
made.
