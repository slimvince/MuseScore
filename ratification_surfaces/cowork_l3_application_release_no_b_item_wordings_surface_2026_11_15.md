## 2. The seven passages

### Task 1

**W1 — the pointer paragraph that replaces the derivation's §5 to §7** (Task 1 (a), lines 4990–4995) [checked].

*What it does.* The derivation's §5 (the measured-cost record), §6 (the independence record) and §7 (where the brief's
decomposition seemed wrong) are about the blind act itself, not about what L3 does. The successor does not copy them.
In their place stands one paragraph that points to them at the derivation. The dispatch gives it as:

> *the measured-cost record, the independence record and the note on the brief's decomposition are the blind act's
> own and stand at the derivation, `cowork_blind_derivation_l3_2026_10_17.md` §5–§7, at the blob `git ls-tree` gives
> for it at the declared start tip, which you write here; they are not restated (#6)*

*Its basis.* The reason given is D-674: a dated record of an act is kept as it is, and the derivation is that record.
The route ruling of 2026-11-02 (§2 item 2) orders the successor as a new file beside the derivation. It says nothing
about §5 to §7 [checked]. So *leaving them out* is the writing side's choice.

*A defect in the words, found by this sitting.* The quoted paragraph is half instruction: *"which you write here"*
tells Claude Code to put the blob in. The words to be written are therefore not fixed. Nor is it said whether a
heading stands above the paragraph. At L2, Claude Code wrote a heading of its own, `## 5. The blind act's own record
— where it stands`, and rephrased the sentence ("at blob `d78ac530…`; they are not restated here (#6)") [checked at
`cowork_derived_specification_l2_2026_10_10.md` lines 2420–2424]. At L3 the same thing would happen: Claude Code
would compose words that no one ruled. Ruling 10 (third Part B record) states the bar in another place: *"The act may
not close it with unruled words."*

*The alternatives.*

1. **Ratify as written.** Claude Code composes the final sentence and any heading, as it did at L2. Towards the
   objective: no effect; the passage is not specification. Towards the principles: it leaves words in the successor
   that were authored by the act and ruled by no one, the gap Ruling 10 bars elsewhere.
2. **Ratify the choice to leave §5 to §7 out, with the exact words fixed by the writing side now.** The heading
   `## 5. The blind act's own record — where it stands` (the L2 successor's, so the two successors read alike), and
   beneath it, in italics: *The measured-cost record, the independence record and the note on the brief's
   decomposition are the blind act's own and stand at the derivation, `cowork_blind_derivation_l3_2026_10_17.md`
   §5–§7, at blob `<the blob git ls-tree gives for it at the declared start tip>`; they are not restated here (#6).*
   Claude Code fills in only the blob. The number 5 fits: the derivation's §0 to §4 are carried with their numbers,
   its §5 is where the pointer stands, and the two sections B1 and B2 add have unnumbered headings (first Part B
   record, Rulings 1 and 2) [checked at the derivation's `##` headings and the dispatch's lines 144–153]. The
   dispatch's (a) is amended to give these words at step 2 (the edits), the
   former wording kept. Towards the objective: no effect. Towards the principles: every word of this passage is
   ruled; only a value read from git is filled in.
3. **Carry §5 to §7 whole into the successor.** Towards the objective: no gain; the sections describe the blind act,
   not L3. Towards the principles: it restates the dated record in a second place, which the dispatch's (#6) and
   D-674's split between a dated record and a live text both argue against.

*The writing side's reading.* Alternative 2. This sitting found nothing towards alternative 3. Alternative 1
differs from 2 only in leaving the words unruled.

### Task 3 — the passages in (d) and (f)

**Task 3 (d) as a whole** (lines 5225–5242) [checked]. The reading file states its own condition in four places: that
every disposition in it is a proposal. Its §13 tables count the proposals. Once the act has added the ruled lines,
those statements would no longer be true of the rows that carry a ruled line. (d) answers this with three passages
that depend on one another:

**W4 — the sentence beneath §13's heading** (lines 5226–5227): *"The tables below are the distribution of the
PROPOSALS; the distribution of the rows placed by the records of 2026-11-02 to 2026-11-08 is §13a."* §13's tables are
not edited.

**W5 — the new §13a: its heading and what it counts** (lines 5228–5235). The heading is `## 13a. The ruled
distribution, derived from the added lines`, inserted after §13 and before §14. It is derived mechanically from the
added lines, for each member and in total. It counts:

- how many row-parts carry a RULED line, by disposition word (HISTORICAL lines from the decided-against form counted
  under HISTORICAL);
- how many carry a RECORDED line, in a column of their own, never added into the ruled counts;
- how many carry two or more lines;
- how many carry none;
- the flagged items answered, outside the row total.

All of these are stated as sums taken at the rows, with the arithmetic shown to close against §13's *Total* line.
There is no ratio, no grade and no comparison beyond that arithmetic.

**W6 — the sentence added in each of four places** (lines 5237–5240): the banner's
`**EVERY DISPOSITION BELOW IS A PROPOSAL.**`, §0's heading paragraph, §15's `**NOTHING.**` paragraph and §16's first
bullet each gain: *"Rows carrying a RULED DISPOSITION line are placed by the record and ruling the line names, rows
carrying a RECORDED DISPOSITION line as that record's own derivation, challengeable at the row; every other row
remains a proposal (the application act of `records/cc/instructions/cc_instruction_l3_application_2026_11_08.md`,
Task 3)."* In each place the former wording is kept. §15 still reads NOTHING: you are asked to rule nothing in the
reading file.

*Their basis.* The route ruling (§2 item 2) orders the act to re-mark the ruled rows in the reading file [checked].
It does not order how the file's statements about its own condition are kept true. (d) is the writing side's answer.

*The alternatives.*

1. **Ratify W4, W5 and W6 as written.** Towards the objective: the reading file says truly which rows are ruled,
   and gives the ruled counts beside the proposed ones. Towards the principles: nothing is deleted (#12), the
   proposal counts stay, and the new counts are taken at the rows, not asserted.
2. **Ratify W6 only, leaving out §13a and the sentence pointing to it.** The ruled counts would then stand only in
   Claude Code's report. Towards the objective: a reader of the reading file sees §13's proposal counts with nothing
   saying how far the rulings have overtaken them. Towards the principles: the file would carry an account of itself
   that is no longer complete.
3. **Leave the four statements as they are.** Towards the objective: a reader of the reading file is told that
   ruled rows are proposals. Towards the principles: the file would say *"every disposition below
   is a proposal"* while its rows carry ruled lines. That is a contradiction written in at the moment the act makes
   it true. This sitting found nothing towards it.

*The writing side's reading.* Alternative 3 contradicts the file and is not a real alternative. Alternative 2 only
removes information. The principles point to alternative 1, so it is put as a confirmation.
